**Your argument is NOT "the priestly text is a literal mechanical procedure manual."** All three positions agree the rite has spatial logic, theological depth, and structural symmetry. The real difference is whether the priestly text's *own determinate distinctions* are taken as authoritative for what the rite does, or whether they are subordinated to a meta-frame (canonical-theological for B, anthropological-structural for C) that the priestly authors themselves did not endorse.

**Your argument is NOT "atonement is a Christian invention foreign to Judaism."** The unified reading has Jewish witnesses — Mishnah Yoma, rabbinic readings of *kipper*. Your real point is that even Mishnaic reception simplifies the priestly text's bifurcation, and that the priestly source itself (PT in Knohl's stratification) is more austerely cultic than its later receptions.

**Against Monk B specifically:** B will press v. 5's singular *lə-ḥaṭṭā't*, the *kipper*-language over the live goat in v. 10, and the chaining of v. 20. Your specific rebuttal: v. 5 is a *category designation* for the day's animals (compare Num 28-29's category labels for multi-animal offerings), and the procedural detail in vv. 7-22 then *bifurcates* what v. 5 names. If v. 5 controlled ritual identity, every anomalous feature of the live-goat procedure (no slaughter, double-handed pressing, verbal confession, *naśā'* verb, wilderness destination) would be unaccountable. The text deviates from ḥaṭṭā't-procedure precisely because the live-goat operation is not, in its actual mechanics, a ḥaṭṭā't — it is an elimination rite folded into the day's ḥaṭṭā't-cluster.

**Against Monk C specifically:** C will press that the entire question is malformed because rites are practices, not propositions, and that theology is secondary to anthropological function. Your specific rebuttal: this move grants modern anthropology epistemic privilege the priestly authors are denied. The priestly authors were *technical specialists* operating a system of remarkable internal coherence. Calling their theology a "secondary self-description" of work they didn't really understand is the most condescending move in modern biblical studies. The two-operations position can absorb structural-symbolic resonance (Douglas's centripetal/centrifugal vectors are *real* and *cohere with* the two-operations reading); what it refuses is the relegation of the priestly mechanics to surface and the elevation of structuralist abstraction to depth.

Your essay should follow this structure (1500-2000 words):

**c. Diagnosis of each opposing failure.** Specific, not dismissive.
- Monk B fails because: it cannot explain *why the procedural detail bifurcates what v. 5 names as one*. If v. 5 is decisive, vv. 7-22's elaborate two-procedure prescription is pointless redundancy. **d. **e. Yom Kippur, *as the priestly authors wrote it*, is not "the day God forgives sins." It is the day the house gets scrubbed and the year's iniquity-load gets carried out. Forgiveness is not the priestly text's category.
