## FRAMING CORRECTIONS

**Your argument is NOT "the priestly authors were primitive and didn't know what they were doing."** You hold the priestly system in high regard — it is a sophisticated technical theology operating an inherited anthropological mechanism with genuine refinement (the demythologization of Azazel, the substitution of the goat for human victims, the calendrical containment, the integration with ethical monotheism). What you deny is that the priestly authors had full reflexive access to the *anthropological substrate* their theology was sitting on top of. No tradition has full reflexive access to its own founding mechanisms. This is not condescension; it is the standard methodological assumption of cultural analysis.

**Your argument is NOT "religion is really just sociology."** You are not a reductionist. Theology and anthropology operate at *different registers, both real*. The rite *is* doing theological work in the priestly self-understanding, and it *is* doing anthropological work at a deeper structural register. These do not exclude each other. But the structural register is *prior* — it frames the theological register, which is the priestly community's self-description of work whose deeper logic the structural analyst can name.

**Against Monk A specifically:** A will press the priestly authors' philological precision and accuse you of granting modern anthropology epistemic privilege the priestly authors are denied. Your specific rebuttal: the privilege you grant the modern analyst is exactly the privilege every interpretive tradition grants its own analytic categories — the priestly authors' *own* later receptions (rabbinic, Christian) used categories the priestly authors did not have, and we credit those receptions with insight. The structural analyst is not unique in working *beyond* the source's vocabulary; she is just more methodologically explicit about it. A's accusation of condescension is itself a tradition-defensive move, not a methodological refutation.

**Against Monk B specifically:** B will press that the priestly text's theological self-presentation is decisive and that bracketing it is interpretive condescension. Your specific rebuttal: B is not actually defending what the priestly text says — B is defending a *canonical* reading that fuses Levitical, Mishnaic, Christian, and Reformation theological developments into one harmonized voice and then projects that voice back onto the priestly source as if the source itself spoke this way. This is selection bias dressed as fidelity. The structural reading actually accepts the priestly text *as it stands*, in its specificity, before any harmonization — and asks what kind of work the text is doing in that specificity. B's "canonical unity" is the most aggressive interpretive overlay in the entire dialectic; B has just normalized the overlay so thoroughly that it feels like the text itself.
