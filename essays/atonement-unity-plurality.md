# The Atonement: One Act or Many Operations? A Scriptural Analysis

## Introduction: The Structural Question

Latter-day Saint discourse routinely speaks of "the Atonement" as a singular noun -- a unified event or act. But when you examine what the scriptures actually attribute to Christ's redemptive work, you find a catalog of distinct operations that follow different rules, apply to different populations, and carry different conditions. This raises a genuine structural question: does scripture itself treat Christ's redemptive work as one thing or many?

This report catalogs every distinct operation the Standard Works attribute to Christ's redemptive work, maps the rules each operation follows, examines whether scripture bundles them into a unified concept, and identifies the key asymmetries that any theology of atonement must address.

## Part 1: The Catalog of Distinct Operations

### 1. Resurrection (Breaking the Bands of Death)

**Scriptural basis:**
- "the death of Christ shall loose the bands of this temporal death, that all shall be raised from this temporal death" ([Alma 11:42](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/11.42))
- "this restoration shall come to all, both old and young, both bond and free, both male and female, both the wicked and the righteous" ([Alma 11:44](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/11.44))
- "For as in Adam all die, even so in Christ shall all be made alive" ([1 Corinthians 15:22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/15.22))
- "he bringeth to pass the resurrection of the dead" ([Mosiah 15:20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/15.20))
- "if Christ had not risen from the dead, or have broken the bands of death that the grave should have no victory, and that death should have no sting, there could have been no resurrection" ([Mosiah 16:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/16.7))
- "he suffereth this that the resurrection might pass upon all men" ([2 Nephi 9:22](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/9.22))
- "through the redemption which is made for you is brought to pass the resurrection from the dead" ([D&C 88:14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/88.14))

**Rules:** Universal. Unconditional. Applies to all humans regardless of faith, repentance, covenant, or moral status. Scripture is emphatic on this point -- "all," "both the wicked and the righteous."

**Temporal status:** Described as both a completed act (Christ has "broken" the bands) and a future event (people "shall be" raised). The enabling cause is completed; the effect unfolds over time.

**Connection to a specific moment:** Tied explicitly to Christ's own resurrection. Mosiah 16:7 makes the logic direct: "if Christ had not risen from the dead... there could have been no resurrection."

### 2. Forgiveness / Remission of Sins

**Scriptural basis:**
- "he shall come among the children of men, to take upon him the transgressions of his people, and that he shall atone for the sins of the world" ([Alma 34:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/34.8))
- "I, God, have suffered these things for all, that they might not suffer if they would repent" ([D&C 19:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/19.16))
- "he offereth himself a sacrifice for sin, to answer the ends of the law, unto all those who have a broken heart and a contrite spirit; and unto none else can the ends of the law be answered" ([2 Nephi 2:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/2.7))
- "mercy claimeth the penitent, and mercy cometh because of the atonement" ([Alma 42:23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/42.23))
- "he is the propitiation for our sins: and not for ours only, but also for the sins of the whole world" ([1 John 2:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-jn/2.2))

**Rules:** Conditional. Requires "a broken heart and a contrite spirit" (2 Nephi 2:7). Requires repentance: "they might not suffer if they would repent" (D&C 19:16). "Mercy claimeth the penitent" (Alma 42:23) -- and by clear implication, only the penitent. The critical 2 Nephi 2:7 passage is explicit: "unto none else can the ends of the law be answered."

**Temporal status:** Described as both a historical sacrifice and an ongoing availability. The sacrifice is completed; forgiveness is received repeatedly as individuals repent.

**Connection to a specific moment:** Connected to suffering and blood. Mosiah 3:7 describes Gethsemane: "blood cometh from every pore." D&C 19:18 confirms: "to tremble because of pain, and to bleed at every pore, and to suffer both body and spirit." Also connected to the cross: "redemption had been wrought through the sacrifice of the Son of God upon the cross" ([D&C 138:35](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/138.35)).

### 3. Satisfying the Demands of Justice

**Scriptural basis:**
- "God himself atoneth for the sins of the world, to bring about the plan of mercy, to appease the demands of justice, that God might be a perfect, just God, and a merciful God also" ([Alma 42:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/42.15))
- "having broken the bands of death, taken upon himself their iniquity and their transgressions, having redeemed them, and satisfied the demands of justice" ([Mosiah 15:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/15.9))
- "mercy can satisfy the demands of justice, and encircles them in the arms of safety" ([Alma 34:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/34.16))
- "Whom God hath set forth to be a propitiation through faith in his blood, to declare his righteousness" ([Romans 3:25](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rom/3.25))

**Rules:** This is the mechanism behind forgiveness rather than an independent operation. But scripture treats it as a distinct conceptual act -- something that had to be done to make mercy possible at all. The satisfaction appears to be universal in scope (justice is satisfied) but conditional in application (mercy "claimeth the penitent"). This is a crucial distinction: the payment is made; the benefit is not automatically disbursed.

**Temporal status:** Described as completed. Mosiah 15:9 uses past participles: "having broken... having redeemed... and satisfied."

**Connection to a specific moment:** Treated as timeless in function. Alma 42:15 gives no temporal marker. Mosiah 15:9 seems to describe the post-resurrection Christ who has "ascended into heaven."

### 4. Sanctification / Transformation of Human Nature

**Scriptural basis:**
- "the natural man is an enemy to God... unless he yields to the enticings of the Holy Spirit, and putteth off the natural man and becometh a saint through the atonement of Christ the Lord" ([Mosiah 3:19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/3.19))
- "they did fast and pray oft, and did wax stronger and stronger in their humility... even to the purifying and the sanctification of their hearts, which sanctification cometh because of their yielding their hearts unto God" ([Helaman 3:35](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/hel/3.35))
- "sanctified in Christ by the grace of God, through the shedding of the blood of Christ, which is in the covenant of the Father unto the remission of your sins, that ye become holy, without spot" ([Moroni 10:33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/moro/10.33))
- "they were called after this holy order, and were sanctified, and their garments were washed white through the blood of the Lamb" ([Alma 13:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/13.11))
- "after being sanctified by the Holy Ghost, having their garments made white, being pure and spotless before God, could not look upon sin save it were with abhorrence" ([Alma 13:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/13.12))
- "Repent, all ye ends of the earth, and come unto me and be baptized in my name, that ye may be sanctified by the reception of the Holy Ghost, that ye may stand spotless before me at the last day" ([3 Nephi 27:20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/3-ne/27.20))
- "he came into the world, even Jesus, to be crucified for the world, and to bear the sins of the world, and to sanctify the world, and to cleanse it from all unrighteousness" ([D&C 76:41](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/76.41))

**Rules:** Conditional and progressive. Requires yielding to the Spirit (Mosiah 3:19), fasting and prayer (Helaman 3:35), repentance and baptism (3 Nephi 27:20), denial of ungodliness and love of God (Moroni 10:32-33). This is described as an ongoing process -- "wax stronger and stronger" (Helaman 3:35).

**Temporal status:** Ongoing process. Not a single event but a trajectory. People "wax" into it. The enabling power (Christ's blood) is completed; the transformation unfolds over a lifetime.

**Connection to a specific moment:** Connected to "the shedding of the blood of Christ" (Moroni 10:33) and to the Holy Ghost as agent (Alma 13:12, 3 Nephi 27:20). This is notable: the blood enables it, but the Spirit effects it.

### 5. Succoring / Empathetic Sustaining

**Scriptural basis:**
- "he shall go forth, suffering pains and afflictions and temptations of every kind; and this that the word might be fulfilled which saith he will take upon him the pains and the sicknesses of his people" ([Alma 7:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/7.11))
- "he will take upon him death, that he may loose the bands of death which bind his people; and he will take upon him their infirmities, that his bowels may be filled with mercy, according to the flesh, that he may know according to the flesh how to succor his people according to their infirmities" ([Alma 7:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/7.12))
- "For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin" ([Hebrews 4:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/4.15))
- "My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness" ([2 Corinthians 12:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/2-cor/12.9))

**Rules:** This is structurally different from all the other operations. It addresses not sin but suffering, not guilt but infirmity. The conditions are not repentance but simply being in need. Alma 7:11-12 describes Christ taking upon himself "pains and sicknesses" -- not for the purpose of forgiving sin, but so that "his bowels may be filled with mercy... that he may know according to the flesh how to succor his people."

**Temporal status:** Ongoing. This is the most clearly present-tense operation. Christ currently succors. The past suffering enables a perpetual capacity.

**Connection to a specific moment:** Connected to Christ's mortal experience of suffering generally, not to a single moment. Alma 7:11 describes a lifetime of "temptations of every kind." Hebrews 4:15 likewise: "in all points tempted like as we are."

### 6. Enabling Repentance Itself

**Scriptural basis:**
- "this being the intent of this last sacrifice, to bring about the bowels of mercy, which overpowereth justice, and bringeth about means unto men that they may have faith unto repentance" ([Alma 34:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/34.15))
- "mercy can satisfy the demands of justice, and encircles them in the arms of safety, while he that exercises no faith unto repentance is exposed to the whole law of the demands of justice" ([Alma 34:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/34.16))
- "the Lord your Redeemer suffered death in the flesh; wherefore he suffered the pain of all men, that all men might repent and come unto him" ([D&C 18:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/18.11))

**Rules:** This is a second-order operation. The sacrifice does not merely forgive sins upon repentance; it "bringeth about means unto men that they may have faith unto repentance." Without the atonement, repentance itself would be meaningless -- there would be nothing to repent toward, no mechanism by which repentance could accomplish anything. D&C 18:11 makes this explicit: Christ suffered "that all men might repent" -- implying that without his suffering, the capacity to repent productively would not exist.

**Temporal status:** The enabling is completed; the access to repentance is ongoing.

### 7. Making Weak Things Strong

**Scriptural basis:**
- "if men come unto me I will show unto them their weakness. I give unto men weakness that they may be humble; and my grace is sufficient for all men that humble themselves before me; for if they humble themselves before me, and have faith in me, then will I make weak things become strong unto them" ([Ether 12:27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/ether/12.27))

**Rules:** Conditional on humility and faith. This is not about sin at all -- it is about mortal limitation. The mechanism is grace, which is "sufficient for all men that humble themselves."

**Temporal status:** Ongoing and progressive.

**Connection to a specific moment:** No connection to a specific historical moment. The source is "my grace" -- an attribute or capacity of Christ, not tied to a particular act.

### 8. Comforting and Filling with Hope

**Scriptural basis:**
- "the remission of sins bringeth meekness, and lowliness of heart; and because of meekness and lowliness of heart cometh the visitation of the Holy Ghost, which Comforter filleth with hope and perfect love" ([Moroni 8:26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/moro/8.26))
- "ye shall have hope through the atonement of Christ and the power of his resurrection, to be raised unto life eternal, and this because of your faith in him according to the promise" ([Moroni 7:41](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/moro/7.41))

**Rules:** Conditional. Moroni 8:26 describes a chain: remission of sins leads to meekness, meekness brings the Holy Ghost, the Holy Ghost fills with hope and love. Moroni 7:41 conditions hope on faith. This operation is downstream of forgiveness -- it comes after, not before.

**Temporal status:** Ongoing.

### 9. Intercession / Advocacy Before the Father

**Scriptural basis:**
- "in mine own name, by the virtue of the blood which I have spilt, have I pleaded before the Father for them" ([D&C 38:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/38.4))
- "Father, behold the sufferings and death of him who did no sin, in whom thou wast well pleased; behold the blood of thy Son which was shed" ([D&C 45:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/45.4))
- "because of the intercession for all, all men come unto God; wherefore, they stand in the presence of him, to be judged" ([2 Nephi 2:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/2.10))

**Rules:** 2 Nephi 2:10 describes the intercession as applying to "all" -- bringing all men before God for judgment. D&C 38:4 is more specific: Christ pleads for "as many as have believed in my name." So intercession has both a universal dimension (all will be brought before God) and a conditional dimension (Christ actively advocates for the faithful).

**Temporal status:** Ongoing. Christ presently pleads before the Father. D&C 45:4 is in present tense.

### 10. Overcoming Hell / Spiritual Death

**Scriptural basis:**
- "O how great the goodness of our God, who prepareth a way for our escape from the grasp of this awful monster; yea, that monster, death and hell, which I call the death of the body, and also the death of the spirit" ([2 Nephi 9:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/9.10))
- "this death of which I have spoken, which is the spiritual death, shall deliver up its dead; which spiritual death is hell; wherefore, death and hell must deliver up their dead" ([2 Nephi 9:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/9.12))
- "he delivereth his saints from that awful monster the devil, and death, and hell, and that lake of fire and brimstone, which is endless torment" ([2 Nephi 9:19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/9.19))
- "the atonement satisfieth the demands of his justice upon all those who have not the law given to them, that they are delivered from that awful monster, death and hell" ([2 Nephi 9:26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/9.26))

**Rules:** Complex. 2 Nephi 9:12 describes hell delivering up "its captive spirits" in language that sounds universal -- parallel to the grave delivering up bodies. But 2 Nephi 9:19 specifies deliverance from hell for "his saints." And 2 Nephi 9:26 introduces a distinct category: those "who have not the law given to them" are delivered from hell specifically because of the atonement, since "where there is no law given there is no punishment" (2 Nephi 9:25). This creates at least three categories: the saints (delivered by covenant), the ignorant (delivered by the atonement's coverage of those without law), and the knowingly wicked (exposed to justice).

### 11. Atonement for Original Guilt / Salvation of Children

**Scriptural basis:**
- "as in Adam, or by nature, they fall, even so the blood of Christ atoneth for their sins" ([Mosiah 3:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/3.16))
- "the Son of God hath atoned for original guilt, wherein the sins of the parents cannot be answered upon the heads of the children, for they are whole from the foundation of the world" ([Moses 6:54](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/moses/6.54))
- "he that saith that little children need baptism denieth the mercies of Christ, and setteth at naught the atonement of him and the power of his redemption" ([Moroni 8:20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/moro/8.20))

**Rules:** Universal and unconditional for children. This is a distinct operation: the atonement retroactively (or preemptively) cancels the guilt of the Fall as it would apply to those who have not reached accountability. No faith, repentance, or ordinance is required.

### 12. Perfection of the Saints / Making Just Men Perfect

**Scriptural basis:**
- "These are they who are just men made perfect through Jesus the mediator of the new covenant, who wrought out this perfect atonement through the shedding of his own blood" ([D&C 76:69](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/76.69))
- "come unto Christ, and be perfected in him... then is his grace sufficient for you, that by his grace ye may be perfect in Christ" ([Moroni 10:32](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/moro/10.32))

**Rules:** Highly conditional. D&C 76:69 describes celestial inhabitants specifically. Moroni 10:32 requires denying "yourselves of all ungodliness" and loving "God with all your might, mind and strength." This is the highest-condition operation: it requires maximal human effort enabled by maximal grace.

## Part 2: Do These Operations Follow the Same Rules?

The answer is decisively no. Here is the spectrum:

| Operation | Universal or Conditional? | Conditions | Completed or Ongoing? | Tied to Specific Moment? |
|-----------|--------------------------|------------|----------------------|-------------------------|
| Resurrection | Universal | None | Completed (enabling); future (effect) | Christ's resurrection |
| Atonement for original guilt | Universal (children) | None (for those under accountability) | Completed | Not specified |
| Forgiveness of sins | Conditional | Broken heart, repentance, faith | Completed (enabling); ongoing (application) | Gethsemane + Cross |
| Satisfying justice | Universal (mechanism); conditional (application) | None for the mechanism; repentance for benefit | Completed | Timeless |
| Enabling repentance | Universal (availability) | None for availability; faith for exercise | Completed | Suffering generally |
| Sanctification | Conditional, progressive | Yielding heart, Holy Ghost, fasting, prayer | Ongoing process | Blood of Christ + Holy Ghost |
| Succoring | Available to all who suffer | Need, not repentance | Ongoing | Christ's mortal suffering generally |
| Making weak things strong | Conditional | Humility, faith | Ongoing | Not specified (grace) |
| Hope and comfort | Conditional | Remission of sins (prerequisite), faith | Ongoing | Not specified |
| Overcoming hell | Complex: universal for ignorant, conditional for knowing | Varies by category | Future | Not specified |
| Intercession | Both universal and conditional dimensions | Belief for advocacy; universal for appearance before God | Ongoing | Past blood, present pleading |
| Perfection of saints | Highly conditional | Deny ungodliness, love God fully, grace | Ongoing | Blood of Christ |

The most important observation here: **these twelve operations do not form a single logical pattern.** Some are universal, some conditional. Some are one-time events, some are progressive processes. Some require repentance, some require only humility, some require nothing at all. Some are tied to specific historical moments, some operate timelessly.

## Part 3: Does Scripture Itself Bundle These as "The Atonement"?

### How the Book of Mormon Uses "Atonement"

The word "atonement" (or "atone," "atoneth," "atoning") appears in approximately 42 Book of Mormon verse files. In the Book of Mormon, the word is used in several distinct ways:

**As a singular noun referring to the whole:**
- "the atonement which has been prepared from the foundation of the world" ([Mosiah 4:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/4.6)) -- a planned, unified event
- "through the atonement of Christ and the power of his resurrection" ([Moroni 7:41](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/moro/7.41)) -- paired with resurrection as a distinct but related thing
- "becometh a saint through the atonement of Christ the Lord" ([Mosiah 3:19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/3.19)) -- the atonement as the means of transformation
- "why not speak of the atonement of Christ, and attain to a perfect knowledge of him" ([Jacob 4:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/jacob/4.12))

**As a description of a necessary condition or mechanism:**
- "it must needs be an infinite atonement -- save it should be an infinite atonement this corruption could not put on incorruption" ([2 Nephi 9:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/9.7)) -- the atonement as the enabling condition for resurrection
- "there must be an atonement made, or else all mankind must unavoidably perish" ([Alma 34:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/34.9))
- "the plan of mercy could not be brought about except an atonement should be made" ([Alma 42:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/42.15))

**As a verb describing a specific act applied to specific objects:**
- "the blood of Christ atoneth for their sins" ([Mosiah 3:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/3.16)) -- atoning for children's inherited condition
- "God himself atoneth for the sins of the world" ([Alma 42:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/42.15))
- "he shall atone for the sins of the world" ([Alma 34:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/34.8))

**Key observation:** The Book of Mormon uses "atonement" primarily in connection with sin and the fall -- not with succoring, strengthening, or comforting. The expansion of "atonement" to include empathetic succoring (Alma 7:11-12) is present in the Book of Mormon, but Alma 7:11-12 never actually uses the word "atonement." It describes Christ's suffering of pains and infirmities, but calls this "the power of his deliverance" (Alma 7:13), not "the atonement."

### How the Bible Uses Related Terms

The KJV uses "atonement" only once in the New Testament: Romans 5:11 -- "by whom we have now received the atonement." The Greek footnote in the LDS edition clarifies: the Greek is "reconciliation, restoration to favor."

The Old Testament uses "atonement" extensively but almost exclusively in the Levitical sacrificial context -- the Day of Atonement rituals in Leviticus 16, sin offerings throughout Leviticus and Numbers. This is ritual purification and restoration to covenantal standing, not the comprehensive concept Latter-day Saints mean by "the Atonement."

Paul uses distinct terms for distinct operations:
- "Propitiation" (Romans 3:25, 1 John 2:2) -- satisfying divine justice
- "Redemption" (Ephesians 1:7, Colossians 1:14) -- buying back, freeing from bondage
- "Reconciliation" (Romans 5:11, Ephesians 2:16) -- restoring relationship
- "Justification" (Romans 5:9) -- being declared righteous
- "Sanctification" (1 Corinthians 6:11) -- being made holy

The Bible thus naturally uses multiple terms for what LDS discourse collapses into "the Atonement."

### How D&C Uses "Atonement"

The D&C uses "atonement" or "atone" in 13 verses. The usage is notably dual:
- As a unified concept: "through his atonement, and by obedience to the principles of the gospel, mankind might be saved" ([D&C 138:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/138.4)) -- a single enabling act paired with human response
- "We believe that through the Atonement of Christ, all mankind may be saved, by obedience to the laws and ordinances of the Gospel" ([Articles of Faith 1:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/a-of-f/1.3)) -- the definitive LDS summary statement
- As specific operations: "who wrought out this perfect atonement through the shedding of his own blood" ([D&C 76:69](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/76.69)) -- the atonement specifically as blood sacrifice
- "whose arm of mercy hath atoned for your sins" ([D&C 29:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/29.1)) -- atoning as a verb directed at sins specifically

### Passages That Explicitly Unify

**2 Nephi 9:6-13** is the strongest unifying passage. Jacob presents a single sequence:
1. Death came upon all men by the Fall (v. 6)
2. There must be "an infinite atonement" or corruption could never become incorruption (v. 7)
3. Without resurrection, spirits would become subject to the devil (vv. 8-9)
4. God prepares escape from "death and hell" -- both physical death and spiritual death (v. 10)
5. Temporal death delivers up its dead (v. 11)
6. Spiritual death/hell delivers up its captive spirits (v. 12)
7. Bodies and spirits are restored by "the power of the resurrection of the Holy One of Israel" (v. 12)
8. All become incorruptible and immortal (v. 13)

This passage treats the "infinite atonement" as one thing that accomplishes multiple results in sequence. But notice: the passage primarily addresses the universal operations (resurrection, reunion of body and spirit). It does not integrate the conditional operations (sanctification, personal forgiveness) into the same narrative until later in the chapter (vv. 21-26), where the language shifts from universal to conditional.

**Alma 34:9-16** presents the "infinite and eternal sacrifice" as one act, but immediately distinguishes its universal enabling function from its conditional application. Verse 15 specifies that the sacrifice brings "salvation to all those who shall believe on his name" and "bringeth about means unto men that they may have faith unto repentance." This is a single sacrifice that produces multiple effects operating under different rules.

**Alma 42:15, 23** unifies by treating the atonement as the foundation from which distinct operations proceed: "God himself atoneth for the sins of the world, to bring about the plan of mercy, to appease the demands of justice" (v. 15). Then in v. 23: "mercy cometh because of the atonement; and the atonement bringeth to pass the resurrection of the dead; and the resurrection of the dead bringeth back men into the presence of God." This is a causal chain: one act (atonement) produces mercy, which produces resurrection, which produces return to God's presence, which produces judgment. But note the chain mixes universal and conditional elements without acknowledging the difference.

### Passages That Suggest Plurality or Distinction

**Alma 11:41** is perhaps the most structurally revealing verse in all the Standard Works: "the wicked remain as though there had been no redemption made, **except it be the loosing of the bands of death**." This verse explicitly carves off one operation -- resurrection -- as the universal minimum, while stating that for the wicked, everything else about the atonement effectively does not apply to them. If "the Atonement" were truly one unified act with one unified effect, this carve-out would be incoherent.

**Alma 7:11-13** distinguishes at least three purposes of Christ's suffering:
1. Taking upon himself death to "loose the bands of death" (v. 12a)
2. Taking upon himself infirmities to "know according to the flesh how to succor his people" (v. 12b)
3. Suffering according to the flesh to "take upon him the sins of his people, that he might blot out their transgressions" (v. 13)

These are presented as distinct purposes of Christ's suffering, operating by different mechanisms (loosing bands vs. gaining experiential knowledge vs. absorbing sin).

**D&C 76** describes three kingdoms of glory, where different people receive categorically different post-resurrection outcomes:
- Celestial: "just men made perfect through Jesus the mediator of the new covenant" ([D&C 76:69](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/76.69))
- Terrestrial: those who "receive not of his fulness" but receive a lesser glory
- Telestial: those who are "heirs of salvation" ([D&C 76:88](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/76.88)) but receive only the ministry of angels, not the presence of Christ or the Father

All three kingdoms are populated by resurrected beings. All are described as saved from something. But the "amount" of what Christ's work provides to each varies enormously. D&C 76:43 says Christ "saves all the works of his hands, except those sons of perdition." The telestial kingdom, though surpassing "all understanding" in glory (v. 89), is occupied by those who "received not the gospel, neither the testimony of Jesus" (v. 82). These are saved by Christ's work in some sense, but not in the fullest sense.

**D&C 88:22-24, 28-31** makes the modularity explicit. People receive resurrection into different degrees of glory based on what law they were able to "abide." Each is "quickened by a portion" of a different glory. The enabling mechanism (Christ's redemptive work) is one, but the outcomes are graduated. This is not one act producing one result; it is one act producing a spectrum of results calibrated to individual reception.

## Part 4: The Resurrection/Exaltation Asymmetry

This is the structural heart of the problem.

**Resurrection operates by one set of rules:**
- Universal: "all shall be raised from this temporal death" ([Alma 11:42](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/11.42))
- Unconditional: "this restoration shall come to all, both old and young, both bond and free, both male and female, both the wicked and the righteous" ([Alma 11:44](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/11.44))
- Automatic: no faith, repentance, covenant, or ordinance required
- One-time: it happens once to each person

**Exaltation/eternal life operates by a completely different set of rules:**
- Conditional: requires faith, repentance, baptism, gift of Holy Ghost, enduring to the end
- Progressive: "wax stronger and stronger" (Helaman 3:35)
- Covenantal: "through Jesus the mediator of the new covenant" (D&C 76:69)
- Involves ordinances: baptism (3 Nephi 27:20), temple covenants implied
- Requires sustained moral effort: "deny yourselves of all ungodliness, and love God with all your might, mind and strength" (Moroni 10:32)

**Does scripture explain the asymmetry?**

Partially. The closest scripture comes to explaining this is in the logic of 2 Nephi 9 and Alma 42:

The Fall produced two kinds of death: physical (body dies) and spiritual (separation from God). The atonement addresses both, but they require different responses because they are different problems:

- Physical death is something that happens *to* you. You did not choose it. You are not morally responsible for it. Therefore the remedy (resurrection) happens *to* you unconditionally.
- Spiritual death, in its personal dimension, results from your own sins -- your own choices. Therefore the remedy (forgiveness, sanctification) requires your own choices in response. You must repent because sin was volitional.

This logic is implicit in 2 Nephi 9:6-7: "as death hath passed upon all men" (passive -- it happened to them) → "there must needs be a power of resurrection" (universal remedy for a universal passive condition). Then in 2 Nephi 9:21-23: "he cometh into the world that he may save all men *if they will hearken unto his voice*" (conditional -- because the problem being addressed is now their own choices).

But this explanation is incomplete. It does not explain why the mechanism of universal resurrection requires the same infinite suffering that enables conditional forgiveness. If resurrection is simply the reversal of physical death, why does it require Christ to suffer "the pains of all men" (2 Nephi 9:21)? The suffering seems calibrated to the forgiveness problem, not the resurrection problem. And yet 2 Nephi 9:7 insists it must be "an infinite atonement" or "this corruption could not put on incorruption" -- tying the infinite quality of the suffering to the resurrection result.

**Alma 42:23** provides the most integrated causal chain: "the atonement bringeth to pass the resurrection of the dead; and the resurrection of the dead bringeth back men into the presence of God; and thus they are restored into his presence, to be judged according to their works." Here, resurrection is not the end but the means -- it returns everyone to God's presence for judgment. The universal operation (resurrection) serves as a platform on which the conditional operations (judgment, mercy, assignment to kingdoms) then operate. Resurrection is not "the whole atonement for the righteous"; it is "the minimum atonement for everyone."

## Part 5: The Three-Day Sequence: One Act or Four?

The historical events of Christ's redemptive work unfold across distinct moments:

1. **Gethsemane:** Suffering, bleeding from every pore, taking upon himself the sins and pains of all humanity (Mosiah 3:7; Luke 22:44; D&C 19:18)
2. **The Cross:** Physical death, "It is finished" (John 19:30); "redemption had been wrought through the sacrifice of the Son of God upon the cross" (D&C 138:35)
3. **The Spirit World:** Preaching to the dead, declaring "liberty to the captives who had been faithful" (D&C 138:18-19)
4. **The Resurrection:** Rising from the dead, breaking the bands of death (Mosiah 15:20; Mosiah 16:7)

Scripture never explicitly states whether these constitute one act or four. The closest to a unifying statement is Jacob's "infinite atonement" language in 2 Nephi 9:7, which treats the whole as a single necessary thing. John 19:30 -- "It is finished" -- suggests the cross is a culmination, not the entire event. D&C 138:35 says "redemption had been wrought through the sacrifice of the Son of God upon the cross," locating the wrought-ness at the cross. But D&C 19:18's Gethsemane description and D&C 138's spirit world preaching both present themselves as essential elements, not optional extras.

The LDS tradition has historically emphasized Gethsemane more than the cross (in contrast to most of Christianity), suggesting an instinct that the suffering-for-sins happened there, while the cross accomplished something additional (physical death enabling resurrection). D&C 138 adds a third stage: the spirit world ministry. And the resurrection itself is the fourth. Scripture does not resolve whether these are chapters of one story or four distinct operations that happen to occur in sequence.

## Part 6: Summary of Findings

### What Scripture Clearly Establishes:

1. Christ's redemptive work produces **at least twelve distinguishable operations** (cataloged above).
2. These operations **follow different rules** -- some universal, some conditional; some completed, some ongoing; some tied to specific moments, some timeless.
3. The Book of Mormon uses "atonement" primarily in connection with **sin and the Fall**, not with empathetic succoring or strengthening -- though it describes those operations as flowing from Christ's suffering.
4. The key unifying passages (2 Nephi 9, Alma 34, Alma 42) present a **single enabling act** that produces **multiple distinguishable effects**.
5. **Alma 11:41** explicitly distinguishes resurrection as the one operation that applies even to those for whom the rest of the redemption effectively does not apply.
6. **D&C 76 and 88** describe a graduated spectrum of outcomes, suggesting the atonement's benefits are **modular** -- different people receive different "portions."

### What Scripture Does Not Resolve:

1. **Why** the same infinite suffering is required for both the universal operation (resurrection) and the conditional operations (forgiveness). The logic connecting "infinite atonement" to "incorruption" (2 Nephi 9:7) is asserted, not explained.
2. Whether **the word "atonement"** should be understood as covering all twelve operations or only the core sin-and-death operations. The Book of Mormon's usage favors the narrower reading; modern LDS discourse favors the broader reading.
3. Whether the three-day sequence is **one act or a sequence of distinct acts** that happen to occur together.
4. Whether Christ's succoring capacity (Alma 7:11-12) is a result of the same suffering that accomplished forgiveness, or whether they are **distinct kinds of suffering** with different purposes.
5. How D&C 76's graduated kingdoms relate to the binary categories (saved/not saved, righteous/wicked) that dominate Book of Mormon atonement discourse.

### The Dialectical Tension:

The scriptures simultaneously push toward unity and plurality:

**Toward unity:** The "infinite atonement" language, the singular "great and last sacrifice," the "plan of mercy" as a singular plan, the persistent use of "the atonement" as a definite singular noun.

**Toward plurality:** The radically different rules governing different operations, the explicit carve-out of resurrection as the universal minimum (Alma 11:41), the graduated kingdoms of D&C 76, the distinct purposes cataloged in Alma 7:11-13, the second-order operations (enabling repentance, making weak things strong) that seem categorically different from the first-order operations (forgiving sin, breaking death).

The best resolution the scriptures themselves seem to offer is in Alma 42:23's causal chain: one act produces multiple cascading effects. The unity is in the source; the plurality is in the effects. But this still does not explain why the effects follow such different rules -- unless the different rules are themselves part of the plan, calibrated to the different problems being addressed (involuntary death vs. voluntary sin vs. mortal limitation vs. ignorance).

This remains an open question in the text itself -- a productive tension rather than a resolved paradox.
