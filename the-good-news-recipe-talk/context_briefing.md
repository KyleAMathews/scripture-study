# Context Briefing: How Do "Small and Simple Things" Become Great?

## The Source Material

Elder John D. Amos's October 2025 General Conference talk "The Good News Recipe" teaches that happiness comes through following "God's recipe" in scripture, and when things go wrong, the remedy is to "add more of what invites Jesus Christ into your life." The talk's central story: a missionary struggling with his companion reads Moroni 7:45 (charity), identifies two small and simple things he could do to be kind and two to be patient, takes them to God in prayer, and over weeks transforms the companionship entirely. The missionary's conclusion: "charity never faileth" AND "by small and simple things are great things brought to pass."

The talk quotes President Nelson's steps for personal revelation: find a quiet place, humble yourself, pour out your heart, pray specifically, write your thoughts, follow through with prompted actions, repeat "day after day, month after month, year after year."

## The Core Tension

**How do small and simple things actually become great things?** Three sub-tensions:

### 1. The Mechanism Question
Two models of how the compounding works:

**Model A — Natural Compounding / Defer-Integrate:** Small faithful actions compound through behavioral feedback loops, habit formation, and accumulated practice. The power is already in the person (D&C 58:28: "the power is in them, wherein they are agents unto themselves"). God built the capacity into human agency itself. The missionary's two kind acts triggered a natural upward spiral: kindness → companion softens → easier to be kind → relationship improves → both missionaries grow. No dramatic divine intervention required. The mechanism is psychological, relational, and organic.

**Model B — Divine Multiplication / Enhance-Strip:** Small faithful actions work because God takes inadequate offerings and multiplies them disproportionate to the input. The widow's mite, the loaves and fishes, Ether 12:27 ("I give unto men weakness... then will I make weak things become strong"). The missionary's two small acts were insufficient to transform a broken companionship — but offered with "real intent" and taken to God in prayer, God amplified them. The mechanism is grace. The "great things" exceed what natural compounding could produce.

### 2. The Perception Problem
What counts as "great things"? Many people doing small and simple things may not recognize the transformation:
- If Model A is right, the transformation looks like natural growth — invisible, gradual, indistinguishable from ordinary life. People miss it because they're looking for fireworks.
- If Model B is right, the transformation may be dramatic but happening in dimensions people don't measure — internal change of heart rather than external circumstances.
- Either way: people may be inside the "great thing" without recognizing it.

### 3. The Grit / Persistence Problem
Many people give up on small and simple things before they compound into great things:
- If Model A: grit means trusting the natural process — Rao's "amble," intrinsic navigation, following your path of least resistance. You sustain because it feels natural, not heroic.
- If Model B: grit means continuing to offer inadequate things to God — faith that He will multiply them. You sustain through covenant commitment, not because you see results.
- The giving-up may stem from expecting one model's feedback when God is operating through the other.

## Venkatesh Rao's Frameworks

### The Calculus of Grit (2011)
Rao argues that grit is not superhuman willpower but following your path of least resistance through your strength landscape. Key concepts:

- **"Hard equals wrong."** If something is ridiculously difficult for you, it signals weakness, not meaningful challenge. Modern success requires introspection about your strengths, not superhuman effort.
- **The Three Rs:** Reworking (deliberate revision, not just accumulation), Referencing (building on your own previous work), Releasing (putting work out frequently without timing the market).
- **Grit feels effortless from inside, superhuman from outside.** "I went around calling this faux-grit... but the more I looked around me, the more I realized it wasn't hard for them either." It's "mindful learning across increasingly demanding episodes that build on the same strengths."
- **Intrinsic vs. extrinsic navigation.** Gyroscope (internal sensors) vs. compass (external landmarks). People attached to extrinsic markers (degrees, prizes, titles) have "90% probability" of failure. Progress must be measured internally.
- **Path of least resistance is Sunzi-influenced.** Navigate through your landscape of strengths, not against obstacles.

### Mediocratopia (2019-2021)
A series reframing mediocrity as sustainable practice vs. excellence as unsustainable optimization:

- **The amble** as the right pace — not sprint (unsustainable fervor) nor marathon (gritting teeth through misery), but a relaxed gait that sustains long-term progress.
- **Stay on slopes, not peaks.** People at peaks have nowhere to go but down; people on slopes face meaningful choices. Don't optimize to a local maximum.
- **Rejecting completionism.** The ensō (incomplete Zen circle). Effort that embraces impermanence without obsessing over finishing everything started.
- **"Fitness-to-purposelessness"** — resilience through embodied abundance rather than narrow optimization.
- **Mediocrity as courage.** The courage to be ordinary. Impact comes from surviving long enough in complex environments, not from exceptional traits.
- **Constant self-disruption.** Level up before hitting diminishing returns. Keeps you mediocre relative to your current challenge tier — which is exactly where growth happens.

## Previous Dialectic: The Divine Repertoire

A prior dialectic on Hardy's competence/blessedness pattern produced a synthesis: God engages human agency through an irreducible repertoire of at least eight modes:

| Mode | Logic | Key Texts |
|------|-------|-----------|
| **Replace** | God acts alone; humans stand still | Red Sea, Alma₁'s deliverance |
| **Strip** | God reduces resources to make divine action visible | Gideon's 300, Ether 12:27 |
| **Enhance** | Spirit manifests as heightened skill | Bezalel, Daniel, Pentecost |
| **Instruct** | God provides blueprint; humans execute | Nephi's ship, brother of Jared |
| **Integrate** | Competence and favor seamlessly entangled | Joseph in Egypt, Nehemiah |
| **Defer** | God builds capacity and tells humans to act | D&C 58, Proverbs, Parable of Talents |
| **Withdraw** | Faithful dependence doesn't produce deliverance | Abinadi, Job, Liberty Jail |
| **Presence** | God shows up as being-with, not acting-upon | Still small voice, sacrament |

The current dialectic maps onto this: is "small and simple things" primarily a **Defer/Integrate** phenomenon or an **Enhance/Strip** phenomenon?

## What the Dialectic Must Engage

1. **The mechanism question directly.** Natural compounding vs. divine multiplication — or some synthesis that reframes why this is the wrong binary.
2. **Rao's frameworks seriously.** The calculus of grit and Mediocratopia are not just secular analogues. They may capture something about HOW God's Defer/Integrate modes actually feel from the inside. Or they may miss something essential about the Enhance/Strip modes that makes spiritual growth categorically different from skill development.
3. **The perception problem.** If people are inside the "great thing" without recognizing it, what does that mean for testimony, for motivation, for pastoral care? How do you sustain people through invisible growth?
4. **The grit/giving-up problem.** What makes people quit? Wrong expectations about which mode God is operating in? Extrinsic navigation when only intrinsic works? Sprinting when they should amble? Or genuine despair when the "great things" never materialize — the Withdraw mode intruding on what was supposed to be Defer?
5. **The scriptural evidence.** The talk's missionary story, Alma 37:6-7, the Liahona, D&C 50:24, the change-of-heart scriptures — what do these actually tell us about the mechanism?
6. **The previous dialectic's repertoire.** How does "small and simple things" relate to the eight modes? Is it a phenomenon that cuts across modes, or is it primarily located in specific modes?
7. **The vine metaphor (John 15).** "Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit of itself, except it abide in the vine." This is an organic growth metaphor — but is the growth natural (the vine does what vines do) or supernatural (abiding in Christ produces fruit that no natural vine could)?
