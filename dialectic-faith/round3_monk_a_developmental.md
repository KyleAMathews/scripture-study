# Monk A: The Developmental Trajectory — God's Metaphors Describe a Single Covenantal Progression

## a. The Ontological Claim

God's scriptural metaphors for His relationship with humanity are not a loose anthology of interchangeable images. They describe a **single developmental trajectory** — a progression from radical asymmetry to full covenant intimacy — and covenant is the structural mechanism that governs every transition between stages.

The trajectory runs:

1. **Potter/Clay** — Creation. Pure sovereign initiative. The clay does not choose, does not contribute, does not even exist until the potter acts. This is the metaphor for the moment before relationship begins: the moment God constitutes the being that will later become a partner.

2. **Light** — Universal availability. The sun shines on all. No covenant, no condition, no personal address. This is the pre-personal ground — the light of Christ (Moroni 7:16) that precedes every other interaction. It is the raw material of invitation, broadcast before anyone has ears to hear.

3. **Shepherd/Sheep** — Seeking. The shepherd goes out to find what is lost. The sheep's role is minimal: recognize the voice and follow. This is the stage of initial awakening, where the human being first becomes aware that there is a voice calling. Alma 5:38 — "I say unto you, that the good shepherd doth call you." Not "the good shepherd waits for you to apply." Calls. Seeks. Initiates contact. But notice: even here, the sheep must recognize and follow. Even the most initiative-heavy metaphor contains the germ of human response.

4. **Physician/Patient** — Diagnosis and healing. The patient must acknowledge illness — must submit to the doctor's expertise, must accept the bitter remedy. This is the stage of conviction and repentance, where the human being confronts brokenness and accepts divine treatment. You do not covenant with your doctor in a formal sense, but you enter a relationship of trust and submission that has covenantal structure: "I will diagnose and prescribe. You will comply and heal."

5. **Father/Child** — Nurture and growth toward likeness. The child is not the parent's equal. The child is dependent, needy, ignorant. But the whole point of childhood is that it ends. The child grows toward the parent's likeness. This is the developmental heart of the trajectory: Mosiah 5:7, where King Benjamin's people become "the children of Christ" through covenant. The metaphor shift from subjects to children happens at the covenant point. The relationship enters a new stage through formal commitment.

6. **Vine/Branches** — Organic union. "Abide in me, and I in you" (John 15:4). The branch draws life directly from the vine. Agency is not exercised *despite* the connection but *through* it. This is the stage of sanctification, where the human being's will and God's will begin to converge — not because the human has been overridden but because participation has produced genuine alignment. Jacob 5 is the fullest expression: the Master of the vineyard prunes, grafts, digs, nourishes, and *weeps*. The investment is total. The interdependence is real.

7. **Bridegroom/Bride** — Full covenant intimacy. Mutual delight. Both parties choose. Both parties rejoice. The bride adorns herself; the groom provides the feast. This is the consummation of the trajectory — the marriage covenant of the temple, where the relationship reaches its fullest expression of mutual commitment and mutual joy.

8. **Friend** — Mature partnership beyond hierarchy. "Henceforth I call you not servants... but I have called you friends; for all things that I have heard of my Father I have made known unto you" (John 15:15). D&C 84:63: "ye are my friends." D&C 93:45: treated as collaborators, not subordinates. This is the eschatological horizon — the stage where the developmental trajectory has produced beings who can stand with God as co-laborers, co-creators, joint heirs.

This is not a metaphor salad. It is a single story told in eight movements. And covenant — formal mutual commitment — is what marks every transition point.

## b. My Opponent's Strongest Case — and Its Destruction

The pluralist opponent argues: these metaphors are irreducible modes with no master structure. God shifts fluidly between them. The shepherd is not a stage before the father; the potter is not a stage before the friend. God is all of these simultaneously, deployed situationally based on what the person needs. The plurality itself is the point. Any attempt to impose a developmental sequence is a Procrustean bed that amputates the richness of God's self-disclosure.

The opponent's strongest evidence: **Isaiah 64:8** — "But now, O Lord, thou art our father; we are the clay, and thou our potter." The most intimate metaphor (father) and the most asymmetric (potter/clay) in the same sentence. If the metaphors described a developmental trajectory, you wouldn't find the first and fifth stages collapsed into one breath. This seems to prove that God is simultaneously potter AND father — that the modes coexist rather than succeed one another.

Second strong evidence: **Jacob 5** — the Master of the vineyard uses multiple strategies on the same tree at different times. Pruning here, grafting there, digging around this one, weeping over that one. This looks like adaptive situational response, not a fixed developmental sequence.

Third strong evidence: **Saul's conversion** — God intervenes as shepherd/physician to a person who is not at the "shepherd" stage of development. Saul was actively persecuting the church. If the trajectory were fixed, God would have no business intervening in a person who hasn't reached the "seeking" stage yet. This looks like God breaking the developmental frame entirely.

Now let me destroy each of these.

**Isaiah 64:8** does not prove simultaneity. It proves **developmental memory**. Isaiah is writing on behalf of a fallen people — Israel in exile or under judgment. They have regressed. And in their regression, they reach back to the earliest, most foundational truth: "Thou art our father" — the relationship exists. "We are the clay" — we are in your hands. This is a people who once stood at Sinai in covenant partnership, now thrown back to the beginning. What Isaiah captures is not the simultaneity of all metaphors but the fact that **regression is possible within the trajectory**. A prodigal can return to the father's house, but when he does, he returns as one who needs to be re-formed, re-shaped, re-created. The potter metaphor emerges inside the father metaphor because the wayward child has put himself back in the hands of the one who first made him. This is developmental — it is the developmental structure surviving even failure. The trajectory bends but does not break.

**Jacob 5** is my best evidence, not my opponent's. The allegory of the olive trees is the most elaborately *developmental* text in all of scripture. It spans epochs. Early in the allegory, the master does everything — digging, pruning, nourishing unilaterally (potter/shepherd stage). In the middle sections, the servant is told to follow instructions (father/child stage). By the climactic verses — Jacob 5:70-75 — the master says "let us labor diligently with our might in the vineyard... that we may prepare the way, that I may bring forth again the natural fruit." He says "**we** will labor together." The servant has become a partner. This is the friend stage. The allegory traces exactly the developmental trajectory I am describing, across the entire sweep of salvation history. The multiple strategies are not simultaneous modes — they are the adaptive responses of a God who is working people through developmental stages at different rates. Some trees need pruning (physician). Some need grafting (new covenant). Some need the master's own tears (vine/branches intimacy). The strategies correspond to stages, and the stages move toward partnership.

**Saul's conversion** does not break the developmental frame. It confirms the role of pre-covenantal grace — the light/shepherd stage that precedes all formal covenant. Saul was at stage zero. He was a clay pot that had hardened against its maker. God intervened as potter (struck him blind — radical re-formation), as light (the vision on the road), and as shepherd (calling him by name). The intervention launched Saul into the developmental trajectory. After Damascus, Paul spent years in Arabia (Galatians 1:17-18) — years of growth, formation, and deepening. He moved through stages: from blinded clay to obedient servant to apostolic friend. The conversion is not God breaking the frame. It is God starting the frame — the pre-covenantal, extra-covenantal grace that the Round 2 synthesis already acknowledged as the launch point of the covenant cycle. And critically, even that unilateral intervention required a covenant response: "Lord, what wilt thou have me do?" (Acts 9:6). The developmental trajectory resumed immediately.

## c. A Structural Diagnosis of Why the Pluralist View Fails

The pluralist position is not wrong in some detail. It suffers from a specific structural failure: **it mistakes description for ontology**.

Here is what I mean. The pluralist observes, correctly, that scripture uses many metaphors. The pluralist observes, correctly, that they are not synonymous. The pluralist then makes the fatal inference: because the descriptions are plural, the underlying reality must be structureless. Because no single metaphor captures everything, no structure can organize them.

This is the fallacy of assuming that irreducibility of description entails irreducibility of structure. It does not.

Consider an analogy from within our theology: the plan of salvation itself. Creation, Fall, Atonement, Resurrection, Judgment, Exaltation — these are described with wildly different metaphors, different emotional registers, different theological vocabularies. Creation is spoken of in artisan language (forming, organizing). The Fall uses legal and narrative language (transgression, exile). The Atonement uses sacrificial, medical, legal, and interpersonal language simultaneously. Resurrection is spoken of in terms of bodily transformation. Exaltation uses familial and royal language. A pluralist could argue: "These are irreducible modes! Creation and Atonement are not stages of the same process — they have fundamentally different structures!" But they ARE stages of the same process. The plan of salvation IS a developmental trajectory. The plurality of descriptive metaphors does not disprove the unity of the underlying structure. It reflects the richness of different stages within a single, ordered progression.

The pluralist's deeper failure is **theological**. The position "God's metaphors have no master structure" implies that God's self-disclosure is fundamentally unsystematic — that God relates to humans in an ad hoc way, deploying whatever image seems fitting at the moment, with no governing logic. This makes God an improviser rather than a planner. It makes the plan of salvation a loose collection of divine gestures rather than an ordered progression with a telos.

But the Restoration's central claim is precisely that there IS a plan — a structured, teleological, developmental progression from intelligence to exaltation. D&C 93:12-14: Christ himself received "grace for grace," going "from grace to grace" — a developmental model applied to the very Son of God. If even Christ's relationship with the Father was developmental, how can the pluralist claim that development is not the governing structure?

The pluralist view is theologically lazy. It accepts the surface complexity of scripture and refuses to do the harder work of discerning the order within the complexity. It is the theological equivalent of looking at a cathedral and saying, "There's stone, and glass, and wood, and iron — clearly there is no architect."

## d. The Deeper Principle at Stake

Why does this question matter for the whole theology of faith?

Because if the metaphors have no master structure, then the participatory knowing cycle established in Round 1 and the covenantal frame established in Round 2 are **local truths at best** — descriptions that work for some modes but not others. If God relates to humans through irreducible, structureless plurality, then there is no unified account of how faith operates. Faith-as-participatory-knowledge might apply when God is in "friend mode" but not when God is in "potter mode." The covenant might govern the bridegroom relationship but not the light relationship. The whole dialectical work of Rounds 1 and 2 dissolves into situational applicability.

But if the metaphors describe a developmental trajectory governed by covenant, then **everything coheres**. The participatory knowing of Round 1 IS the mechanism by which one moves through the stages. The covenantal structure of Round 2 IS the architecture that marks and governs the transitions. The whole project snaps into focus:

- Faith begins with the light of Christ (stage 1-2: potter/light). Pre-personal. Pre-covenantal. Pure gift.
- Faith awakens as the shepherd's voice is recognized (stage 3). The first participatory response: hearing and following.
- Faith confronts brokenness as the physician diagnoses (stage 4). Conviction, repentance, submission to the remedy.
- Faith enters covenant as the child enters the family (stage 5). Baptism. The first formal covenant. The relationship shifts from pre-covenantal to covenantal.
- Faith deepens as the branch draws life from the vine (stage 6). Sanctification. The sacramental cycle. Deepening organic union.
- Faith consummates as the bride enters the marriage covenant (stage 7). The temple. The highest ordinances. Full mutual commitment and mutual delight.
- Faith matures as the servant becomes a friend (stage 8). D&C 84:63. The relationship reaches its eschatological fullness. Not hierarchy dissolved but hierarchy transcended — the child has grown up.

The temple endowment enacts this progression. Creation (potter). Fall (the need for a physician/shepherd). Instruction (father/child). Covenants at every stage transition. The veil (the final passage). And beyond the veil: the embrace, the welcoming, the friend standing in the presence of the Friend. The endowment IS the developmental trajectory ritualized. If you want the master structure, go to the temple. It is there.

The deeper principle: **God is not an improviser. God is a developer.** The metaphors are not a scatterplot. They are a curriculum. And covenant is not one metaphor among many — it is the structural mechanism by which a being moves from one stage to the next. You do not drift from clay to friend. You are covenanted from clay to friend, stage by stage, ordinance by ordinance, commitment by commitment.

This matters for the theology of faith because it means faith has a *direction*. It is not just participatory knowing in general. It is participatory knowing that *goes somewhere* — that moves a person through a structured progression toward a specific telos: becoming the kind of being who can stand with God as friend, co-creator, joint heir. Faith without developmental direction is spiritual wandering. Faith within the covenantal trajectory is pilgrimage.

## e. The Extreme Formulation

Let me take this to its most radical, committed form.

**Every metaphor God uses for His relationship with humans can be located on a single developmental axis, and the position on that axis is determined by the covenant status of the relationship.**

There are no free-floating metaphors. There are no modes that exist outside the trajectory. Even the apparent exceptions confirm the rule:

- The light of Christ is not a free-standing mode. It is stage zero — the pre-covenantal ground that enables all subsequent development. It exists to move people into the trajectory.
- God as physician is not a permanent relational mode. It is a *repair* mode — what happens when someone has fallen back in the trajectory and needs to be restored to forward motion. Every physician encounter in scripture is followed by a covenant call: "Go and sin no more." The healing is not the endpoint. The healing restores the person to the developmental path.
- God as potter appears whenever someone has regressed to the point of needing re-formation. It is the trajectory's reset mechanism, not a permanent concurrent mode.
- Even the "situational" deployments follow the developmental logic. God is physician to the sick *in order to make them well enough to be sheep*. God is shepherd to the lost *in order to bring them into the fold where they can become children*. God is father to the children *in order to grow them into friends*. Every metaphor points forward. Every metaphor is a stage serving the next stage.

The most radical claim: **the plurality of metaphors is not evidence of structurelessness. It is evidence of how many stages the developmental journey contains.** The fact that God uses eight or ten or twenty metaphors does not prove there is no master structure. It proves the journey is long and rich and varied. A curriculum with twenty lessons is not structureless because it has twenty lessons. It is structured precisely because each lesson has a specific place in the sequence.

And here is the razor's edge: **if you deny the developmental structure, you cannot explain the temple.** The temple endowment presents a fixed, ordered, non-reversible sequence of covenants, each building on the last, each opening access to the next stage of the divine relationship. If God's metaphors were truly irreducible modes with no master structure, the temple would be incoherent — why present them in a fixed order? Why make each covenant build on the previous one? Why structure the entire ritual as a journey from creation to the presence of God? The temple IS the developmental trajectory made liturgical. Deny the trajectory and you deny the temple's own internal logic.

D&C 93:13-14: "And he received not of the fulness at first, but continued from grace to grace, until he received a fulness." Even the Son of God — the Vine himself, the Bridegroom himself, the Good Shepherd himself — underwent the developmental trajectory. He did not start at the end. He moved through stages. "From grace to grace." If Christ's relationship with the Father was developmental, then development is not a contingent feature of our fallenness. It is the ontological structure of how persons relate to God. Period.

Alma 32 — the foundational text of this entire dialectic — is relentlessly developmental. Seed. Sprout. Sapling. Tree. Fruit. Not: "sometimes a seed, sometimes a tree, sometimes fruit, deployed situationally." Always: seed THEN sprout THEN sapling THEN tree THEN fruit. The developmental sequence is not optional. It is the structure of the thing.

## f. Reasoning Skeleton

1. Scripture uses multiple metaphors for God's relationship with humans (potter, light, shepherd, physician, father, vine, bridegroom, friend). [Empirical observation]

2. These metaphors are not synonymous — they describe relationships with different levels of human agency, divine initiative, and mutuality. [Analysis of briefing table]

3. The metaphors can be ordered along a single axis of increasing mutuality and human agency: potter (none) → light (receive/hide) → shepherd (recognize/follow) → physician (submit/comply) → father/child (grow/become) → vine/branches (abide/bear fruit) → bridegroom (choose/delight) → friend (converse/collaborate). [Structural observation — **LOAD-BEARING**]

4. Scripture explicitly describes transitions between these stages, and the transitions are marked by covenant. Mosiah 5:7: subjects become children through covenant. John 15:15: servants become friends through relational deepening. D&C 84:33-40: the oath and covenant of the priesthood ties the modes (servant, son, friend) to covenant stages. [Scriptural evidence — **LOAD-BEARING**]

5. The temple endowment presents these stages in a fixed, ordered, non-reversible sequence, with covenants marking every transition. [Liturgical evidence — **LOAD-BEARING**]

6. D&C 93:12-14 applies the developmental model to Christ himself ("from grace to grace"), establishing development as the ontological structure of divine-personal relationship, not merely a feature of human fallenness. [Christological evidence]

7. Alma 32 presents faith itself as an irreversibly developmental process (seed → sprout → tree → fruit), not a set of simultaneous modes. [Foundational text of the dialectic]

8. The apparent counter-examples (Isaiah 64:8's simultaneous metaphors, Saul's extra-covenantal conversion, Jacob 5's multiple strategies) are better explained as developmental regression, pre-covenantal launch, and stage-adaptive response within the trajectory than as evidence of structurelessness. [Counter-argument rebuttal]

9. **Therefore:** The metaphors describe a single developmental trajectory, and covenant is the structural mechanism governing transitions between stages. The plurality of metaphors reflects the richness of the developmental journey, not the absence of structure.

**Load-bearing premises:** 3, 4, and 5. If the ordering in premise 3 is arbitrary rather than developmental, the argument fails. If the covenant transitions in premise 4 are coincidental rather than structural, the argument fails. If the temple's ordering in premise 5 is liturgical convention rather than ontological structure, the argument fails. I believe none of these are the case — the convergence of scriptural sequence, covenant theology, and temple liturgy all pointing to the same developmental structure is not coincidence. It is revelation.

The pluralist must explain the temple. The pluralist must explain D&C 93. The pluralist must explain Alma 32. The pluralist must explain why John 15:15 says "henceforth I call you NOT servants... but friends" — using a temporal marker ("henceforth") that explicitly signals a stage transition, not a mode switch. The pluralist cannot. The developmental trajectory can. That is why I believe it, and I believe it is true.
