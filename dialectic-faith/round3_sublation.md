# Phase 6: Round 3 Sublation — The Economy of Salvation

## Initial Guess (Pre-synthesis)
The metaphors describe differentiated roles within an economy of salvation where a covenant community mediates God's saving work to all humanity, with development and plurality both real but operating at different scales.

## The Synthesis: The Vineyard, Not the Tree

### The Reframe

Both monks analyzed the wrong unit. Monk A asked: how does an *individual's* relationship with God develop through stages? Monk B asked: how does God relate to *an individual* through multiple simultaneous modes? Both assumed the metaphors describe God's relationship with a person. But the scriptural metaphors predominantly operate at a different scale: God's relationship with a **people**, deployed within a **strategy** to reach **all His children**.

The controlling metaphor is not any single image of God-to-person relationship. It is **Jacob 5: the vineyard**. The Master of the vineyard is not managing one tree. He is managing a vineyard — an ecosystem of trees in different conditions, different soils, different stages, requiring different interventions, all in service of a single goal: "that I may preserve unto myself the natural fruit" (Jacob 5:74).

The vineyard is the economy of salvation. And the metaphors both monks debated are not descriptions of *what God is to you*. They are descriptions of **what God is doing in the vineyard** — the differentiated operations of a God managing a global, trans-temporal strategy for bringing all His children home.

### Development Is Real — But It Describes the Instrument, Not the Universal

Monk A was right that there is developmental structure. There IS a progression from potter/clay through shepherd/sheep through father/child to friend. The temple IS this trajectory ritualized. Covenants DO mark the transitions. Alma 32's seed-to-tree IS irreversibly developmental.

But this trajectory describes the **formation of the instrument** — how a person is shaped into someone who can serve in God's economy. The developmental trajectory exists not to produce finished individuals but to produce **workers in the vineyard**. Jacob 5:70: "Ye shall labor with me in the vineyard for the last time." The end of the developmental trajectory is not personal completion. It is commission. You develop FROM clay TO friend so that you can be deployed as shepherd, physician, light-bearer, and leaven for others.

The temple confirms this. The endowment does not end with personal exaltation. It ends with the veil — and what happens at the veil? You are received into God's presence, yes. But the temple's daily practice is: you go through the endowment ON BEHALF OF THE DEAD. The developmental trajectory is completed in vicarious service. The instrument is formed so that it can be used.

D&C 84:33-40 — the oath and covenant of the priesthood — confirms the same structure. You progress from servant to son to friend... and then what? "All that my Father hath shall be given unto him" — not as a reward but as a commissioning. You receive everything the Father has so you can DO what the Father does: seek, heal, nurture, covenant, gather, save.

### Plurality Is Real — But It Describes the Vineyard's Needs, Not God's Nature

Monk B was right that the metaphors are genuinely plural and irreducible. The shepherd mode does different work than the physician mode. The potter mode addresses different situations than the friend mode. No single metaphor subsumes the others.

But this plurality is not a revelation about God's *nature* (as Monk B claimed) — it is a revelation about the **vineyard's diversity**. God uses multiple modes because His children are in radically different conditions. Some are lost (shepherd). Some are broken (physician). Some are unformed (potter). Some are growing (father/child). Some are mature (friend). The plurality of modes matches the plurality of human conditions, not some irreducible plurality in God's being.

This is what Jacob 5 shows with extraordinary precision. The Master doesn't use different strategies because he has an "irreducibly plural nature." He uses different strategies because different trees are in different conditions. Some need pruning. Some need grafting. Some need to be transplanted to different soil. Some need weeping over. The plurality of operations is driven by the diversity of the vineyard, not the nature of the gardener.

And critically: the modes are deployed THROUGH the covenant community, not just by God directly. The shepherd seeks the lost — through missionaries. The physician heals the broken — through ministers, bishops, friends who sit with you in your darkness. The light illuminates — through covenant people living as "a light unto the Gentiles" (Isaiah 49:6). The leaven transforms — through the small community's influence on the larger world. God's multi-modal engagement with His children is operationalized through a covenant people who have themselves been developed (Monk A's trajectory) into instruments capable of performing these differentiated roles.

### The Economy: How the Few Serve the Many

Here is the emergent structure that neither monk could see:

**The economy of salvation has three layers:**

**Layer 1: Universal Grace (Pre-covenantal)**
The light of Christ given to every person (Moroni 7:16). Conscience. Moral intuition. The baseline awareness that there is something more. This is God as light — constant, universal, pre-personal. It reaches 100% of God's children. It does not depend on covenant, community, or even awareness of God. It is the ground floor.

The narrow way essay's triad of obstacles — pride, hardness, worldliness — operates primarily at this layer. Most humans never move beyond Layer 1 in mortality. They have the light of Christ but never encounter the shepherd's call in covenantal form. They are the trees in the far corners of Jacob 5's vineyard, the wild olive branches that don't even know there IS a vineyard.

**Layer 2: The Covenant Community (Instrumental)**
The small minority who enter covenant. They have been found by the shepherd, healed by the physician, adopted by the Father, grafted into the vine. They are INSIDE the developmental trajectory. They are moving from clay to friend, from grace to grace.

But — and this is the crucial move — they are not developing for their own sake. They are being formed as instruments. The Abrahamic covenant is explicit: "in thee shall all families of the earth be blessed" (Genesis 12:3). The covenant is not privilege; it is commission. "I will also give thee for a light to the Gentiles" (Isaiah 49:6). "Ye are the salt of the earth" (Matthew 5:13). "Saviors on Mount Zion" (Obadiah 1:21).

The metaphor plurality becomes intelligible here. The covenant community doesn't deploy just ONE mode toward the world. It deploys ALL of God's modes: shepherding the lost, healing the broken, nurturing the new, teaching the growing, partnering with the mature. The church needs to be simultaneously hospital, school, search party, family, and team — because the vineyard's needs are plural. The irreducible plurality of metaphors describes the irreducible plurality of the community's MISSION, not God's nature.

**Layer 3: The Trans-Temporal Extension (Vicarious)**
This is the Restoration's most radical claim: the covenant extends beyond mortality. Temple work for the dead. The spirit world as continuation of the gospel opportunity. D&C 128:15: "they without us cannot be made perfect — neither can we without them be made perfect."

The economy of salvation is not complete in mortality. The vast majority of God's children who lived and died without covenant access are not abandoned by the economy — they are its FUTURE recipients. The covenant community in Layer 2 exists to extend the covenant to Layer 1 populations, both living (missionary work) and dead (temple work). The developmental trajectory is not limited to the mortal covenant minority. It is the trajectory that the economy EVENTUALLY extends to everyone, through the mediating work of the covenant community.

This dissolves the "narrow way" tension. Why does God work through a small covenant people? Because the economy of salvation is an expanding circle. The covenant community is the expanding edge — the leaven working through the dough. The smallness is not failure but design: a small, faithful, well-formed community can permeate and transform the whole, given enough time. And God's timetable extends beyond mortality.

### Why Both Monks' Questions Were Malformed

Monk A's question — "are the metaphors a developmental sequence?" — was malformed because it assumed the developmental trajectory IS the structure of God's relationship with all humans. It isn't. It's the structure of how the instrument is formed.

Monk B's question — "are the metaphors irreducible modes of God's nature?" — was malformed because it located the plurality in God's nature rather than in the vineyard's needs. God's nature may well be irreducibly rich. But the REASON scripture gives us multiple metaphors is not primarily to reveal God's nature. It is to describe the differentiated operations of a God running a complex, multi-modal, trans-temporal economy of salvation through a covenant people.

The abduction test: if the economy-of-salvation frame is true, both monks' positions become *predictable*. OF COURSE the metaphors have developmental structure — because the covenant community members ARE developing through stages as they are formed into instruments. OF COURSE the metaphors are irreducibly plural — because the vineyard's needs are irreducibly diverse. OF COURSE the metaphors coexist simultaneously — because the community is simultaneously shepherding, healing, nurturing, teaching, and partnering in different contexts. The original contradiction dissolves: development describes the instrument's formation; plurality describes the instrument's mission. Both are real, operating at different scales, serving the same economy.

### The Sacrament, Reframed Economically

The Round 2 synthesis described the sacrament as a micro-cycle of the individual covenant. The economy frame adds a dimension: the sacrament is a **communal recommissioning**. Every week, the covenant community gathers, renews its commitment, receives the Spirit — and is re-sent into the vineyard. "Always remember him" is not just individual devotion. It is the instrument remembering its purpose. "Keep his commandments" is not just individual obedience. It is the covenant community renewing its commission to be salt, light, leaven, and witness to the world.

The bread and water go to every person in the congregation — the newly baptized and the long-faithful, the struggling and the strong, the child and the aged. The sacrament does not operate on a developmental gradient (more sacrament for the mature, less for the new). It operates on the economy's logic: everyone in the covenant community receives the same renewal because they are all instruments being re-calibrated for the same mission.

### How the Instrument Actually Works: D&C 121:34-46

The auditor's sharpest critique was: "The economy works only if its participants do not experience it as an economy. The moment the shepherd thinks 'I am an instrument being deployed,' the shepherding becomes bureaucratic and loses its power."

D&C 121 answers this directly — and in the process connects all three rounds of the dialectic.

**The instrument-failure problem is real and nearly universal.** "We have learned by sad experience that it is the nature and disposition of almost all men, as soon as they get a little authority, as they suppose, they will immediately begin to exercise unrighteous dominion" (v39). The covenant community IS unreliable. Almost all who receive authority corrupt it. "Many are called, but few are chosen" (v34) — not because God fails to choose them but because they disqualify themselves through pride, worldliness, and compulsion (v35).

**The failure mode is specific: switching to domination.** When someone exercises "control or dominion or compulsion upon the souls of the children of men" — Satan's mode, Morgan's Metaphor #8 — "the heavens withdraw themselves; the Spirit of the Lord is grieved; and when it is withdrawn, Amen to the priesthood or the authority of that man" (v37). The instrument breaks the moment it adopts the mode the economy was designed to reject. The Round 2 insight (domination vs. covenant) returns here at the operational level.

**But the remedy reveals what the instrument IS:** "No power or influence can or ought to be maintained by virtue of the priesthood, only by persuasion, by long-suffering, by gentleness and meekness, and by love unfeigned; by kindness, and pure knowledge" (v41-42). The instrument that works is not a tool being deployed. It is a PERSON whose character has been formed to operate the way God operates — through persuasion, patience, and genuine love. The instrument IS the person.

**And then the payoff — which connects all three rounds:** "Let thy bowels also be full of charity towards all men, and to the household of faith, and let virtue garnish thy thoughts unceasingly; then shall thy **confidence wax strong in the presence of God**" (v45). This is the Lectures on Faith definition of faith. Confidence before God, produced by charity toward all and virtue in thought. The participatory knowing of Round 1 — knowing God by becoming like God — is HERE. What produces confidence before God? Not theological achievement. Not developmental stage completion. Charity toward all men. Virtue in thought. The person who loves like God develops the knowledge of God that IS faith.

**The telos:** "Without compulsory means it shall flow unto thee forever and ever" (v46). The developmental trajectory's endpoint is not commission as a task but transformation into a person whose influence flows naturally from who they are. The economy's participants don't experience it as economy because their service flows from their CHARACTER, not from a role they perform. The instrument and the person are the same thing. Becoming like God — full of charity, operating through persuasion and love — just IS being an effective participant in the economy of salvation. There is no gap between personal development and instrumental deployment because what the economy needs is people who love like God does.

This dissolves the instrumentalization concern entirely. The economy does not subordinate individuals to their function. The function IS the fullest expression of who the individual has become. Moses 1:39 ("to bring to pass the immortality and eternal life of man") and Jacob 5:70 ("let us labor together") are not competing goals. They are the same goal described from different angles — because a person whose eternal life is being brought to pass IS a person who labors in the vineyard through love unfeigned, and a person who labors in the vineyard through love unfeigned IS a person whose eternal life is being brought to pass.

### The War in Heaven, Economically Reframed

Round 1 framed the war in heaven as a dispute about participatory knowing. Round 2 framed it as a dispute about covenantal vs. domination models. The economy frame adds: Satan's plan would have eliminated the NEED for an economy of salvation.

If every person is compelled to obedience, there are no lost sheep to seek, no sick to heal, no lost to find, no ignorant to teach. The entire multi-modal, multi-generational, trans-temporal economy becomes unnecessary. One mode suffices: compliance. The richness of the metaphor landscape — shepherd, physician, father, friend, light, vine, leaven, salt — exists BECAUSE the plan preserves agency, which means people will be in wildly different conditions, which means God needs a multi-modal strategy, which means God needs a covenant people formed and deployed as differentiated instruments.

The plurality of metaphors is a consequence of agency. A universe with genuine freedom is a vineyard with trees in every possible condition, requiring every possible intervention. A universe without freedom would need only one metaphor: master/slave. The richness of God's relational vocabulary is itself evidence of the seriousness of His commitment to agency.

D&C 121 makes this even more precise: the economy's instruments can ONLY operate through the non-compulsory modes — persuasion, long-suffering, love. The moment they switch to compulsion, the powers of heaven withdraw. The economy is structurally incompatible with domination. This is not a policy preference but a metaphysical constraint: the powers of heaven "cannot be controlled nor handled only upon the principles of righteousness" (v36). The economy runs on love or it doesn't run.

### Jacob 5 as Master Text

Jacob 5 is the synthesis text — the one that contains both monks' insights and transcends them:

- It is **developmental**: the allegory spans epochs, the trees mature (or deteriorate), the strategies adapt to changing conditions over time.
- It is **plural**: the Master uses radically different strategies for different trees — pruning, grafting, transplanting, digging, nourishing, weeping.
- It is **communal**: the Master works with servants ("let us labor together"), and the servants become progressively more central to the work. By the end, the servants are partners.
- It is **economic**: the entire allegory is about producing fruit — not for the trees' own sake but for the vineyard's sake. The goal is not individual tree perfection but vineyard-wide fruitfulness.
- It is **trans-temporal**: the allegory spans the whole of human history and beyond, with the final harvest still in the future.
- It is **emotionally invested**: the Master weeps (Jacob 5:41). This is not a bureaucrat managing a project. This is a person who loves the trees and grieves when they fail to bear fruit.

The Master of the vineyard is not just potter, or just shepherd, or just father, or just friend. He is all of these and more, deployed adaptively across an economy that spans all of history, working through servants who are themselves being developed from hired hands into partners, all in service of one goal: "that I may preserve unto myself the natural fruit" — that every tree in the vineyard, eventually, might bear the fruit it was created to bear.

## Model Update

- **Before (Round 2):** The participatory knowing cycle operates through covenant — mutual commitment with asymmetric reliability. Human agency is the decisive variable within a structure that is God's gift. The covenant is launched by pre-covenantal grace and sometimes interrupted by extra-covenantal divine initiative.
- **After (Round 3):** The covenant is the structure, but the FUNCTION of the covenant is not individual spiritual development as a private achievement — it is the formation of persons who operate the way God operates: through persuasion, long-suffering, gentleness, and love unfeigned (D&C 121:41-42). God's multiple metaphors describe: (1) the developmental trajectory of formation (potter → shepherd → father → friend, marked by covenant transitions), and (2) the plural modes of mission (shepherding, healing, teaching, nurturing, partnering — matching the vineyard's diverse needs). Development and plurality are both real, operating at different scales. The economy extends trans-temporally through vicarious work for the dead. The covenant people are not the endpoint of God's saving work but the expanding edge — leaven, salt, light. There is no gap between personal development and instrumental deployment because what the economy needs IS people who love like God does, and becoming a person who loves like God does IS eternal life (D&C 121:45-46). D&C 121:34-46 and Jacob 5 are the master texts.
- **Because:** Both monks assumed the individual as the unit of analysis and God's direct relationship with that individual as the subject. The narrow way essay revealed that the vast majority of God's children are OUTSIDE formal covenant, which means any account of how God relates to humans must explain the economy by which the covenant few serve the non-covenant many. The metaphors describe not just what God is to you but what God is doing in the vineyard — and what role you are being formed to play in it.

## New Contradictions Generated

1. **The quality-of-instrument problem.** If the covenant community is supposed to be the instrument through which God reaches all His children, the instrument is manifestly unreliable. Israel repeatedly failed its commission. The church has repeatedly turned inward. Salt loses its savor. If the economy depends on human instruments, the economy inherits all the failures of human agency — which the Round 2 synthesis already identified as the source of ALL failure in the cycle. Does the economy have a mechanism for surviving instrument failure? Or is instrument failure the reason "few there be that find it"?

2. **The "almost all men" problem.** D&C 121:39 — "almost all men, as soon as they get a little authority... will immediately begin to exercise unrighteous dominion." If the economy depends on instruments who operate through love and persuasion, and almost all who receive authority corrupt it into compulsion, the economy's throughput is devastatingly low. This may be the structural explanation for "few there be that find it" — not that the gate is hard to find, but that the instruments who should be guiding people to it keep breaking. What does the economy look like when the instrument failure rate is nearly 100%?

3. **The grief of God.** Jacob 5:41 — the Master weeps. The economy of salvation costs God something. If God could work faster, use better instruments, bypass the narrow way — but doesn't, because of agency — then the economy of salvation is simultaneously the most loving and the most painful possible arrangement. What does it mean theologically that God chose an economy He knew would cause Him grief?

4. **The eschatological horizon.** D&C 93:12-14 and the developmental trajectory both point toward a telos: beings who can stand with God as friends, co-creators, joint heirs. But the economy of salvation is not just about getting there. It is about EVERYONE getting there. What does it mean for the economy to be "finished"? Is there a point where the vineyard's work is done — where every tree has borne fruit, every person has been reached, every covenant extended? Or is the economy itself eternal?
