# Context Briefing: What Is Faith?

## The Dialectical Question

Does faith function primarily as an **accumulated foundation** — a growing body of knowledge about God's character that produces confidence and enables action — or primarily as a **leap into darkness** — the willingness to act at the edge of what you know, precisely where your foundation runs out?

Both are scriptural. Both are valued. The question is which is *primary* — which is the engine, and which is the byproduct.

## The User's Position and Interview Material

The user holds a sophisticated, integrated view of faith that combines:

1. **Faith as knowledge that drives action.** Faith is knowledge of things as they are (or as you hope they are), and this knowledge pushes you to action. Drawing on Lectures on Faith: to have faith you must know who God is, understand his character, and understand how he views the course of your life. This is how you know how to interact with God — do you have confidence before God or not?

2. **Faith as desire and recognition.** The veil in this life is intended to draw out innermost desires. Faith in God exists largely because we *want* there to be someone like God. The elect are described as people who "hear my voice and know who I am" — recognition of something already known, or already desired.

3. **Faith as character formation, not short-term reward-seeking.** Faith doesn't drive you toward actions with expected short-term rewards — that's a recipe for failure. It makes you "sure and steadfast, always abounding in good works" (Ether 12:4). You're an agent free to act without being commanded (D&C 58:26-28).

4. **The irreducible gap.** There's always a gap between what you know and what you hope. A lot of what we do is guesswork. This gap is a *feature* of mortality, not a bug — the veil is designed to test what we'll do with incomplete knowledge.

The user explicitly rejected the idea that knowledge and action are in tension — "those feel like the same thing." Knowledge of God enables and directs action; without it you're drifting. But the user also acknowledged that "a lot of what we do is guesswork" and that the leap — acting on a mustard seed — is real and recurrent.

## Key Scriptural Evidence

### Foundation Side

**Lectures on Faith (Lecture 1):** "Faith is the principle of action in all intelligent beings." Faith operates universally — God himself framed the worlds through faith.

**Lectures on Faith (Lecture 3):** Three prerequisites for faith:
1. Knowledge that God exists
2. Correct understanding of God's character, perfections, and attributes
3. Knowledge that one's course of life is according to God's will

**Lectures on Faith (Lecture 4):** Six attributes of God that are necessary for faith: knowledge, power, justice, judgment, mercy, truth. Without knowledge of these attributes, "the mind of man could have nothing upon which it could rest with certainty: all would be confusion and doubt."

**Alma 32:28:** The seed experiment — plant the word, and if it's good, "it beginneth to enlarge my soul; yea, it beginneth to enlighten my understanding." Faith produces experiential knowledge that accumulates.

**Alma 32:34:** "Your knowledge is perfect in that thing, and your faith is dormant" — after the experiment, knowledge replaces faith on that specific point. Faith becomes dormant where knowledge has been gained.

**Ether 3:19:** The brother of Jared: "he had faith no longer, for he knew, nothing doubting." Faith explicitly ends when perfect knowledge arrives. His accumulated faith *completed its trajectory.*

**Hebrews 11:1:** "Faith is the substance [GR: assurance, basis, foundation] of things hoped for, the evidence [GR: proof] of things not seen." Note the Greek — substance/assurance/foundation.

**Hebrews 11:6:** "He that cometh to God must believe that he is, and that he is a rewarder of them that diligently seek him." Prerequisite knowledge: God exists and rewards faithfulness.

**D&C 93:24:** "Truth is knowledge of things as they are, and as they were, and as they are to come." Faith as proto-truth, moving toward full knowledge.

### Leap Side

**Alma 32:27:** "Even if ye can no more than desire to believe, let this desire work in you." Faith can begin from bare desire — no foundation required, just willingness.

**Ether 12:6:** "Ye receive no witness until after the trial of your faith." The confirmation comes *after* the leap, not before. You must act in darkness first.

**Ether 12:4:** Faith "maketh an anchor to the souls of men, which would make them sure and steadfast, always abounding in good works." The fruit of faith is character and steady action, not knowledge acquisition.

**Ether 12:19:** "Many whose faith was so exceedingly strong... who could not be kept from within the veil, but truly saw with their eyes the things which they had beheld with an eye of faith." Faith as a way of *seeing* — an eye of faith that precedes and enables physical seeing.

**Ether 12:27:** "I give unto men weakness that they may be humble." Weakness — the gap — is given by God. It's structural, not accidental.

**D&C 58:26-28:** "It is not meet that I should command in all things... men should be anxiously engaged in a good cause, and do many things of their own free will." Faith produces agents who act freely in darkness, not servants who wait for light.

**Hebrews 11:17:** "By faith Abraham, when he was tried, offered up Isaac." Abraham had received promises, had direct experience with God — massive foundation. Still required to leap into something his foundation could not justify.

**Boyd K. Packer (BYU talk):** Harold B. Lee taught: "You must learn to walk to the edge of the light, and then a few steps into the darkness." This wasn't advice for beginners — it was counsel for an apostle. The darkness doesn't go away.

### The Tension in Scripture

The scriptures seem genuinely inconsistent — or at least more complex than a single linear model:

- **Ether 3:19** says faith ends when knowledge arrives (brother of Jared).
- **Hebrews 11:17** says the most knowledgeable person in scripture (Abraham, who had spoken with God face to face) was asked to make the hardest leap.
- **Alma 32:34** says faith becomes dormant on the specific point where knowledge is gained — but **Alma 32:36** immediately says "neither must ye lay aside your faith, for ye have only exercised your faith to plant the seed." The knowledge is partial; the tree still needs nourishing.
- **Alma 32:41** describes nourishing the tree "by your faith with great diligence, and with patience" — faith is still active even as knowledge grows.

## Ontological Question

What IS faith — in its deepest nature? Is it a mode of knowing (epistemic), a mode of being (existential/characterological), or something else entirely? And does the answer change what it means to "have" faith, "exercise" faith, and "lose" faith?

## Degenerate Framings to Avoid

- "Faith vs. works" — this is a different debate entirely (James 2). Both positions here affirm that faith produces action.
- "Blind faith vs. evidence-based faith" — this flattens the tension. Both sides agree faith requires *some* knowledge. The question is whether knowledge is the engine or the platform.
- "Faith is just trust" — too simple. The Lectures on Faith framework is more specific than generic trust.
- "Faith is the first step, knowledge is the destination" — this is the foundation view stated too simply. It doesn't account for Abraham.
