# Faith Is the Leap: An Argument from the Edge

## I. What Faith Is

Faith is the act of stepping beyond what you know into what you do not know, and acting there. It is not the knowledge that precedes the step. It is not the confidence that accumulates through experience. It is not the platform you have built over years of spiritual practice. Faith is what happens at the edge of that platform, when the platform runs out, and you move forward anyway.

This is not a claim that faith is blind. Blindness implies no orientation, no sense of direction, no relationship with the terrain. The person who steps into darkness in faith has a platform beneath them -- they know something of God, something of his character, something of what he asks. They are not falling at random. But the platform is not the faith. The platform is the platform. Faith is the motion beyond it, into the space where the platform does not reach.

The ontological claim is precise: faith is an existential act, not an epistemic state. It is a mode of being, not a mode of knowing. You do not *have* faith the way you have knowledge. You *exercise* faith the way you exercise a muscle -- in real time, under load, at the point of resistance. The moment you are no longer at the edge, you are no longer exercising faith. You are resting on knowledge. And rest is not faith.

## II. The Opponent's Strongest Case

The foundation position's strongest claim is that the Lectures on Faith define faith as requiring three specific epistemic prerequisites -- knowledge that God exists, correct understanding of his character and attributes, and assurance that one's life course aligns with his will -- and that without these, faith cannot operate. Lecture 4 states explicitly that without knowledge of God's attributes, "the mind of man could have nothing upon which it could rest with certainty: all would be confusion and doubt." The foundation position reads this as definitional: faith *is* this knowledge-producing-confidence, and the act of stepping forward is merely the downstream expression of an already-complete faith. They cite Ether 3:19 -- "he had faith no longer, for he knew, nothing doubting" -- as the clinching proof that faith and knowledge sit on a single continuum, that faith is proto-knowledge, and that faith's telos is to graduate into knowledge. On this reading, faith is the engine of a knowledge trajectory; the gap is not the site of faith but the obstacle faith is designed to close.

This is a serious argument. It has textual support. It has systematic coherence. And it fails at the structural level.

## III. Where the Foundation Fails

The foundation position fails because it cannot account for Abraham.

Hebrews 11:17 is the wrecking ball. Abraham is the single most knowledgeable mortal in the scriptural record on the dimension that matters. He has spoken with God face to face. He has received covenantal promises -- specific, named, generational promises -- about Isaac. His foundation is not a mustard seed. It is bedrock. By the Lectures on Faith framework, Abraham possesses all three prerequisites in their fullest possible mortal expression: he knows God exists, he understands God's character with experiential depth no other mortal matched at that point, and he has received explicit confirmation that his life course accords with God's will.

And then God asks him to kill Isaac.

This is not a request that Abraham's foundation can process. His knowledge of God's character -- that God keeps promises, that God is just, that God is truthful -- directly contradicts the command. His knowledge does not produce confidence here; it produces a collision. If faith were the knowledge-and-confidence, Abraham would have *more* faith available to handle this than anyone in history, and the sacrifice of Isaac would be the *easiest* act of faith ever performed, not the hardest. But every reader of Genesis knows it is the hardest. The text treats it as the supreme trial precisely because Abraham's foundation gives him every reason not to act.

What Abraham does on Moriah is not an expression of accumulated knowledge. It is a leap beyond knowledge into a space where his knowledge cannot follow. He acts in a darkness that his own foundation has made darker, not lighter. And *that* -- not the decades of conversation with God that preceded it -- is what Hebrews calls faith.

The foundation position might respond: Abraham's knowledge of God's character -- specifically, that God keeps promises -- is exactly what enabled him to trust that God would resolve the contradiction. Hebrews 11:19 says Abraham "accounting that God was able to raise him up, even from the dead." So the foundation produced the leap after all.

But notice what this concession actually says. It says Abraham's foundation got him to the edge. It gave him a reason to believe the contradiction might resolve. It did not tell him *how*. It did not eliminate the darkness. It did not make the knife lighter in his hand. The foundation brought him to the cliff's edge and then -- this is the critical structural point -- faith was the act of stepping off. The foundation is the necessary condition; the leap is the sufficient condition. And sufficient conditions are what define the thing.

## IV. The Lectures on Faith, Read Correctly

The Lectures on Faith do not actually support the foundation position as cleanly as it claims. Lecture 1 defines faith as "the principle of action in all intelligent beings." Action. Not knowledge. Not confidence. Faith is what makes beings *act*. The three prerequisites of Lecture 3 are conditions *for* faith, not the substance *of* faith. Lecture 3 is describing what you need to have on the platform before you can leap; it is not describing the leap. A runway is not a flight.

The Lectures even support this reading internally. Lecture 6 says that to have faith unto salvation, a person must be "willing to sacrifice all earthly things." Sacrifice. The willingness to give up what you have -- including, implicitly, the certainty you have built -- for something you cannot yet see. This is not a description of standing confidently on knowledge. It is a description of releasing your grip on the platform.

And Ether 3:19 -- the brother of Jared's faith ending in knowledge -- does not prove what the foundation position thinks it proves. Yes, faith became dormant on *that specific point* when knowledge arrived. But Alma 32:36 immediately warns: "neither must ye lay aside your faith, for ye have only exercised your faith to plant the seed." The knowledge is local. The tree still grows. New edges appear. Faith does not graduate into knowledge as a permanent state; it graduates into knowledge on one point and then discovers it is standing at a new edge, facing a new darkness, requiring a new leap. The brother of Jared's faith ended on the question of God's nature. It did not end on every question. The veil did not dissolve permanently. He still had to live, act, lead, and die without knowing everything. Ether 3:19 describes a moment, not a trajectory's completion.

## V. The Deeper Principle

The veil is not an obstacle to be overcome. It is the instrument of spiritual formation.

Ether 12:27 states: "I give unto men weakness that they may be humble." God gives the gap. He designs it. He maintains it. If faith were fundamentally a knowledge trajectory whose telos is the elimination of the gap, then God is designing a system whose purpose is to make itself obsolete. But the veil does not thin as you accumulate knowledge. Ask any apostle. Boyd K. Packer recounted Harold B. Lee's counsel: "You must learn to walk to the edge of the light, and then a few steps into the darkness." This was not advice for investigators. It was counsel from one apostle to another. The darkness does not retreat. Each new foundation only moves the edge.

D&C 58:26-28 reveals the design purpose: "It is not meet that I should command in all things... men should be anxiously engaged in a good cause, and do many things of their own free will." God wants agents who act in darkness, not servants who wait for light. The gap is not a deficiency in the system. It is the system. Faith is the capacity to act within that permanent gap, and the gap is permanent because agency requires it.

## VI. The Extreme Formulation

Here is the uncomfortable version: the more you know, the harder faith gets, not the easier. Every new piece of knowledge extends the platform, but it also extends the edge. Abraham's trial was harder than Alma's converts' trial precisely because Abraham knew more. The brother of Jared's faith was exceptional not because he had built a towering foundation -- though he had -- but because at each stage he was willing to step beyond that foundation into requests he could not justify from what he already knew. "What will ye that I should do?" the Lord asks about the barges' light. The brother of Jared has to *invent the answer*. He has to act in the dark. His knowledge of God got him to the mountain. His faith was the moment he held out the stones.

If faith were the foundation, then the spiritually mature would find faith easy. But every testimony from every prophet suggests the opposite. The trials scale with the knowledge. The darkness at the edge is always exactly as dark as it needs to be to require a genuine leap. This is because faith is not the foundation. Faith is what the foundation makes possible but cannot perform. Faith is the leap.

## VII. The Reasoning Skeleton

My argument rests on four load-bearing claims:

1. **Faith is ontologically an act, not a state.** The Lectures on Faith define it as "the principle of action." If faith were a state (knowledge-plus-confidence), it would not require exercise; it would simply persist. But scripture consistently describes faith as something you *do*, not something you *have*.

2. **The veil is permanent and purposive.** God designed the gap, maintains it, and scales it to each person's growth. The gap is not a problem faith solves; it is the environment faith requires.

3. **Abraham is the decisive case.** The person with the greatest foundation was given the hardest leap. This is inexplicable if faith is the foundation. It is perfectly explicable if faith is the leap the foundation enables but cannot replace.

4. **Knowledge replaces faith locally, not globally.** Alma 32:34 and Ether 3:19 describe faith becoming dormant on specific points. But new edges always appear. Faith is never finished because the darkness never runs out.

If claim 3 falls -- if someone can show that Abraham's sacrifice was *easy* because of his foundation, or that it was not actually an act of faith -- then my argument collapses. But I do not think any honest reader of Genesis 22 can make that case. The knife was heavy. The mountain was dark. And the walk up Moriah, not the decades of conversation that preceded it, is what scripture calls faith.
