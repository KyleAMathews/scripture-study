# The Cycle Is Governed by Human Response

## a. The Ontological Claim

The participatory cycle of faith works because of what *we* do at each node. God provides input -- light, Spirit, enticings, opportunity -- and His provision is faithful, constant, and universally available. The variation that determines whether the cycle spirals upward into the full knowledge of God or collapses downward into the chains of hell is *entirely on the human side*. We are the variable. God is the constant. "The power is in them, wherein they are agents unto themselves" (D&C 58:28).

This is not a claim that God is passive. God is the sun. The sun shines on every field. But whether a field yields wheat or weeds depends on what the farmer plants and whether the farmer cultivates. The sun does not "calibrate" itself to individual fields. It does not shine harder on the diligent farmer and dimmer on the lazy one. It shines. The variation is the farmer's, and the harvest is the farmer's, and the failure is the farmer's. To credit the sun with the variation is to misunderstand what kind of system agriculture is. And to credit God with the variation in human spiritual trajectories is to misunderstand what kind of system agency is.

Alma 12:9-11 maps the cycle with surgical precision: God grants "the word" according to "the heed and diligence which they give unto him." Those who do not harden their hearts receive "the greater portion of the word, until it is given unto him to know the mysteries of God in full." Those who harden their hearts receive "the lesser portion of the word until they know nothing concerning his mysteries; and then they are taken captive by the devil." Read the grammar. The independent variable is human response -- heed and diligence versus hardening. The dependent variable is how much of the word is received. God does not decide to give some people less. People decide to receive less. Alma does not say "God withholds from the hard-hearted." He says the hard-hearted *receive* the lesser portion. The passive voice is doing the theological work. God offers the full feast. Some people eat; some push the plate away.

This is the load-bearing claim: in the participatory knowing cycle established in Round 1, the human node -- the decide-and-act node -- is what determines trajectory. Every upward spiral and every downward spiral traces back to a human choice to give heed or to harden.

## b. The Opponent's Strongest Case -- Stated Fairly, Then Destroyed

My opponent will argue that God is not merely a constant provider but an active *initiator*. The Spirit *entices* (Mosiah 3:19). Grace *enables*. Light is not simply available to be noticed; it is sent, directed, calibrated. D&C 50:24 says "he that receiveth light, and continueth in God, receiveth more light" -- and the opponent will emphasize the divine side of that transaction: God *gives* more light. The light does not simply accumulate through human effort. It is actively dispensed. And the dispensation is responsive -- God gives *more* to those who receive, suggesting divine calibration, not merely human filtering of a constant signal.

The strongest form of this argument appeals to Mosiah 3:19: "the natural man is an enemy to God... unless he yields to the enticings of the Holy Spirit." The word is *yields*. It implies that the Spirit moves first. The Spirit entices, and the human either yields or resists. On this reading, God is the initiator, and human response is secondary -- real but secondary. The sequence is: God entices, human yields, God entices further. The engine is divine initiative. The human side is merely a gate that opens or closes.

Here is why this gets the causal structure exactly backwards.

"Yields" does not mean "is acted upon." It means "stops resisting." The natural man is *already* in a state of opposition to God -- that is what the verse says. The Spirit's enticing meets resistance by default. The decisive act is the human decision to *stop* resisting. The Spirit's enticing is constant -- it entices every natural man, all the time, everywhere. The variation is not in the enticing. The variation is in the yielding. Some yield. Some do not. The Spirit did not entice Lamoni's father more aggressively than it enticed Laman and Lemuel. It enticed them all. Lamoni's father yielded. Laman and Lemuel did not. The difference was entirely human.

And D&C 50:24 -- "receiveth more light" -- confirms rather than undermines my position. The word *receiveth* is active. You receive. You continue in God. Then more light comes. The human act of receiving and continuing is the condition. The light is the consequence. My opponent wants to emphasize that God *gives* the additional light, and yes, God does. The sun also gives the photons. But the photons fall on every field equally, and the fields that flourish are the ones that were prepared by human hands. The giving is constant. The receiving is variable. And variable determines trajectory.

## c. Why the Grace-Driven View Fails

The grace-driven view -- the claim that God actively calibrates input based on some divine assessment independent of human choice -- fails for a precise structural reason. It makes God the author of the downward spiral.

If God is the one calibrating -- giving more light to the faithful, less to the unfaithful -- then when a person spirals downward into knowing "nothing concerning his mysteries," God is an active participant in that descent. God *chose* to give less. God *decided* to withdraw light. This is not the God of scripture. This is a God who punishes ignorance with more ignorance, who responds to weakness with abandonment, who calibrates *away* from the struggling soul.

The human-response model avoids this entirely. God does not give less to the hard-hearted. The hard-hearted *receive* less because they have closed the aperture through which light enters. God's broadcast does not change. The receiver's tuning changes. A person who hardens their heart is not being punished by a God who dims the signal. They are choosing to turn down the volume on their own receiver. When Alma says they receive "the lesser portion," he is describing a human-caused filtering effect, not a divine rationing decision.

This matters theologically because it preserves what the Round 1 synthesis identified as essential: the veil creates space for genuine participation. If God actively calibrates light levels to individual souls -- turning the dial up for the righteous and down for the wicked -- then God is *controlling the participation environment*. This slides dangerously toward the exact problem Round 1 identified with Satan's plan: external control of the conditions under which agency operates. It is a soft determinism dressed in grace language. If God decides how much light you get, then God decides, in significant measure, what your epistemic situation is, and your "choice" to respond is constrained by a situation God engineered for you specifically. That is not agency. That is a rigged game with a compassionate dealer.

The human-response model preserves genuine agency because the conditions are the same for everyone. The light shines. The Spirit entices. The word is offered. Every soul gets the same broadcast. What differs is what each soul does with it. And *that* -- not some divine calibration algorithm -- is what determines whether you spiral toward the perfect day or toward the chains of hell.

## d. The Deeper Principle at Stake

What is at stake is whether agency is *real* in the strongest possible sense -- whether human beings are genuinely the authors of their spiritual trajectories, or whether they are participants in a system whose outcomes are primarily determined by how God allocates grace.

The Round 1 synthesis established that faith is participatory knowledge of God. The war in heaven was fought over whether this participatory mode of knowing matters. Satan's plan would have compelled the outcome. God's plan required genuine participation. But participation is only genuine if the participant is the decisive variable. If God calibrates the difficulty, adjusts the input, titrates the grace -- if the divine side is doing the active tuning -- then participation is a controlled experiment where the Experimenter adjusts conditions to ensure desired outcomes. That is benevolent manipulation, not genuine agency.

The human-response model takes the war in heaven's logic seriously all the way down. God created the conditions for participation: mortality, the veil, the constant availability of the Spirit. Then God stepped back -- not in indifference, but in *respect for agency* -- and let the human variable do what the human variable does. "It is not meet that I should command in all things... the power is in them" (D&C 58:26-28). This is not a God who calibrates. This is a God who provides and then *watches what you do with it*. The watching is not passive. It is the most profound act of divine love imaginable -- the willingness to let agents be agents, even when they choose the downward spiral, even when they harden and harden until they know nothing.

This is the Father who let a third of His children walk away. He did not calibrate the enticings to prevent it. He did not adjust the input to make rebellion harder. He provided light, and love, and truth, and opportunity -- and then He let them choose. A third chose wrong. If God were the active calibrator my opponent imagines, the war in heaven is inexplicable. Why would a God who actively tunes grace to each individual soul allow a third of those souls to fall permanently? Because He doesn't tune. He provides. We choose. The power is in us.

## e. The Extreme Formulation

Here is the hardest version: every person who has ever fallen away from God did so because of their own choices, fully and without remainder. No one was given insufficient light. No one was enticed too weakly. No one was dealt a hand so poor that their downward spiral was inevitable. The light was there. The Spirit was there. The word was available. They hardened. They refused. They said "we have enough" and from them was taken even that which they had (2 Nephi 28:30) -- taken not by a God who withdrew it, but by the structural consequence of their own refusal.

And every person who has ascended toward the perfect day did so because they gave heed and diligence. They planted the seed. They nourished it with great care and patience. They did not neglect the tree. The harvest was theirs because the labor was theirs. God provided the soil and the sun and the rain. But a thousand farmers stand in the same soil under the same sun in the same rain, and some harvest abundantly while others starve. The difference is the farmer. It has always been the farmer.

## f. The Reasoning Skeleton

1. **God's provision is constant and universal.** The Spirit entices every person (Moroni 7:16). Light is available to all who will receive it (D&C 50:24). The word is offered to all (Alma 12:9). (*Textual premise.*)

2. **The variation in spiritual trajectory is determined by human response.** "According to the heed and diligence which they give" (Alma 12:9). "Unto him that receiveth I will give more" (2 Nephi 28:30). Receiving and heeding are human acts. (*Textual premise -- load-bearing.*)

3. **If God calibrates input independently of human choice, God becomes the author of the downward spiral.** Withdrawing light from the hard-hearted makes God an active agent of their descent. (*Theological premise -- load-bearing.*)

4. **Active divine calibration undermines genuine agency.** If God adjusts the epistemic environment for each soul individually, the participation is a controlled experiment, not genuine agency. This contradicts the Round 1 synthesis's account of the war in heaven. (*Structural premise.*)

5. **Therefore, the participatory cycle is governed by human response.** God provides the constant input. We determine the trajectory by what we do with it. The power is in us, wherein we are agents unto ourselves.

If premise 2 falls -- if someone can show that human response itself is determined by prior divine allocation (that God gives some people *more capacity* to heed, not just more light) -- then my argument collapses into a regress. And if premise 3 falls -- if there is a way for God to actively calibrate without becoming the author of failure -- I need to hear it. But I do not believe either falls. The scriptures place the weight where it belongs: on us.
