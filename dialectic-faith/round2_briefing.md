# Round 2 Context Briefing: The Mechanics of the Participatory Cycle

## Prior Synthesis (Round 1)

Faith is participatory knowledge of God — a relational knowing constituted by engagement, where knowing and acting are the same motion. The cycle is "receive, decide, act, receive again" with the will active at every turn. The war in heaven was a dispute about whether this participatory mode of knowing matters. Satan's plan would have produced propositional compliance without participation. God's plan requires the veil/gap because participatory knowing can only exist through genuine engagement under uncertainty.

## The Round 2 Tension

The Round 1 synthesis named the cycle but didn't map its mechanics. Two questions emerge simultaneously:

1. **How does the cycle vary based on our responses?** What happens at each node? What are the real failure modes (insincere engagement vs. unrealistic expectations vs. failure to recognize feedback)?
2. **Where is God's side of the cycle?** The Round 1 synthesis emphasized human participation. But is God a passive provider of input, or an active, responsive participant who calibrates to where we are?

These combine into one question: **Is the participatory cycle primarily human-driven (we determine the outcome by our responses, God provides consistent input) or primarily grace-driven (God actively calibrates, and the cycle works because of divine responsiveness)?**

## User's Position from Interview

The user rejected the idea that "sincere participation produces silence" as a real failure mode. The promise is we'll never be left alone forever. The real problems people have are:
- (a) Insincere engagement — going through motions without genuine orientation of will
- (b) Unrealistic expectations — looking for the wrong kind of feedback

This implies the user sees the human response side as the primary variable. But the grace question remains: is God just providing constant input that we filter through our choices, or is God actively adapting?

## Key Scriptural Evidence

### Human-Response Side

**Alma 12:9-11:** God gives "according to the heed and diligence which they give unto him." Those who harden hearts receive lesser portion of the word. Those who don't harden receive greater portion "until it is given unto him to know the mysteries of God until he know them in full." Those who harden continually receive lesser and lesser "until they know nothing concerning his mysteries; and then they are taken captive by the devil." The human response (heed/diligence vs. hardening) determines the trajectory.

**2 Nephi 28:30:** "Unto him that receiveth I will give more; and from them that shall say, We have enough, from them shall be taken away even that which they have." Receiving vs. refusing to receive is the human variable. Negative feedback loop: stop participating, lose what you had.

**D&C 58:26-28:** "The power is in them, wherein they are agents unto themselves." Human agency as the engine. Not commanded in all things. Anxiously engaged of their own free will.

**Alma 32:28-43:** The entire seed experiment places responsibility on the planter. You plant, you nourish, you are patient, you are diligent. If the seed doesn't grow, Alma doesn't blame God — he says "it is not a good seed" or you "neglect the tree." Human action at every stage.

**Alma 32:39:** "If ye neglect the tree, and take no thought for its nourishment, behold it will not get any root; and when the heat of the sun cometh and scorcheth it, because it hath no root it withers away, and ye pluck it up and cast it out." Failure is attributed to neglect, not to God withholding.

### Grace/Divine-Calibration Side

**D&C 50:24:** "He that receiveth light, and continueth in God, receiveth more light; and that light groweth brighter and brighter until the perfect day." God is the source of the light. The cycle requires God's active provision of increasing light.

**Mosiah 3:19:** "Unless he yields to the enticings of the Holy Spirit." The Spirit *entices*. God's side is active — inviting, drawing, initiating. The human side is "yielding" — not generating but accepting, opening, not resisting.

**Moroni 7:16:** "The Spirit of Christ is given to every man, that he may know good from evil." Universal baseline input from God. Every person starts with divine light, not from zero.

**2 Nephi 25:23:** "By grace that we are saved, after all we can do." Grace as the ultimate enabling condition. Human effort is necessary but not sufficient.

**Ether 12:27:** "My grace is sufficient for all men that humble themselves before me." Grace is sufficient — but conditional on humility (a human response).

**D&C 93:20:** "If you keep my commandments you shall receive of his fulness, and be glorified in me as I am in the Father." God promises a specific response to human faithfulness.

### The Responsive Cycle in Scripture

Alma 12:9-11 and D&C 50:24 together reveal a cycle that is BOTH human-responsive AND divinely calibrated:
- God provides light/word (divine input)
- Human gives heed and diligence OR hardens heart (human response)
- God provides MORE or LESS based on the human response (divine calibration)
- The cycle either spirals upward (more light → more response → more light → "perfect day") or downward (hardening → less → more hardening → "know nothing" → "chains of hell")

## Degenerate Framings to Avoid

- "Faith vs. works" / "grace vs. works" — this is a different debate. Both sides here affirm that human action and divine grace are both real.
- "God does everything / God does nothing" — neither position is this extreme. The question is about the *structure* of the interaction.
- "Synergism" as a generic label — too vague. The question is specifically about which side of the cycle is *primary* or *load-bearing*.

## Ontological Question for Round 2

In the participatory knowing cycle, what is the relationship between human response and divine calibration? Is grace the *initiator* that we respond to, or is human agency the *engine* that God responds to? Or is the question itself malformed — is there a way the cycle works where neither side is "primary"?
