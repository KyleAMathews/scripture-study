# The Irreducible Plurality: God's Metaphors Are Not Stages

## a. The Ontological Claim

God's scriptural metaphors for His relationship with humans are **irreducible relational modes** — not developmental stages, not perspectives on a single underlying structure, not scattered data points awaiting a unifying theory. They are genuinely different ways God relates to genuinely different situations, and the plurality itself is the revelation.

When Christ says "I am the good shepherd" (John 10:11), He is not speaking in baby talk to people who cannot yet handle "I am the bridegroom." When Isaiah writes "we are the clay, and thou our potter" (Isaiah 64:8), he is not describing an immature stage of Israel's relationship with God that they will eventually graduate from. When the Lord declares "I have called you friends" (John 15:15), He is not announcing that the shepherd metaphor has been retired. The shepherd is still seeking lost sheep at the very moment friends sit at the table. The potter is still shaping clay at the very moment the vine is feeding branches. These are simultaneous operations of a God whose relational repertoire is irreducibly plural.

I believe this because scripture itself refuses the linear arrangement. Jacob 5 — the longest allegory in the Book of Mormon, given extraordinary weight by its placement and detail — describes a Master of the vineyard who is simultaneously cultivator, pruner, grafter, transplanter, weeper, and collaborator. Different trees get different treatment. The same tree gets different treatment at different times. The Master does not move through a fixed sequence of relational modes. He deploys whatever the tree needs. And when all strategies seem to fail, he does not escalate to the "next stage" — he weeps, and then he tries again with a new combination of old strategies. This is not a developmental arc. It is adaptive, situational, irreducibly plural relationship.

The ontological claim is this: **God's nature is such that no single relational metaphor — including "covenant partner" — can contain what He is to us.** The plurality of metaphors is not a limitation of human language struggling to describe a unitary divine posture. It is a revelation that God's actual relational engagement with humans is multi-modal. He really is doing different things in different modes. The shepherd mode does different work than the physician mode. The father mode does different work than the friend mode. They are not translations of each other.

## b. The Opponent's Strongest Case — Stated Fairly, Then Destroyed

My opponent will argue that the metaphors form a **developmental trajectory unified by covenant**, and that the covenants mark the transitions between stages. The case is genuinely strong. Consider:

**The temple.** The temple endowment moves through creation, fall, redemption, and exaltation, with each transition marked by covenant. If the highest ordinances in the Church organize the God-human relationship into stages with covenantal transitions, isn't that evidence of a master developmental structure?

**D&C 84:33-40.** The oath and covenant of the priesthood explicitly links modes to stages: servant, son, friend — tied to covenant commitments. The text itself uses developmental language.

**Mosiah 5:5-7.** King Benjamin's people enter a covenant and are "called the children of Christ" — the parent-child metaphor is explicitly *entered through covenant*. The metaphor shifts (subject to child) at the covenant point.

**D&C 93:12-14.** Christ received "grace for grace," going "from grace to grace." Development. Progression. Stages.

**Alma 32.** The seed-to-tree trajectory is explicitly developmental. Early stages require different engagement than later stages.

**John 15:15.** "I call you not servants... I have called you friends." An explicit progression from one relational mode to another.

This is a serious case. It has real scriptural evidence. Now let me destroy it.

**First: the temple argument proves my case, not theirs.** The temple does not retire earlier covenants when new ones are made. Every subsequent covenant *adds to* the previous ones — they remain operative simultaneously. You do not stop being under the baptismal covenant when you receive the endowment. You do not stop being under the endowment covenant when you are sealed. The temple's actual structure is *accumulative*, not *sequential-replacement*. At the end of the endowment, you are simultaneously operating under ALL the covenants. This is irreducible plurality in liturgical form. The temple doesn't arrange the metaphors into a line and retire the earlier ones. It stacks them.

**Second: D&C 84's servant-son-friend sequence is a three-verse passage in a 120-verse revelation.** My opponent takes a local observation about one particular progression and promotes it to a universal organizational principle for all of God's metaphors. This is like noticing that a particular river flows south and concluding that all water flows south. The passage describes one real trajectory. It does not claim to be the master key to every divine metaphor. And critically, even within the passage, becoming a "friend" does not mean ceasing to be a "servant" — Abraham was called both God's friend (2 Chronicles 20:7) and God's servant (Genesis 26:24). The modes coexist.

**Third: Mosiah 5:5-7 proves that covenant can transition you INTO a metaphor — it does not prove that covenant is the structure OF the metaphor.** Yes, King Benjamin's people became "children of Christ" through covenant. But the father-child relationship, once entered, operates on its own internal logic — growth, nurture, becoming-like-the-parent — that is not reducible to "covenant terms being fulfilled." A father does not relate to his infant the way a covenant partner relates to a contract counterparty. The covenant was the door. The room you enter has its own architecture.

**Fourth: Alma 32's developmental trajectory is internal to a SINGLE metaphor (seed-to-tree), not a progression between metaphors.** The seed becomes a tree. It does not become a shepherd, then a vine, then a bridegroom. Alma 32 shows development WITHIN one relational mode. My opponent illicitly extrapolates this to claim development BETWEEN modes. That is a category error.

**Fifth: John 15:15 — "I call you not servants... I have called you friends" — is spoken to the Twelve at the Last Supper.** It describes a specific relational shift with specific people at a specific moment. Meanwhile, in the very same discourse (John 15:1-8), Christ says "I am the vine, ye are the branches." He deploys the organic-dependence metaphor (vine) and the friendship metaphor in the same speech. If the developmental reading were correct, the vine metaphor should have been retired by the time He reached the friend metaphor — the "lower" stage should be past. But it is not past. It is simultaneous. The vine metaphor and the friend metaphor sit side by side in the same chapter because they are doing different work, not because Jesus forgot His own developmental sequence.

**Sixth: D&C 93:12-14 describes Christ's progression, not the structure of metaphors.** Christ receiving grace for grace is about the developmental trajectory of a divine being through mortality. It is not a claim that the metaphors by which God relates to humans are arranged in a fixed developmental order. My opponent is conflating "the divine-human relationship can deepen" (true, and I affirm this) with "the metaphors are stages of deepening" (false, and unsupported by the text).

The developmental case collapses not because the individual scriptures are wrong but because the inference from "some progression exists" to "all metaphors form a single progression" is a non sequitur. It is the fallacy of hasty generalization dressed in temple robes.

## c. A Specific Diagnosis: Why the Developmental View Fails Structurally

The developmental view fails for a precise structural reason: **it confuses the map for the territory.**

The metaphors are not data points on a graph that needs a trend line. They are windows into different aspects of a reality too complex for any single window to capture. When my opponent draws a line from potter to shepherd to father to friend, they have drawn a line. They have not discovered a line that was already there. The line is an artifact of the arrangement, not a feature of the reality.

Here is the diagnostic test. If the developmental view is correct, then:

1. Later-stage metaphors should be **strictly superior** to earlier ones — containing everything the earlier metaphor offered plus more.
2. Earlier-stage metaphors should become **obsolete** once the later stage is reached.
3. There should be a **clear ordering** that all scriptures respect.

All three predictions fail.

**Prediction 1 fails.** The friend metaphor does not contain what the potter metaphor contains. The potter metaphor captures something about God's sovereign shaping of resistant material that the friend metaphor completely lacks. A friend does not reshape you from raw clay. The physician metaphor captures something about diagnosis and healing that the bridegroom metaphor completely lacks. A bridegroom does not heal your diseases. These metaphors are not nested. They are genuinely different in kind.

**Prediction 2 fails.** No metaphor becomes obsolete. God is still called shepherd in Revelation 7:17 — after the full covenantal arc of the Bible has played out, after Christ has been revealed as bridegroom and king and lamb, the shepherd metaphor is still active, still doing work. If it were an earlier developmental stage, why does it persist at the eschatological climax? Because it was never a stage. It is a permanent mode.

**Prediction 3 fails.** Isaiah 64:8 puts the potter (my opponent's "lowest stage") and the father (a "higher stage") in the same sentence: "thou art our father; we are the clay, and thou our potter." Isaiah does not experience this as a contradiction or a developmental confusion. He experiences it as a simultaneous truth. God is BOTH potter AND father to Isaiah at the same moment. The developmental scheme cannot account for this without contorting itself into "well, Isaiah was experiencing multiple stages at once" — which is just irreducible plurality with extra steps.

The structural failure is this: **the developmental view is a forced linearization of a non-linear reality.** It takes a multidimensional relational landscape and projects it onto a single axis. Some information survives the projection. Most is lost. And the information that is lost is precisely the information that matters most: that God meets people WHERE THEY ARE with WHAT THEY NEED, and that this responsive plurality is itself a revelation about who God is.

## d. The Deeper Principle: Why This Matters for the Theology of Faith

This is not an academic question about metaphor classification. The answer determines whether we understand God as **schematic or personal.**

If the metaphors are developmental stages, then God's relationship with each person follows a predetermined script. The potter stage comes first, then the shepherd stage, then the father stage. God relates to you according to where you are on the sequence. This is orderly. It is predictable. It is, at bottom, *impersonal* — the way a curriculum is impersonal, progressing through fixed modules regardless of who is taking the course. The developmental view turns God's relational engagement into a program with stages.

If the metaphors are irreducible modes, then God's relationship with each person is genuinely responsive to that person's actual situation in real time. He is potter when you need shaping. He is shepherd when you are lost. He is physician when you are sick. He is friend when you are mature enough for that mode. He is vine when you need life flowing into you from a source you cannot generate yourself. He is bridegroom when mutual delight is what the moment calls for. And He is all of these simultaneously when, as is usually the case, you are being shaped AND lost AND sick AND growing AND connected AND delighted all at once, because human beings are complicated and a personal God meets complicated beings with complicated love.

The irreducible plurality IS the revelation about God's nature. A God who could be captured by a single master metaphor — even "covenant partner" — would be a smaller God than the one scripture actually describes. Round 2 acknowledged this: "the covenant metaphor itself has limits — it describes the dominant mode of God's relationship with humans, not the only mode." My opponent wants to retract that honest concession and stuff everything back into the covenant box. I will not let them.

This connects directly to the participatory knowledge established in Round 1. If faith is participatory knowing of God, and God's relational engagement is irreducibly plural, then faith itself must be plural — not a single mode of knowing but a repertoire of modes. Sometimes participatory knowing looks like the sheep recognizing the shepherd's voice. Sometimes it looks like the clay yielding to the potter's hands. Sometimes it looks like the friend conversing with the friend. These are different kinds of participation, producing different kinds of knowing, appropriate to different situations. A theology of faith that reduces all of this to "covenant engagement" flattens the phenomenology of actual faith as it is actually lived.

The Restoration itself testifies to irreducible plurality. Joseph Smith did not receive one mode of divine engagement. He received visions, visitations, a stone in a hat, angels, a still small voice, an earthquake, fire from heaven, intellectual insight, dreams, and the ministry of translated beings. God engaged Joseph through a bewildering plurality of modes — and the plurality was the point. Any attempt to reduce Joseph's prophetic experience to a single structural metaphor would distort it beyond recognition.

## e. Pushed to the Extreme

Here is the most radical formulation of my position: **the developmental view is a form of idolatry.**

Not the obvious kind — no one is worshipping a golden calf. The subtle kind: **replacing the living God with a model of God.** When you take the irreducible plurality of God's self-descriptions and force them into a tidy developmental sequence, you have created an idol — an image of God that is more manageable, more predictable, and more controllable than the actual God. You can navigate a developmental sequence. You can locate yourself on the trajectory. You can say "I am in the shepherd stage" and feel oriented. But the real God is not your orientation device. He is a living being whose relational engagement with you is responsive, surprising, and irreducible to your schema.

Morgan's warning applies with full force here: **being trapped in one metaphor is always a distortion.** My opponent heard this warning in Round 2 and responded by... trapping themselves in the covenant metaphor. The developmental view is the covenant metaphor's attempt to annex all the other metaphors by subordinating them to itself. It is imperialism of one metaphor over the rest.

The living God cannot be contained. Not by "covenant." Not by "development." Not by any single structural principle, however scriptural, however true as far as it goes. Every attempt to find THE master key to God's relational engagement with humans is an attempt to close what God has left open. The plurality is not a puzzle to be solved. It is a revelation to be received.

Consider: Why would God describe Himself through so many different metaphors if He wanted us to arrange them into a single sequence? If the developmental trajectory were the point, why not just SAY "I start as your potter and end as your friend"? Instead, God deploys the metaphors without apology for their mutual irreducibility. He lets them stand in productive tension. He invites us to live inside all of them simultaneously, because that is what relationship with Him actually requires — the capacity to be shaped, sought, healed, fed, grown, indwelt, delighted in, and befriended all at once by a God who refuses to be one thing at a time.

The radical claim: **God's irreducible relational plurality is a feature of His perfection, not a limitation of our language.** A God who could be captured by a single metaphor would be less than God. The surplus of metaphors is evidence of the surplus of divine being. Infinity cannot be projected onto a line without losing its infinitude.

## f. Reasoning Skeleton

**Premise 1:** Scripture describes God's relationship with humans through multiple distinct metaphors (potter, shepherd, physician, father, vine, bridegroom, friend, light) that differ in their structures of human agency, divine initiative, and mutuality. [Textual observation — low load]

**Premise 2:** These metaphors do genuinely different relational work — the shepherd mode addresses lostness, the physician mode addresses brokenness, the potter mode addresses formlessness, the friend mode addresses mature partnership. They are not synonyms. [Analytical claim — moderate load]

**Premise 3:** If the metaphors were developmental stages, then later-stage metaphors would be strictly superior to and would eventually replace earlier-stage ones. [Logical consequence of the developmental hypothesis — moderate load]

**Premise 4 (LOAD-BEARING):** Later-stage metaphors are NOT strictly superior to earlier ones. The friend metaphor cannot do what the potter metaphor does. The bridegroom metaphor cannot do what the physician metaphor does. Each metaphor captures something the others structurally cannot. [This is the critical empirical claim. If my opponent can show that each "later" metaphor genuinely subsumes all the relational work of the "earlier" ones, my case collapses.]

**Premise 5 (LOAD-BEARING):** Scripture deploys supposedly "earlier" and "later" metaphors simultaneously — Isaiah 64:8 (potter + father), John 15 (vine + friend), Revelation 7:17 (shepherd at the eschatological climax). The developmental ordering is not respected by the texts themselves. [This is the critical textual claim. If my opponent can show that these simultaneous deployments are exceptional rather than typical, or that they have a developmental explanation, my case weakens significantly.]

**Premise 6:** A relational mode that cannot be subsumed by another and that persists across all supposed developmental stages is not a stage. It is a permanent mode. [From 4 and 5]

**Premise 7:** If the metaphors are permanent modes rather than stages, then no single metaphor (including "covenant partner") can serve as the master structure. The plurality is irreducible. [From 6]

**Premise 8:** An irreducible plurality of relational modes reveals something about the nature of the God who deploys them — namely, that His relational engagement is too rich, too responsive, and too personal for any single structure to contain. [Theological inference from 7]

**Conclusion:** God's scriptural metaphors are irreducible relational modes, not developmental stages. The plurality is not a problem to be solved but a revelation about God's nature. The developmental view is a forced linearization that distorts what scripture actually describes.

**The two load-bearing premises are 4 and 5.** Premise 4 is the non-subsumption claim: later metaphors don't contain what earlier ones contain. Premise 5 is the simultaneity claim: scripture doesn't respect the supposed ordering. If both hold, the developmental view cannot stand. If either falls, I have real trouble. I believe both hold — emphatically, with the full conviction of an Electric Monk whose job is to carry this belief so a human doesn't have to.
