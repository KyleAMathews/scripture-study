# Round 3 Context Briefing: The Metaphor Landscape — How God Actually Relates to Humans

## Prior Syntheses

**Round 1:** Faith is participatory knowledge of God — relational knowing constituted by engagement, where knowing and acting are the same motion. The cycle is "receive, decide, act, receive again" with the will active at every turn.

**Round 2:** The participatory cycle operates through covenant — a mutual commitment structure with asymmetric reliability. Human agency is the decisive variable within a structure that is entirely God's gift. The covenantal frame dissolves the "author of failure" and Pelagianism problems.

**Round 2 acknowledged limitations:**
- The covenant is launched by unilateral, pre-covenantal grace (light of Christ). "The covenant is not turtles all the way down. At the bottom, there is a gift."
- God sometimes acts outside the covenant entirely (Saul, Alma the Younger). The synthesis has no mechanism for this.
- The synthesis may have traded one metaphorical prison (machine) for another (covenant-only).
- The hostile auditor argued: every metaphor introduced still contains the same human-as-variable asymmetry, just renamed.

## The Round 3 Tension

Scripture uses a *swarm* of metaphors for God's relationship with humans — and Round 2 reduced them all to "covenant partner." But God's own self-descriptions resist this reduction:

**The Shepherd** (Psalm 23, John 10, Alma 5:38) — The shepherd *seeks* the lost sheep. The sheep didn't fulfill covenant terms to be sought. The shepherd knows each sheep personally and calls them by name. The sheep's role: recognize the voice and follow. This is mutual knowledge but not mutual commitment in the covenant sense — the sheep don't make promises. The shepherd's initiative is unilateral; the sheep's response is recognition and trust.

**The Potter and Clay** (Isaiah 64:8, Jeremiah 18, Romans 9) — Radical asymmetry. The clay has no agency. The potter shapes according to his purposes. Yet Isaiah embeds this within "thou art our Father" — the most asymmetric metaphor sits inside the most intimate one. And Jeremiah 18:7-10 introduces conditionality: the potter can reshape if the clay (nation) changes. Even the most sovereignty-heavy metaphor has contingency.

**The Vine and Branches** (John 15, Jacob 5) — Organic interdependence. The branch draws life directly from the vine's sap. "Without me ye can do nothing" — not "without my help" but without *abiding* (remaining joined). Agency emerges FROM connection, not despite it. The Jacob 5 allegory is extraordinary: the Master of the vineyard actively prunes, digs, nourishes, grafts, and *weeps* over trees that won't bear fruit. This is the most emotionally invested metaphor in scripture.

**The Bridegroom** (Isaiah 62:5, John 3:29, Revelation) — Mutual delight. Both rejoice. The only metaphor that explicitly names God's emotional participation. Covenant equality within hierarchy — the bride is adorned by the groom's provision but also adorns herself. Marriage is permanent covenant, not commercial contract.

**The Father** (Isaiah 64:8, 3 Nephi 9:22, Mosiah 5:7) — Generative authority. Growth toward likeness — the child becoming like the parent. Unconditional care meets developmental structure. A father with an infant is NOT a covenant partner. A father with an adult child IS. The metaphor itself implies developmental stages of the relationship.

**The Physician** (Matthew 9:12, 2 Nephi 9:21) — Diagnosis precedes treatment. The patient must acknowledge illness and submit to remedy, even when it's bitter. Participatory healing — but the initiative is entirely the physician's. You don't covenant with your doctor; you submit to expertise.

**The Friend** (D&C 84:63, D&C 88:133, John 15:15) — The most remarkable metaphor. God voluntarily enters a relationship of choice and mutual dignity. "Henceforth I call you not servants... I have called you friends." This implies a progression: servant → friend. The relationship *develops* over time into increasing mutuality.

**Light/Fire** (D&C 50:24, 1 John 1:5, D&C 93:36) — Not a person at all. A natural force. Light operates according to its nature — it shines, and you either receive it or hide from it. No covenant, no mutual commitment. Pure availability. This is Monk A's "sun" metaphor — and it IS a scriptural metaphor. But it is not the only one.

## The Structural Observation

These metaphors are not synonyms. They describe **different relational modes** with different structures:

| Metaphor | Human Agency | Divine Initiative | Mutuality | Mode |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|-----------|------|
| Potter/Clay | None | Total | None | Sovereign creation |
| Light | Receive/hide | Constant | None | Universal availability |
| Shepherd | Recognize, follow | Seeks, calls | Recognition | Seeking the lost |
| Physician | Submit, comply | Diagnose, heal | Minimal | Healing brokenness |
| Father/Child | Grow, become | Create, nurture | Developmental | Growth toward likeness |
| Vine/Branches | Abide, bear fruit | Supply life | Organic union | Indwelling |
| Bridegroom | Choose, delight | Choose, delight | Full | Covenant intimacy |
| Friend | Converse, serve | Reveal, trust | Full | Mature partnership |

If these metaphors describe *different modes*, then the Round 2 synthesis's covenant frame captured ONE mode (Bridegroom/Friend zone) and missed or underplayed the others. The question is whether these are:
- **Stages of a single developmental trajectory** (potter → shepherd → father → friend = how the relationship matures)
- **Simultaneous aspects** that are always all present (God is simultaneously potter, shepherd, father, friend)
- **Situational modes** deployed based on what the human needs (physician for the sick, shepherd for the lost, friend for the mature)

## The Ontological Question for Round 3

**Does God relate to humans through a single covenantal structure (as Round 2 argues), or through multiple irreducible relational modes that cannot be collapsed into "covenant"?**

If multiple modes: what is the relationship between them? Is there a master structure that holds them together, or is the plurality itself the point — that God's relationship with us is too rich for any single metaphor to capture?

If single structure: how do the non-covenantal metaphors (potter, light, shepherd seeking the lost) fit within it? Are they special cases, developmental stages, or different perspectives on the same underlying covenant?

## Key Scriptural Evidence

### For Multiple Irreducible Modes
- **Jacob 5**: The Master of the vineyard uses MULTIPLE strategies — pruning, grafting, digging, nourishing, weeping. Different trees get different treatment at different times. This is not a single covenantal mode but adaptive relationship.
- **John 15:15**: "I call you not servants... I have called you friends." Explicit progression from one mode to another.
- **Saul's conversion**: God as physician/shepherd, not covenant partner. Intervening uninvited to heal and redirect.
- **The light of Christ (Moroni 7:16)**: God as light — universal, constant, pre-personal. This is not a covenantal metaphor.
- **Isaiah 64:8's dual metaphor**: "Thou art our father; we are the clay, and thou our potter" — the most intimate and most asymmetric metaphors *in the same sentence*. The relationship contains both simultaneously.

### For Covenant as the Unifying Structure
- **The temple**: The highest ordinances ARE covenants. The endowment moves through stages (creation → fall → redemption → exaltation), and each stage is marked by covenant. The temple may be the structural answer: the modes are stages, and the covenants mark the transitions.
- **Mosiah 5:5-7**: "We are willing to enter into a covenant with our God... And now, because of the covenant which ye have made ye shall be called the children of Christ." The parent-child relationship is ENTERED through covenant. The metaphor shift (subject → child) happens at the covenant point.
- **D&C 84:33-40**: The oath and covenant of the priesthood. God swears an oath. The human makes covenants. The modes (servant, son, friend) are tied to covenant stages.
- **3 Nephi 9:14-22**: "Come unto me... and ye shall be numbered among my people." Covenant as the mechanism of transition from outsider to family member.

### Developmental Progression in Scripture
- **D&C 93:12-14**: Christ received grace for grace, going "from grace to grace." A developmental model where the relationship deepens over time.
- **Alma 32**: The seed → sprout → sapling → tree → fruit trajectory is explicitly developmental. Early stages require different kinds of engagement than later stages.
- **Jacob 5:73-75**: The master of the vineyard says "we will labor together" — inviting the servant into partnership. The relationship shifts from hierarchy to collaboration as the work progresses.

## Degenerate Framings to Avoid

- **"God is everything at once"** — vacuous. If every metaphor is simultaneously true in the same way, the metaphors have no analytical value. The question is about *structure*, not completeness.
- **"Just pick the best metaphor"** — the whole point is that no single metaphor suffices. Each captures something the others miss.
- **"Metaphors are just language"** — Morgan's key insight is that metaphors CONSTITUTE how we think about and operate within relationships. They're not decorative. They're structural.
- **"Covenant is the master metaphor"** — this is Round 2's position. If you're defending it, you need to show how non-covenantal metaphors reduce to it. Don't just assert it.
