# Phase 6: Sublation — Faith as Participatory Knowledge of God

## The Synthesis

**Faith is participatory knowledge of God.**

Not knowledge *about* God (propositional, storable, accumulated like facts) and not action *toward* God (contentless, directionless, mere willingness). Faith is a mode of knowing that is constituted by the relationship between the knower and God — a knowing that *cannot exist except through participation.*

You do not know a person by reading their biography. You know a person by interacting with them — by making commitments, being surprised, adjusting your model, making new commitments based on the adjusted model, being surprised again. Each interaction deepens your knowledge, but the knowledge *is* the interaction. Separate the knowledge from the relationship and you have facts. Separate the relationship from the knowledge and you have sentiment. Neither is knowing-a-person.

Faith works the same way. Alma 32 is not an epistemic experiment followed by action, or an action that happens to produce knowledge. It is a description of *participatory knowing*: you plant (act), you feel the swelling (experience), your understanding is enlightened (know), you nourish with diligence (act), the tree grows (experience), and at each point *the knowing and the doing are the same motion*. Alma never separates them. "Your knowledge is perfect in that thing" — yes, because you participated in it. "Neither must ye lay aside your faith" — yes, because there is more participation ahead.

## The War in Heaven as Structural Key

The war in heaven was fundamentally a dispute about whether participatory knowledge matters.

Satan's plan: remove agency, compel obedience, eliminate the gap, guarantee the outcome. On this plan, everyone returns to God's presence. The "knowledge" of God is propositional — you know the facts, you follow the rules, you arrive at the destination. The veil is unnecessary because the gap is unnecessary because participation is unnecessary. Everyone "knows" God in the sense that they have correct information about God and comply with his commands.

God's plan: preserve agency, institute the veil, create the space for genuine engagement under uncertainty. On this plan, the knowing of God is participatory — you plant seeds, you step into darkness, you act from within a relationship you can't fully see. The veil isn't a regrettable cost of mortality. It's the structural feature that makes participatory knowing possible. Without it, there is no space for participation, and without participation, there is no faith-knowledge.

This reframes the stakes entirely. The foundation monk is right that knowledge of God matters — but Satan also had "knowledge of God" in the propositional sense. He knew God's character, attributes, and plan better than any mortal ever has. He had all three Lectures on Faith prerequisites in their fullest form. And he rebelled. His knowledge was propositional, not participatory. He knew *about* God without knowing God *through engagement*.

### Why Satan's Participation Failed: The Orientation of Will

An objection: Satan participated in pre-mortal existence alongside God. If participatory knowledge is the key, why didn't his participation produce it? Because participation is not passive exposure. It is a cycle: you receive input (light, experience, revelation) and then you *decide* how to act on it. The will is active at every turn. Satan received the same input as every other pre-mortal intelligence. He decided differently — "give me thine honor" (Moses 4:1). He oriented his participation toward his own glory rather than toward knowing God as God is.

Participatory knowledge is not just engagement — it is engagement with the will oriented toward the thing being known. Desire matters. "Even if ye can no more than desire to believe, let this desire work in you" (Alma 32:27). The desire isn't a pre-faith feeling. It is the orientation of will that opens you to participatory knowing. Without it, you can participate in God's presence for eons and still only accumulate propositional knowledge — facts about God that you use for your own purposes rather than relational knowledge that transforms you.

Alma 32 maps this cycle precisely: you plant the seed (act), you feel the swelling (receive input), then you decide whether to nourish or neglect (will). The nourishing is the will acting on the input. Each cycle deepens the knowing — but only if the will keeps choosing to engage. Faith-as-participatory-knowledge is not "be near God." It is "receive, decide, act, receive again" — with the will active and oriented at every turn.

### The Two Plans, Restated

The leap monk is right that action under uncertainty is essential — but the action isn't valuable because darkness is formative in itself. It's valuable because *participation is the mode of knowing that produces the kind of beings God wants*. D&C 58's "anxiously engaged in a good cause, of their own free will" describes someone who has internalized God's character through participation — not someone who memorized the rules (Satan's plan) and not someone who leaps blindly (chaos). They know God well enough to act in God's character without being commanded, because their knowing IS their acting.

## Contested Scriptures, Reframed

**Ether 3:19** (brother of Jared's faith ending): His participatory knowledge of God on *that specific dimension* became so complete that the veil couldn't hold. He had participated so deeply in knowing God's nature that the barrier dissolved on that point. But participatory knowledge of one dimension doesn't cover all dimensions. He still had to live, lead, and die — still participating, still at edges the veil hadn't yet dissolved on.

**Abraham on Moriah**: Abraham knew God the way you know a person you've spent 25 years with. His knowledge was relational, not propositional. When the command came, it contradicted his propositional expectations — but his *relational* knowledge of God (this is a being who keeps promises, who is not capricious, who has reasons) enabled him to act into the contradiction. It was genuinely hard — relational knowledge doesn't eliminate uncertainty the way propositional knowledge does. You can know a person deeply and still be surprised. The trial was real. But it was not a contentless leap — it was the deepest expression of participatory knowing, where you act *from within* the relationship even when the relationship demands something you can't yet understand. The trial drew out Abraham's innermost desires — not his theological propositions, but the quality of his participatory relationship with God.

**The brother of Jared's stones**: He knew God well enough to *collaborate*. "What will ye that I should do?" God asks. The brother of Jared doesn't receive instructions. He invents a solution based on his participatory knowledge of what God can do, and brings it as an offering. This is neither deduction (foundation) nor random leap. It's creative initiative within a relationship — the hallmark of participatory knowing.

**D&C 58:26-28**: "Anxiously engaged in a good cause, of their own free will." This is the behavior of someone with participatory knowledge. They know God well enough to act in God's character without being commanded. Not because they've memorized God's rules (propositional), and not because they're leaping blindly (contentless action), but because their participation in the relationship has given them an *orientation* — a sense of what God would do, how God would act, what God values. They are, in a real sense, *knowing God by acting like God*.

**"Eye of faith" (Ether 12:19)**: Those who "beheld with an eye of faith" before physically seeing — this is the perceptual organ of participatory knowing. They *saw* something real through their engagement, before the physical evidence arrived.

**Alma 32:27 — desire to believe**: Desire is the first form of participatory orientation toward God. It is not yet knowledge, but it is the beginning of engagement. "Let this desire work in you" — Alma is describing the very first motion of participatory knowing, the initial reaching-out that begins the relationship. This is why even a mustard seed of desire counts: it's the first participation.

## Why the Veil is Both Purposive AND God's Posture is Disclosure

Participatory knowledge *requires* participation. If God revealed everything propositionally, there would be nothing to participate in — it would be Satan's plan by another route, an information download that produces compliance rather than relationship. The veil ensures that knowing God requires *doing something* — planting, nourishing, stepping into darkness, holding out stones, walking up mountains. God discloses progressively because each disclosure creates new territory for participatory engagement. The darkness isn't the cost of mortality (contra Monk A) or the permanent formative instrument (contra Monk B, who's closer). The darkness is the *space in which participation happens*. Without it, there is no participation, and without participation, there is no faith-knowledge.

## The Faith-Credulity Distinction, Resolved

Participatory knowledge is self-correcting in a way that both propositional knowledge and contentless leaps are not. When you participate in knowing God — when you plant the seed and nourish it — you get feedback. "It beginneth to enlarge my soul; it beginneth to enlighten my understanding; it beginneth to be delicious to me" (Alma 32:28). Credulity doesn't produce this. A false seed, planted and nourished, does not swell. Participatory knowledge has built-in verification that neither propositional knowledge (which can be wrong but feel certain) nor contentless leaps (which have no feedback mechanism) possess.

This also explains why "the elect hear my voice and know who I am" — recognition is a feature of participatory knowing. You recognize God's voice because you've been participating in the relationship. It's not propositional recall ("I remember these facts about God"). It's relational recognition ("I know this person").

## Abduction Test

Does this synthesis make the original contradiction *predictable*? If someone heard "faith is participatory knowledge of God" first, would they predict both monks' positions?

**Yes.** They would predict the foundation monk because participatory knowledge IS knowledge — it's real, accumulating, and knowledge of God's character matters enormously. The Lectures on Faith are describing genuine prerequisites for participatory engagement. They would predict the leap monk because participatory knowledge requires PARTICIPATION — it can't exist in the abstract, it must be exercised at the edge where new participation is required. Each side correctly perceives one essential feature of participatory knowledge and mistakes it for the whole.

**Abduction type:** Conditional-creative (b). Introduces a new relationship between known concepts (knowledge, action, participation, relationship) rather than a genuinely novel concept. A stronger synthesis might introduce a new structural principle — but this one genuinely resolves the contradiction rather than splitting the difference.

## Model Update

- **Before:** Faith is either accumulated knowledge of God (foundation) or willingness to act beyond what you know (leap), and the two are in tension.
- **After:** Faith is participatory knowledge of God — a relational knowing constituted by engagement, where knowing and acting are the same motion. Knowledge of God's character matters enormously (it's real knowledge, not metaphor) and action at the edge matters enormously (participation is how the knowledge exists) — but they are not two separate things in sequence or tension. They are two descriptions of one thing.
- **Because:** The contradiction between the monks revealed that both were trapped in a shared assumption — that knowing and acting are separate categories that need to be related. The war in heaven illuminates this: Satan's plan would have produced propositional knowledge without participation. God's plan requires the gap precisely because participatory knowing is the kind of knowing that produces beings who can be "anxiously engaged in a good cause, of their own free will."

## "He Cannot Save Them in Their Sins" (Alma 11:37)

The word is *cannot*, not *will not*. This is a structural claim, not a policy preference. The participatory knowledge framework explains why.

If the whole point of God's plan is that the mode of knowing matters — that you have to *become* a certain kind of being through participatory engagement with God — then you cannot skip the participation and arrive at the destination. The destination isn't a location. It is the kind of being that participatory knowing produces. An unclean thing cannot inherit the kingdom of heaven for the same reason a person who has never been in water cannot "be a swimmer." The state cannot be downloaded. It must be participated into.

Satan's plan — compel obedience, guarantee the return — fails not because God arbitrarily rejects it, but because it is structurally impossible. Propositional compliance does not produce the transformation. Only participatory knowledge does. Saving someone *in* their sins would mean placing a being who has not participated in knowing God into the presence of a God they do not participatorily know. It would be meaningless — like placing someone who has never learned music into an orchestra and calling them a musician.

This also reframes what "saved *from* sins" means. It is not primarily a legal declaration (God stamps your file "clean"). It is a participatory transformation — faith-as-participatory-knowledge actually changes what you are. The knowing-through-doing is the mechanism of sanctification itself. Repentance, on this model, is not paying a penalty. It is re-entering participation after you have stepped away from it.

## Live Vulnerability: The Failure Problem

Both validation monks independently identified this as a crack in the load-bearing structure, not a downstream question. It is acknowledged here as unresolved.

If faith is participatory knowledge with built-in verification — plant the seed, feel the swelling — what happens to the person who participates sincerely and feels nothing? The synthesis claims credulity doesn't produce the swelling but real faith does. This is either:
- **Circular:** "If you felt nothing, you weren't really participating" (unfalsifiable)
- **Incomplete:** It smuggles back propositional prerequisites ("you need the right seed first"), which partially collapses toward the foundation position

The orientation-of-will addition helps — participation requires willing engagement oriented toward God, not just exposure. But it doesn't fully resolve the case of sincere, well-oriented participation that produces no feedback. Is the feedback guaranteed? Delayed? Dependent on conditions the synthesis hasn't named?

This is the open wound. The synthesis does not pretend it is resolved. It is the primary candidate for Round 2.

## Remaining Contradictions (Queued)

1. **The failure problem (sharpened).** What makes participatory knowing succeed or fail? If the answer is "the right orientation of will," then what orients the will? If the answer is "desire," then what produces the desire? Is there an infinite regress, or does it bottom out somewhere? How does this model handle doubt, faith crises, and the dark night of the soul?

2. **The eschatological problem.** If the veil creates space for participation, what is the *resurrected* relationship with God? When participation is no longer constrained by the veil, does faith transform into something else, or is it the same participatory knowing without the constraint? Is the celestial kingdom a place where participatory knowledge is complete, or where it operates on new dimensions?

3. **The communal problem.** Most scriptural examples are individual. But the church is a collective body. Is communal faith participatory knowledge of God exercised collectively? How does one person's participation affect another's? What does it mean for a community to "know God" participatorily?

4. **The Satan problem (partially addressed).** The orientation-of-will addition explains Satan's failure: participation without the right orientation produces propositional knowledge, not relational. But this raises a deeper question: does propositional knowledge of God *hinder* participatory knowledge? Can knowing too much *about* God become an obstacle to knowing God *through engagement*? (The Pharisees as a case study.)
