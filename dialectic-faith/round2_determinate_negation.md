# Phase 5: Round 2 Determinate Negation — The Mechanics of the Participatory Cycle

## 5.0 Internal Tensions

### Monk A (Human-Driven) undermines itself
It argues God is a constant (the sun metaphor) — uniform, universal, not calibrating. But Monk A's own proof texts feature a God who speaks *directly* to specific people. D&C 58:26 is a personal communication to a specific group at a specific time about their specific situation. That IS calibration. The brother of Jared is rebuked for three hours and then given the most intimate theophany in scripture — calibrated rebuke AND calibrated reward. Monk A needs God to be personal enough to create the plan of salvation and institute the veil, but impersonal enough to be a uniform broadcast. It can't have both.

Also: if God's provision is truly constant and the variation is entirely human, where does the human "variable" come from? Why do some intelligences choose differently than others given identical input? Monk A needs to either smuggle in prior divine differentiation or assert brute contingency — neither of which it addresses.

### Monk B (Grace-Driven) undermines itself
It argues "the seed does the growing" — but Alma 32:39 explicitly says "if ye neglect the tree... it will not get any root." If the seed is truly the engine, neglect shouldn't kill it. A powerful-enough seed should grow on its own power. But it doesn't. It requires active human nourishment. Monk B's own metaphor breaks under its own claim.

Also: the asymmetry argument (divine withdrawal = annihilation, human withdrawal = pause) doesn't prove what Monk B claims. In ANY relationship, if one party ceases to exist, the relationship ends. That doesn't make the still-existing party "primary." If I die, my marriage ends — that doesn't make me the "load-bearing" spouse.

## 5.1 Surface Contradiction

Human agency is the decisive variable in the participatory cycle vs. divine grace (active, calibrated, responsive) is the decisive variable.

## 5.2 Shared Assumptions

1. **Both assume the cycle has an "engine" — a primary mover.** Both seek which side is "load-bearing," "sufficient," or "drives" the process. Neither considers that in a relationship between persons, there is no primary mover.

2. **Both use machine metaphors.** Engine, load-bearing, variable, constant, calibration, broadcast, receiver. These are engineering concepts applied to a personal relationship. Machines have engines. Relationships have something else.

3. **Both treat the divine and human contributions as separable and weighable.** "How much is God? How much is human?" This assumes the contributions are decomposable. But Round 1 established that participatory knowledge is constituted by the *interaction*. The interaction isn't decomposable into percentages.

4. **Both assume a temporal sequence.** God moves first (Monk B) or God provides then we choose (Monk A). Neither considers that the relationship might be a *structure* rather than a sequence.

## 5.3 Determinate Negation

- **Monk A fails** because the sun metaphor (constant/impersonal God) cannot account for the personal, responsive God of the Restoration who speaks to individuals, gives specific commands, and adapts to specific people. This failure reveals: Monk A needs a framework for divine responsiveness that doesn't make God "the author of failure."

- **Monk B fails** because "the seed does the growing" cannot account for the decisive role of human nourishment/neglect in every scriptural example. If grace is the engine, human failure should be harder than scripture says it is. This failure reveals: Monk B needs a framework for genuine human responsibility within a grace-saturated system.

The failures are complementary: A can't explain divine responsiveness. B can't explain human responsibility. Both need a structure where both are fully active and neither is "primary."

## 5.4 The Hidden Question

Not "who drives the cycle?" but **"what is the *structure* of the relationship in which the cycle operates?"**

The monks are arguing about the engine of a machine. But the cycle isn't a machine — it's a relationship between persons. The question for a relationship isn't "who drives?" but "what kind of relationship is this, and what makes it productive?"

## 5.5 Boydian Decomposition

### Generic space
Both share a causal model (input → processing → output) where one side provides the dominant input. This is the skeleton to transcend.

### Atomic components (stripped from positions)
- God provides light universally (Moroni 7:16)
- God grants according to heed and diligence (Alma 12:9)
- Greater/lesser portion based on hardening (Alma 12:10-11)
- Light grows brighter unto perfect day (D&C 50:24)
- Line upon line, precept upon precept (2 Nephi 28:30)
- Spirit entices, humans yield (Mosiah 3:19)
- "Power is in them, agents unto themselves" (D&C 58:28)
- Seed enlarges/enlightens; human nourishes (Alma 32:28, 39)
- By grace after all we can do (2 Nephi 25:23)
- Saul's unsought conversion
- Brother of Jared's creative initiative
- War in heaven: a third fell
- Both monks agree: failure is always human, never divine
- Sun metaphor (constant, impersonal)
- Teacher metaphor (responsive, personal)

### Surprising cross-connections

- **"Failure is always human" (A) + "God never withdraws first" (B)** → Same claim, different angles. Both agree the asymmetry is about *reliability*, not primacy. God is perfectly reliable. Humans are not. In a covenant, the reliable party's role differs from the unreliable party's role, but neither is "the engine."

- **"The Spirit entices" + "the power is in them"** → Not competing claims. Two sides of a covenant interaction. Mosiah 3:19's "yield to the enticings" is a covenant response: God entices (divine commitment), humans yield (human commitment). Different roles within a single structure.

- **Alma 12:9 + D&C 50:24** → Both describe a responsive feedback loop. But the loop isn't driven by either side alone. The *mutual commitment structure* creates the loop. God commits to respond to faithfulness. Humans commit to be faithful. Break the human commitment → loop degrades. God's commitment holds → loop can always resume.

### Adjacent-domain material

**LDS covenant theology.** Covenants ARE the structural mechanism of the God-human relationship. Baptism, sacrament, temple — in each, God makes specific promises and the human makes specific promises. The covenant creates a mutual commitment structure within which the participatory cycle operates.

**The sacrament as micro-cycle.** Every week: remember (receive God's input), recommit (human will), partake (act), receive the Spirit (divine responsive grace). The sacrament prayer names both sides explicitly: "willing to take upon them the name... always remember him... keep his commandments" (human) → "that they may always have his Spirit to be with them" (divine response). This IS the covenantal structure of participatory knowing.

**Parent-child development.** A parent who provides everything regardless produces dependence. A parent who provides nothing produces abandonment. A good parent provides a structured environment within which the child develops through their own choices — responsive, adapting the structure to development. Neither "drives" the growth. The structured relationship drives it.

## 5.6 Sublation Criteria

- Must preserve Monk A's insight: human choice is genuinely decisive, failure is always human, agency is real
- Must preserve Monk B's insight: God is active, responsive, personal; grace initiates and is indispensable
- Must dissolve: the shared assumption that one side must be "primary" or "load-bearing"
- Must answer: what is the structure of the relationship in which the cycle operates?
