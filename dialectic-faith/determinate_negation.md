# Phase 5: Determinate Negation — What Is Faith?

## 5.0 Internal Tensions

### Monk A (Foundation) contradicts itself
It argues that faith is knowledge-and-confidence, then spends its most forceful section explaining how Abraham's *knowledge* enabled his *leap*. But if faith is the knowledge itself, why does the Abraham story need a leap at all? Monk A keeps needing the leap to make its own examples work, then insisting the leap isn't the essential thing. Its strongest move — "Abraham reasoned about resurrection" — reduces Moriah to a theological calculation, which doesn't match the tradition's treatment of it as the *supreme trial.* If Abraham simply reasoned his way through it, it's not much of a trial. Monk A's own logic demands that the leap be real and costly, but its thesis can't accommodate that cost.

### Monk B (Leap) contradicts itself
It insists the leap is the "sufficient condition" — the thing that defines faith. But then it repeatedly acknowledges the platform is the "necessary condition" and that without it you're just falling. It says "a runway is not a flight" — but a flight without a runway is a crash. If the leap's entire meaning, direction, and faithfulness (vs. credulity) comes from the foundation, then the foundation is doing more structural work than Monk B admits. Monk B wants faith to live in the act, but the act's *content* is entirely derived from the knowledge. A contentless leap is, as Monk A correctly notes, a coin flip.

## 5.1 Surface Contradiction

Faith is fundamentally knowledge of God (epistemic state) vs. faith is fundamentally action at the edge (existential act).

## 5.2 Shared Assumptions

Both monks share several assumptions they don't realize they share:

1. **Both treat knowing and acting as separate categories.** Foundation says knowledge drives action. Leap says action is the faith-part. Neither considers that they might be inseparable — that there's a kind of knowing that IS acting.

2. **Both treat knowledge as propositional.** "Knowledge of God's character" is treated like information — facts about God you accumulate and store. Neither asks whether knowing God might be more like knowing a *person* than knowing a *theorem*.

3. **Both assume a linear model.** Foundation: knowledge accumulates toward completion. Leap: edges keep appearing as knowledge grows. Both assume the direction is forward/upward, just disagreeing about whether you arrive or asymptote.

4. **Both center the individual's epistemic state.** Neither deeply engages faith as *relational* — as something that exists in the space between the person and God, constituted by the interaction itself.

## 5.3 Determinate Negation

- Monk A fails because it reduces faith to information-about-God. It cannot explain why the most knowledgeable people face the hardest trials — if knowledge IS faith, more should mean easier, not harder. Its Abraham move (he reasoned about resurrection) is technically defensible but experientially hollow. It evacuates Moriah of its weight.

- Monk B fails because it cannot explain what makes a leap *faithful* rather than foolish without appealing to the very foundation it claims is not the faith. It locates faith in the act but the act's meaning comes entirely from the knowledge. It's parasitic on the thing it denies is primary.

**Both fail in the same specific way:** they're trying to locate faith in one half of something that only exists as a whole. This is like asking whether swimming is "in" the water or "in" the swimmer's movements. The question has no coherent answer because swimming is constituted by their interaction.

## 5.4 The Hidden Question

The real question is not "is faith knowledge or action?" It's: **"What kind of knowing is faith?"**

Both monks assume knowing is passive — you acquire information, you possess it, it sits in your mind. But what if faith is a kind of knowing that *cannot exist without acting*? A knowing-through-participation? A relational knowledge that is constituted by the engagement between knower and known?

## 5.5 Boydian Decomposition

### Atomic components stripped from positions:
- Knowledge of God's character / Confidence before God / Faith-credulity distinction requires content / Faith becomes dormant where knowledge reigns / Epistemic trajectory toward fullness
- The act at the edge / Veil as purposive instrument / Trials scale with knowledge / "Principle of action" / Agency requires the gap / Knowledge replaces faith locally not globally / Brother of Jared *inventing* the stones / Lecture 6's sacrifice of all things / "Eye of faith" (Ether 12:19)

### Surprising cross-connections:
- "Faith/credulity distinction requires knowledge" + "the veil is the instrument" → The veil doesn't test *courage*. It tests whether your knowledge of God is real enough to *function under uncertainty*. It tests the quality of your participatory knowing.
- "Faith becomes dormant where knowledge reigns" + "knowledge replaces faith locally, not globally" → Both monks actually agree: faith is active at the *frontier* and dormant in *settled territory*. Faith is a living boundary, not a static state.
- "Brother of Jared inventing the stones" + "knowing God's character" → He knew God well enough to *propose* something creative. This is neither pure knowledge (he didn't deduce the answer) nor pure leap (he wasn't random). It's creative initiative grounded in relational knowledge. He knew God the way you know a collaborator.
- "Agency requires the gap" + "God's posture is disclosure" → Both true simultaneously. Each disclosure opens new territory for agency. God reveals, which gives you more to work with, which gives you more edges to act at.

### Adjacent-domain material:
The concept of *participatory knowledge* — knowing-through-doing. You don't know how to swim by reading about swimming. You know it by swimming. The Hebrew *yada* (to know) carries connotations of intimate, experiential, relational knowledge — not abstract information. In LDS theology, D&C 93 describes growing "from grace to grace" — a participatory, progressive relationship, not an information download.

### The War in Heaven as structural key:
Satan's plan was to remove agency — compel obedience, eliminate the gap, guarantee the outcome. God's plan preserves agency — the veil, the darkness, the space for genuine participation. This maps directly onto the dialectic: Satan's plan would have produced propositional knowledge without participation. Everyone would have "known" the right answers but never actually engaged with God relationally. God's plan requires the gap precisely because participatory knowledge can only exist through genuine engagement under uncertainty. The war in heaven was, in a real sense, a war over whether faith (participatory knowledge) matters — whether the knowing-through-doing is the point, or whether the correct outcome (everyone returns) is the point regardless of how you get there.

## 5.6 Sublation Criteria

The synthesis must:
- Preserve Monk A's insight that knowledge of God's character is structurally essential — not optional, not just a platform
- Preserve Monk B's insight that faith requires active participation at the edge, that the veil is purposive, and that agency is the point
- Dissolve the shared assumption that knowing and acting are separate categories
- Answer the hidden question: what kind of knowing is faith?
- Account for the war in heaven: why does God's plan require the gap?
