# Phase 6: Round 2 Sublation — The Covenantal Cycle

## The Metaphor Trap

Both Round 2 monks are imprisoned in the same metaphor: **organization as machine** (Gareth Morgan's Metaphor #1). Engine, load-bearing, variable, constant, calibration, broadcast, receiver — every key term in both essays is a machine concept. The machine metaphor naturally asks "who drives?" because machines have engines. But the participatory knowing cycle established in Round 1 is not a machine. It is a living relationship between persons. Asking "who drives the relationship?" is like asking "who drives a conversation?" — the question reveals you're thinking about the wrong kind of thing.

Three alternative metaphors are more illuminating:

**Organization as Organism (Metaphor #2):** Alma 32's seed/tree IS an organism metaphor. Organisms grow through interaction with their environment — neither the organism nor the environment "drives" the growth. The seed needs soil and nourishing; the soil needs the seed. Remove either and nothing happens. But no one asks "is the seed the engine or is the soil the engine?" because the question is incoherent for living systems. Growth is an emergent property of the interaction.

**System of Change and Flux (Metaphor #7):** The participatory cycle is a dynamically stable pattern — like a whirlpool maintained by continuous flow. The river flows, the rock shapes the flow, and the whirlpool emerges as a standing pattern at their interaction. Neither the river nor the rock "drives" the whirlpool. The whirlpool is an emergent structure that exists only at their interface. The covenantal cycle is this kind of pattern: a stable, self-maintaining interaction between divine faithfulness and human engagement that produces participatory knowing as an emergent property. Disrupt either side and the pattern dissipates.

**Instrument of Domination (Metaphor #8, inverted):** Satan's plan IS the domination metaphor applied to salvation. Compel obedience, guarantee outcomes, remove agency. God's plan explicitly rejects the domination metaphor in favor of the covenantal/organic: mutual commitment, growth through interaction, emergent transformation.

## The Synthesis: Covenant as the Structure of Participatory Knowing

**The participatory knowing cycle operates through covenant — a mutual commitment structure with asymmetric reliability.**

The question "who drives the cycle — human agency or divine grace?" is malformed. It applies a machine metaphor to a living covenantal relationship. Covenants don't have engines. They have two parties with different roles, different reliability profiles, and a mutual commitment that creates a productive feedback loop.

A covenant is a mutual commitment structure:
- **God commits** to provide light, Spirit, word, responsive grace — faithfully, personally, calibrated to the person
- **The human commits** to receive, give heed and diligence, nourish, act with genuine engagement
- **The interaction between fulfilled commitments** creates the participatory knowing cycle as an emergent property — like the whirlpool at the interface of river and rock

Neither party is "the engine." The covenant is the engine. Or rather: there IS no engine. There is a living pattern sustained by mutual faithfulness.

### The Asymmetry: Reliability, Not Primacy — But an Honest Concession

The covenant IS asymmetric, but the asymmetry is about *reliability*, not *primacy*:

- **God's side is perfectly reliable.** He always keeps his covenant commitments. He never breaks the pattern from his side. This is what Monk B correctly perceived — grace is indispensable, God initiates, God is faithful.
- **The human side is variable.** We sometimes harden, neglect, or break covenant. This is what Monk A correctly perceived — the variation in spiritual trajectory maps to the variation in human response. Failure is always on our side.

But "perfectly reliable" ≠ "primary." It means *the cycle is always resumable*. Because God never leaves the covenant, repentance is always possible. You can re-enter the cycle at any point because God's side is still there, still enticing, still holding out the covenant commitment. The cycle is more robust to human failure than to divine withdrawal — not because God is "the engine" but because the reliable party's continued commitment preserves the structure even when the other party falters.

**An honest concession:** The claim "neither side is primary" sits in tension with "failure is always human." If all variance in outcome traces to the human side, then human response IS the decisive variable in a meaningful sense — Monk A was more right about the *locus of variation* than this synthesis initially acknowledged. What the covenantal frame adds is not a denial of this, but *context*: the human variable operates within a divinely created and sustained structure. Human choice is decisive, but it is not self-generated. A farmer's choices determine the harvest, but the farmer did not create the soil, the rain, or the sun — and without those, the farmer's choices mean nothing. The honest position is: **human agency is the decisive variable within a structure that is entirely God's gift.** Monk A was right about where the variation lives. Monk B was right about what makes the variation meaningful. Neither insight reduces to the other.

**A further complication — D&C 82:10:** "I, the Lord, am bound when ye do what I say; but when ye do not what I say, ye have no promise." God's covenantal obligations are conditional. When humans break covenant, God's promises are voided — not because God has broken faith, but because the covenant terms have explicit activation conditions. This is not "God never breaks covenant" in a naive sense. It is: God's character is perfectly reliable, God's covenant terms are faithfully kept, but those terms include conditions. A covenant in which one party has failed to perform is a covenant whose promises are suspended, not a covenant God has abandoned. The structure is preserved; the promises await re-activation through repentance.

### Monk A's "Author of Failure" Problem, Dissolved

Monk A argued: if God calibrates (giving more to the receptive, less to the hard-hearted), God becomes the author of the downward spiral.

The covenantal frame dissolves this. God's calibration is not rationing — it is *covenant faithfulness*. The covenant says: I will give according to heed and diligence. When you give heed, God gives more — that's keeping the covenant. When you harden, the lesser portion is not punishment; it is the structural consequence of the human side of the covenant going unfulfilled. God doesn't withdraw. You stop receiving. The covenant structure explains why it looks like calibration (it IS responsive) without making God the agent of failure (the human broke covenant).

This is exactly how the organic metaphor works: if you stop watering the tree, it withers. The sun didn't withdraw. The rain didn't stop. You neglected your side of the relationship with the living thing you planted. The tree's death is the consequence of your neglect, not the sun's punishment.

### Monk B's Pelagianism Problem, Dissolved

Monk B argued: if human agency is the engine, God is furniture — a spiritual vending machine. That's Pelagianism.

The covenantal frame dissolves this too. In a covenant, the human side is genuine and decisive — you can break it, and your breaking it has real consequences. But the human side operates *within* a structure God created and sustains. You don't generate the light. You don't create the seed. You don't invent the covenant. God created all of that and continuously sustains it. Your role is to engage, nourish, and act within the structure God provides. That is not Pelagianism (self-salvation through effort). It is covenant faithfulness — genuine human agency operating within a divinely created and sustained relational structure.

### The Sacrament as Micro-Cycle

The sacrament enacts the full covenantal cycle in miniature every week:

1. **God's covenant commitment** — the bread and water, representing the Atonement. The offering. God's side, already given.
2. **Human covenant commitment** — "willing to take upon them the name of thy Son, and always remember him, and keep his commandments." The recommitment. The human side, renewed.
3. **The covenant promise activated** — "that they may always have his Spirit to be with them." The emergent property: the Spirit as the ongoing medium of participatory knowing, produced by the interaction of mutual faithfulness.

This is not a machine (insert coin, receive Spirit). It is not one-sided (God provides, you receive). It is covenantal: both parties fulfill commitments, and the interaction produces something neither party could produce alone.

### The Failure Modes, Explained Covenantally

The user identified two real failure modes. Both are covenantal failures:

**Insincere engagement** = bad-faith covenant-keeping. Going through the motions of the sacrament without genuine recommitment. The covenant structure is intact but the human side is performing compliance rather than genuine participation. This is the Alma 12:10 "hardening" — not refusing the covenant outright but refusing to engage it with real heed and diligence. The machine metaphor would call this "low effort." The covenantal metaphor calls it what it is: bad faith. You are at the table but not keeping your promise.

**Unrealistic expectations** = misunderstanding what God covenanted to provide. Expecting dramatic revelations when the covenant promises the Spirit. Expecting immediate answers when the covenant promises "line upon line." The feedback IS there — "enlarging my soul, enlightening my understanding, delicious" (Alma 32:28) — but the person is looking for something God didn't promise. The machine metaphor would call this "output mismatch." The covenantal metaphor reveals it as a misunderstanding of the covenant terms.

**Why "sincere participation that produces silence" is not a real failure mode — and the epistemic risk of saying so:** God keeps his covenants. If you genuinely engage the covenant — remember, repent, recommit, act with real heed and diligence — God's covenant promise activates: you will have his Spirit. The Spirit's presence may not look like what you expected (failure mode b). Silence from a covenant-keeping God to a covenant-keeping human would be a broken covenant, and God doesn't break covenants.

**But this claim carries an epistemic risk that must be acknowledged.** If every reported experience of silence gets reclassified as either insincere engagement (a) or unrealistic expectations (b), the model becomes unfalsifiable — no evidence could ever count against it. This is a real concern, not a dismissible one. The honest response is: the covenant promise IS real, but our ability to assess our own sincerity and expectations is imperfect. The model predicts that genuine covenant-keeping produces real feedback. It does not claim that *we can always tell from the inside* whether our engagement is genuine or our expectations are calibrated. The gap between the covenant's reliability and our self-knowledge is where the experienced difficulty lives. This is not a flaw in the model — it IS the veil doing its work. But it means the model offers assurance at the theological level ("God keeps his promises") without offering certainty at the experiential level ("I can always tell if I'm receiving"). That tension is real, and a mature faith holds both.

### Ordinances as Covenant On-Ramps

Baptism, sacrament, temple ordinances are not arbitrary rituals. They are the structural entry points into the participatory knowing cycle — the moments where the mutual commitment is formally made.

Without the covenant structure, the "receive, decide, act, receive again" cycle has no framework. It's individual spiritual experience with no mutual commitments, no promises, no structure. The ordinances create the covenantal architecture within which participatory knowing operates. They are to the participatory cycle what the veil is to the mortal experience: the structural feature that makes the process possible.

### The Pre-Covenantal Grace: What Launches the Cycle

The covenantal frame describes how the participatory cycle *operates*. But it does not explain how the cycle *begins*. This is Monk B's strongest surviving insight: before any human has entered any covenant, God has already acted.

The light of Christ (Moroni 7:16) is given to every person — not as a covenant fulfillment but as a unilateral gift. No one entered a mutual commitment to receive it. No one yielded to get it. It precedes baptism, precedes the sacrament, precedes every ordinance. It is pure grace: unconditioned, unearned, prior to any human response whatsoever.

This means **the covenant is not turtles all the way down.** At the bottom of the stack, there is a gift. The covenantal structure is what sustains and deepens the participatory cycle, but something that is not covenantal — something purely gracious, purely initiated by God — is what makes the cycle *possible* in the first place. The light of Christ is the pre-covenantal ground that enables the first yielding of Mosiah 3:19. You cannot yield to enticings you have not received. God provides the enticings before any mutual commitment exists.

**The Saul problem reinforces this.** Saul on the Damascus road was in no covenant with Christ. He was in active, violent opposition to Christ's church. There was no mutual commitment structure. God broke in — not as coercion in the Satan's-plan sense (Saul still had to choose what to do with the experience), but as unilateral initiative that created a new possibility where none existed. Alma the Younger is the same pattern: an angel came not because Alma had fulfilled covenant terms, but because of his father's prayers. The covenantal frame has no mechanism for this. These are cases where God acts *outside* the covenant to create the conditions under which a person *can* enter the covenant.

The honest position: **the covenantal cycle is the ongoing structure, but it is launched by unilateral grace and sometimes interrupted by unilateral grace.** God's relationship with humans has at least two modes — covenantal (mutual commitment, ongoing cycle) and pre-covenantal or extra-covenantal (unilateral initiative, creating the conditions for covenant). The synthesis's covenantal frame describes the dominant mode but not the only mode. This is a live limitation, and the Round 3 exploration of God's multiple relational metaphors will need to address it directly.

### The War in Heaven, Covenantally Reframed

Satan's plan was to bypass the covenant entirely — the domination metaphor (Morgan's #8) applied to salvation. Compelled obedience requires no mutual commitment because there is no genuine human party making genuine promises. No covenant. No participation. No participatory knowing.

God's plan is the covenantal plan: I will provide, you will engage, and the mutual commitment will produce beings who know me participatorily. The third who fell refused the covenant — not because they received insufficient grace, but because they rejected the covenantal structure itself. They wanted the outcome without the mutual commitment.

## Model Update

- **Before (Round 1):** Faith is participatory knowledge of God operating through a cycle of "receive, decide, act, receive again." The cycle requires the orientation of will.
- **After (Round 2):** The cycle operates through *covenant* — a mutual commitment structure with asymmetric reliability. God's side (grace, light, Spirit) is perfectly reliable and actively responsive. The human side (heed, diligence, nourishment) is genuinely decisive but sometimes fails. Human agency is the decisive variable within a structure that is entirely God's gift — Monk A was right about where the variation lives, Monk B was right about what makes the variation meaningful. God's covenant terms are conditional (D&C 82:10) — promises activate when conditions are met, suspend when broken, and await re-activation through repentance. The covenant is launched by unilateral, pre-covenantal grace (light of Christ, Moroni 7:16) and sometimes interrupted by extra-covenantal divine initiative (Saul, Alma the Younger). Ordinances are the structural on-ramps. The sacrament is the cycle in miniature. The model's epistemic risk: the promise that sincere engagement produces feedback is theologically robust but experientially uncertain, because our self-knowledge is imperfect — and that gap IS the veil doing its work.
- **Because:** Both monks were trapped in machine metaphors (#1 in Morgan's eight metaphors framework), trying to identify the "engine" of a living covenantal relationship. The organism (#2) and flux/change (#7) metaphors reveal that the cycle is an emergent pattern, not a machine with a driver. The domination metaphor (#8) applies to Satan's plan, which the covenantal plan explicitly rejects. But the covenant metaphor itself has limits — it describes the dominant mode of God's relationship with humans, not the only mode. God also operates in pre-covenantal and extra-covenantal modes that the Round 2 synthesis acknowledges but does not fully map.

## New Contradictions Generated

1. **The covenant-breaking spiral.** If God never breaks covenant and the cycle is always resumable, what about Alma 12:11 — "they know nothing concerning his mysteries; and then they are taken captive by the devil"? Is there a point of no return? Can the human side of the covenant degrade so far that resumption becomes functionally impossible even though God hasn't withdrawn? What does "chains of hell" mean covenantally?

2. **The pre-covenant problem.** The synthesis says ordinances are covenant on-ramps. But people exercise faith before baptism. Investigators, children, people who've never heard the gospel. If the covenantal structure is what makes the cycle productive, how does faith work for people who haven't entered the covenant yet? Is there a pre-covenantal mode of participatory knowing?

3. **The communal covenant.** Covenants in LDS theology are often communal — temple work connects the living and the dead, Zion is a covenant community. How does the individual covenantal cycle interact with the communal one? Does one person's covenant faithfulness create conditions for another's?

4. **The covenant and the natural man.** Mosiah 3:19 says the natural man is an enemy to God "unless he yields." But yielding requires... what? If you need the covenant to orient the cycle, and you need to yield to enter the covenant, what enables the initial yielding? Is the light of Christ (Moroni 7:16, given to every person) a pre-covenantal grace that enables the first step?
