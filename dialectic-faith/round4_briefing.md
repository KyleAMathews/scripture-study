# Round 4 Context Briefing: The Institution vs. The Economy

## Prior Syntheses

**Round 1:** Faith is participatory knowledge of God — relational knowing constituted by engagement, where knowing and acting are the same motion.

**Round 2:** The participatory cycle operates through covenant — mutual commitment with asymmetric reliability. Human agency is the decisive variable within a structure that is God's gift.

**Round 3:** The covenant is the structure, but its function is the formation of persons deployed within an economy of salvation. Development describes how the instrument is formed (potter → friend); plurality describes what the instrument does (matching the vineyard's diverse needs). The economy extends trans-temporally through vicarious work. Jacob 5 is the master text. D&C 121:34-46 is the operational text: the instrument works only through persuasion, long-suffering, love unfeigned. The moment it switches to compulsion, the powers of heaven withdraw. There is no gap between personal development and instrumental deployment — becoming like God (full of charity, operating through love) just IS being effective in the economy.

**The critical tension Round 3 surfaced:** D&C 121:39 — "almost all men, as soon as they get a little authority, as they suppose, they will immediately begin to exercise unrighteous dominion." The economy runs on love or it doesn't run (v36). But the instruments keep breaking — nearly universally.

## The Round 4 Tension

The Round 3 synthesis describes an economy of salvation that operates through persuasion, gentleness, love unfeigned, and pure knowledge (D&C 121:41-42). The economy's instruments are covenant people whose development has made them like God in character — who serve not from assignment but from transformed nature.

But the ACTUAL institutional church through which this economy operates is a hierarchical organization with:
- **Centralized prophetic authority** — the prophet speaks for God; obedience to the prophet is framed as obedience to God
- **Hierarchical priesthood structure** — stake presidents, bishops, quorum presidents, with formal authority over members
- **Worthiness interviews** — access to temple (the economy's highest ordinances) is gatekept by interviews about personal behavior
- **Membership councils** (formerly "disciplinary councils") — the church can revoke membership, restrict sacrament access, excommunicate
- **Correlation** — centralized curriculum, standardized programs, reduced local autonomy
- **Social pressure** — cultural expectations about appearance, behavior, political views, family structure
- **Callings** — assignments given by leaders, expected to be accepted as from God

The question: **Is the institutional church the economy of salvation's necessary vehicle, or does the institution sometimes become the obstacle the economy is supposed to overcome?**

D&C 121 was written FROM JAIL — by a prophet whose own institutional authority had been stripped by the state. The passage about unrighteous dominion is not abstract theology. It is a diagnosis of what happens when human institutions handle divine authority. And the diagnosis is devastating: "almost all men" fail.

## Key Evidence

### For the Institution as Necessary Vehicle

**D&C 1:30:** "The only true and living church upon the face of the whole earth, with which I, the Lord, am well pleased, speaking unto the church collectively and not individually." God claims the institution despite its flaws.

**D&C 84:19-22:** The ordinances of the priesthood are necessary — "without the ordinances thereof, and the authority of the priesthood, the power of godliness is not manifest unto men in the flesh." The economy REQUIRES institutional ordinances. You cannot baptize yourself. You cannot ordain yourself. You cannot perform temple work alone. The economy is structurally dependent on authorized institutional channels.

**3 Nephi 11:21-28:** Christ personally established institutional structure — baptismal authority, mode, words. He did not leave it to individual encounter. He structured it.

**Alma 13:** The priesthood was "prepared from the foundation of the world" — the institutional authority structure is part of the eternal plan, not an afterthought.

**D&C 107:** Detailed institutional structure — quorums, keys, councils, procedures. God cares about institutional design.

**Practical reality:** The church's institutional structure is what enables the economy's trans-temporal extension. Temple work requires buildings, records, authorized ordinance workers, genealogical databases. Missionary work requires organization, training, coordination. The economy of salvation at scale requires institutional infrastructure.

### For the Institution as Obstacle

**D&C 121:34-40:** The definitive text. "Many are called, but few are chosen" — because of pride, worldly aspiration, and unrighteous dominion. This is written ABOUT institutional authority holders, not about members in general. The priesthood holder who exercises compulsion loses the Spirit (v37) and is "left unto himself" (v38) while still holding institutional office. The institution can contain people with formal authority but no actual spiritual power.

**3 Nephi 16:10:** "When the Gentiles shall sin against my gospel... I will bring the fulness of my gospel from among them." God will remove the gospel from institutional holders who corrupt it. He has done this before (apostasy).

**Mormon 8:35-38:** Moroni saw our day and condemned institutional religion that tolerates pride, fine clothing, and neglect of the poor. "Why do ye adorn yourselves with that which hath no life, and yet suffer the hungry, and the needy, and the naked... to pass by you, and notice them not?"

**The Pharisees:** The definitive scriptural case of institutional religion obstructing the economy. Perfect institutional compliance. Perfect doctrinal knowledge. Perfect authority structure. And they killed the Messiah. The institution's own authority structure could not recognize the Person the institution was supposed to serve.

**Historical cases:**
- The Kirtland Safety Society — institutional economic decisions that damaged the community
- The Nauvoo period — concentration of authority leading to internal conflicts
- 20th century correlation — centralizing spiritual authority in ways that reduced local responsiveness
- The 2015 exclusion policy (reversed 2019) — institutional policy that many felt contradicted the economy's purpose
- Ongoing tensions around race, gender, and LGBTQ+ inclusion where institutional positions create barriers

**Jacob 5:48:** Even the Lord of the vineyard asks "What could I have done more for my vineyard?" — acknowledging that despite His perfect management, the institutional structure (tame trees, wild branches, natural fruit) keeps producing corruption. The institution is necessary but perpetually failing.

### The Structural Tension

The economy of salvation REQUIRES institutional structure (ordinances need authority, temple work needs buildings, missionary work needs organization). But institutional structure GENERATES the failure mode the economy most fears (unrighteous dominion, compulsion, pride, gatekeeping). The institution is simultaneously necessary and dangerous.

This is not "institutions are bad" vs. "institutions are good." It is: **the same feature that makes the institution necessary (concentrated authority for ordinances) is the feature that makes it dangerous (concentrated authority invites domination).** The institution is necessary AND the institution is the primary site of instrument failure.

## The Ontological Question for Round 4

**What is the relationship between the institutional church and the economy of salvation? Is the institution the economy's vehicle, its obstacle, or something more structurally complex — and does D&C 121 provide a model for how divine authority can operate through fallen institutional structures without being destroyed by them?**

## Degenerate Framings to Avoid

- **"The church is perfect, people aren't"** — this dodges the structural question. D&C 121 says the STRUCTURE of authority invites corruption. The people aren't failing despite the institution — the institution is generating the failure mode.
- **"Just follow the prophet"** — this is one side of the debate, not a resolution. It needs to be argued, not assumed.
- **"The church is just a human institution"** — this ignores D&C 1:30, D&C 84's claims about ordinance authority, and the economy's structural dependence on authorized channels.
- **"It's complicated"** — yes. Say something specific about HOW it's complicated.
- **Anti-institutional cynicism** — the institution has also produced enormous good: millions of people served, lives changed, ordinances performed, temples built, dead redeemed. The question is structural, not evaluative.
