# Dialectic Queue: What Is Faith?

## Explored

### Round 1: Foundation vs. Leap
- **Thesis:** Faith is accumulated knowledge of God that produces confidence (Foundation)
- **Antithesis:** Faith is the act of stepping beyond what you know into darkness (Leap)
- **Synthesis:** Faith is participatory knowledge of God — a relational knowing constituted by engagement, where knowing and acting are the same motion
- **Key addition:** The war in heaven as structural key; orientation of will as what makes participation fruitful; Alma 11:37 "cannot save them in their sins" as structural claim about why participation can't be skipped
- **Live vulnerability:** The failure problem — is the feedback mechanism circular or incomplete?
- **Files:** `monk_a_foundation.md`, `monk_b_leap.md`, `determinate_negation.md`, `sublation.md`

### Round 2: Human Response vs. Divine Calibration (Directions A+B combined)
- **Thesis:** The participatory cycle is governed by human response — God is the constant (sun), we are the variable (farmer). Human heed/diligence vs. hardening determines trajectory. (Monk A)
- **Antithesis:** The cycle is governed by divine calibration — grace is the load-bearing structure. God actively calibrates, the seed does the growing, human agency without grace is Pelagianism. (Monk B)
- **Synthesis:** The cycle operates through *covenant* — a mutual commitment structure with asymmetric reliability. Neither side is "the engine." The covenant is the structure within which participatory knowing emerges as a property of mutual faithfulness.
- **Key move:** Both monks were trapped in Morgan's Machine metaphor (#1), asking "who drives?" The Organism (#2) and Flux/Change (#7) metaphors reveal the cycle as an emergent pattern, not a machine with a driver. The Domination metaphor (#8) applies to Satan's plan, which the covenantal plan explicitly rejects.
- **Key additions:** Sacrament as micro-cycle enacting the full covenant; ordinances as covenant on-ramps; failure modes reframed covenantally (insincere engagement = bad-faith covenant-keeping, unrealistic expectations = misunderstanding covenant terms); "author of failure" problem dissolved (God's calibration IS covenant faithfulness, not rationing)
- **Files:** `round2_briefing.md`, `round2_monk_a_human_response.md`, `round2_monk_b_grace.md`, `round2_determinate_negation.md`, `round2_sublation.md`

### Round 3: Developmental Trajectory vs. Irreducible Plurality (Direction A: Metaphor Landscape)
- **Thesis:** God's metaphors describe a single developmental trajectory (potter → shepherd → father → friend) with covenant marking every transition. The temple is this trajectory ritualized. (Monk A)
- **Antithesis:** God's metaphors are irreducible simultaneous modes — the plurality itself is the revelation about God's nature. No single structure (including development or covenant) can contain them. (Monk B)
- **Synthesis:** The economy of salvation. Both monks analyzed the wrong unit (individual). The metaphors describe God's multi-modal, trans-temporal strategy for reaching ALL His children through a small covenant people. Development describes the formation of the instrument (how covenant people are shaped). Plurality describes the instrument's mission (matching the vineyard's diverse needs). Three-layer economy: universal grace (light of Christ) → covenant community (instrument) → trans-temporal extension (vicarious work). Jacob 5 as master text.
- **Key move:** The narrow way essay revealed that both monks were debating the interior architecture of a minority experience. The vast majority of God's children are outside formal covenant. The real question is how the covenant few serve the non-covenant many. The covenant people are not the endpoint but the expanding edge — salt, light, leaven.
- **Key additions:** The three-layer economy; development as instrument-formation, not personal growth as endpoint; plurality as vineyard-needs, not God's-nature; the sacrament as communal recommissioning; the war in heaven as the origin of the economy's necessity (agency creates diversity of conditions, which requires multi-modal strategy); D&C 128:15 as structural key (mutual interdependence of living and dead)
- **User intervention:** The narrow way essay — the vast majority of God's children don't have covenant relationships and don't know Him. Both monks were ignoring this.
- **Files:** `round3_briefing.md`, `round3_monk_a_developmental.md`, `round3_monk_b_pluralist.md`, `round3_determinate_negation.md`, `round3_sublation.md`

## Queued for Round 4

### Contradiction 1: The Quality-of-Instrument Problem (STRONGEST CANDIDATE)
- **Source:** Round 3 synthesis, new contradiction #1
- **Tension:** If the economy depends on human instruments (the covenant community), the economy inherits all the failures of human agency. Israel repeatedly failed its commission. Salt loses its savor. The church turns inward. Does the economy have a mechanism for surviving instrument failure? Or is instrument failure the structural explanation for "few there be that find it"?
- **Why it matters:** The entire Round 3 synthesis rests on the covenant community functioning as instrument. If the instrument is unreliable, the economy is fragile. This tension connects directly to the covenant-breaking spiral from Round 2.

### Contradiction 2: The "Almost All Men" Problem
- **Source:** Round 3 synthesis (post-validation), D&C 121:39
- **Tension:** "Almost all men, as soon as they get a little authority... will immediately begin to exercise unrighteous dominion." If the economy depends on instruments who operate through love and persuasion, and almost all corrupt authority into compulsion, the economy's throughput is devastatingly low. This may structurally explain "few there be that find it" — not that the gate is hard to find, but that the instruments keep breaking.
- **Why it matters:** The quality-of-instrument problem is now specified: the failure mode is not just unreliability but corruption into the domination mode. The economy runs on love or doesn't run (D&C 121:36). Nearly universal instrument failure would explain much of salvation history.

### Contradiction 3: The Grief of God
- **Source:** Round 3 synthesis, new contradiction #3
- **Tension:** Jacob 5:41 — the Master weeps. God chose an economy He knew would cause Him grief, because the alternative (compulsion) would destroy agency. What does it mean theologically that God's saving strategy is the most loving AND the most painful possible arrangement?
- **Why it matters:** Connects the economy of salvation to the Atonement — the cost God bears for running this particular economy.

### Direction C (Carried Forward): When Knowledge Blocks Knowing
- **Source:** Satan problem (Round 1, partially addressed)
- **Tension:** Can correct theology obstruct participatory knowing? Does the propositional substitute for the participatory?
- **Why it matters:** Would sharpen the distinction between propositional and participatory knowledge with real scriptural cases.
