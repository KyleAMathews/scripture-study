# The Cycle Is Governed by Divine Calibration

## a. Ontological Claim: Grace Is the Load-Bearing Structure

The Round 1 synthesis established that faith is participatory knowledge of God — a cycle of "receive, decide, act, receive again." Now the question: what makes this cycle *run*? What is its engine, its animating force?

The answer is not complicated. The cycle runs on grace. Not grace as a supplement bolted onto the side of human effort. Grace as the active, responsive, calibrated participation of God in the knowing-relationship — the thing without which the human side has nothing to respond to, nothing to act on, and nowhere to go.

Start with the ontological ground. Moroni 7:16: "The Spirit of Christ is given to every man, that he may know good from evil." Before any human has done anything — before the first act, the first prayer, the first seed planted — God has already moved. The light of Christ is not earned. It is *given*. It is the baseline condition of mortal existence. Every human being who has ever drawn breath arrived into a world already saturated with divine initiative. The cycle does not begin with a human choice. It begins with a gift.

Mosiah 3:19 sharpens this: "The natural man is an enemy to God... unless he yields to the enticings of the Holy Spirit." The verb assigned to the Spirit is *entice*. That is an active verb — drawing, inviting, initiating. The verb assigned to the human is *yield*. That is a receptive verb — opening, allowing, not-resisting. The grammatical structure of the atonement itself places divine action first and human response second. God entices. We yield. The enticing is the engine. The yielding is the necessary condition for the engine to do its work.

D&C 50:24: "He that receiveth light, and continueth in God, receiveth more light; and that light groweth brighter and brighter until the perfect day." Who is the subject doing the growing? The *light*. God's light grows brighter. The human receives. The human continues. But the light — the divine input — is what intensifies. The trajectory toward "the perfect day" is a trajectory of increasing divine disclosure, not increasing human effort.

And 2 Nephi 28:30, the verse the briefing assigns to the human-response camp, actually proves my case when read carefully: "Unto him that receiveth I will give more." Who is speaking? God. Who is acting? God — *giving*. The human receives. God gives more. The human's role is to not refuse the gift. The variation in outcome is real — some receive and some say "we have enough" — but the *active force producing the variation in input* is God's calibrated response. God gives more to those who receive. God withdraws from those who refuse. The human choice is binary: receive or refuse. The divine response is graduated, adaptive, personalized.

This is what Alma 12:9-11 describes: God grants "according to the heed and diligence which they give unto him." God *grants*. God reads the human's heed and diligence and *calibrates* the portion of the word accordingly. This is not a light switch left permanently on. This is a teacher who watches each student, assesses their readiness, and provides exactly the input they can absorb. The sophistication, the intelligence, the responsiveness — all of that is on God's side.

## b. The Opponent's Strongest Case — Stated Fairly, Then Destroyed

My opponent will argue something like this: "God provides the input, sure, but the engine of the cycle is human agency. D&C 58:26-28 says 'the power is in them, wherein they are agents unto themselves.' Alma 32 puts all the verbs of action on the human — plant, nourish, be patient, be diligent. If the tree withers, it's because *you* neglected it, not because God withheld rain. The variation in outcome maps to the variation in human response. Grace is the constant; agency is the variable."

This is a clean argument. It is also wrong at the root.

Here is what D&C 58:26-28 actually says. "It is not meet that I should command in all things; for he that is compelled in all things, the same is a slothful and not a wise servant." This verse is not about the *source* of the cycle's power. It is about the *mode* of the human's engagement within the cycle. God is telling mature participants that they should not need micromanagement. This is a statement about the character of someone who already has deep participatory knowledge — someone so saturated in the relationship that they can act "of their own free will" in alignment with God's purposes. It presupposes that the person has already received enormous divine calibration. The brother of Jared inventing the stones is exactly this: a human acting creatively *because God has already given him so much participatory knowledge that he can operate with initiative*. The initiative is downstream of the grace. Take away the years of divine calibration that got the brother of Jared to that mountain, and he is just a man with rocks.

Alma 32 is even more revealing. Yes, the human plants, nourishes, and tends. But what is the seed? Alma 32:28 — "it beginneth to enlarge my soul; it beginneth to enlighten my understanding; it beginneth to be delicious to me." The *seed does the enlarging*. The seed does the enlightening. The human plants, but the growth, the swelling, the light, the deliciousness — all of that is the seed's own life force manifesting. The human provides conditions. The seed provides the life. And what is the seed? The word of God. Divine input. The human is a gardener, not a creator. Remove the seed and no amount of human nourishment produces anything.

## c. Specific Diagnosis: Why the Human-Driven View Fails

The human-driven view, pressed to its logical conclusion, is Pelagianism. If human agency is the engine — if the variation in outcome is fundamentally a function of human effort, human diligence, human heed — then what do you need God for? A Pelagian universe has God as an inert resource: always available, never varying, a spiritual vending machine that dispenses light when you insert the coin of effort. The human becomes the protagonist. The human generates. The human determines. God is furniture.

This is not the God of scripture. The God of scripture *searches* — "the Son of Man is come to seek and to save that which was lost" (Luke 19:10). He *pursues* — Enos prays and the voice comes, not as a standardized broadcast but as a personal response to a specific man wrestling in specific anguish. He *adapts* — the brother of Jared gets rebuked for three hours for not praying, then gets the most intimate theophany in all of scripture. God calibrates the rebuke AND the reward to the specific person. He *initiates* — Saul on the Damascus road did not seek God. God knocked him off his horse.

If the human-driven view were correct, Saul's conversion would be incoherent. A man actively persecuting the church cannot be the "engine" of his own participatory cycle. But God broke in. God disrupted. God *initiated a new cycle* that Saul had not started and did not deserve. The human-driven model has no room for this. The grace-driven model predicts it.

## d. The Deeper Principle at Stake

What is really at stake is the nature of God. Is God a person or a principle? A principle provides consistent output regardless of who engages it. Gravity doesn't calibrate. Gravity doesn't meet you where you are. If God's role in the participatory cycle is to provide constant light that humans filter through their choices, then God is functionally a law of nature — the spiritual equivalent of a gravitational field.

But the entire theology of the Restoration insists God is a person. A person *responds*. A person reads the room. A person gives Nephi a different answer than Laman because Nephi is a different person with different readiness — and God knows the difference. A personal God does not provide uniform input. A personal God calibrates.

The "line upon line, precept upon precept" of 2 Nephi 28:30 is not a description of human incremental learning. It is a description of divine *pedagogy* — God teaching in a structured, adaptive sequence that matches the student's capacity. The teacher determines the sequence. The teacher determines when to introduce the next precept. The teacher is doing the sophisticated intellectual and relational work. The student's job is to show up and receive.

## e. Push to the Extreme

Take this all the way. If God were to withdraw from the cycle entirely — if the light of Christ were extinguished, if the Spirit ceased to entice, if grace were suspended — what would happen? The human side would have nothing. Not diminished capacity. *Nothing*. No seed to plant. No light to receive. No swelling, no enlarging, no deliciousness. Human agency without divine input is a gardener standing over bare dirt with no seeds, no water, and no sun. You can dig all you want.

Now reverse it. If a human were entirely passive but God continued to entice, continued to pour out light, continued to calibrate — would anything happen? No. Because yielding is genuinely necessary. But notice the asymmetry. When God withdraws, the cycle *cannot exist*. When the human withdraws, the cycle *does not operate but could resume at any moment*, because God is still there, still enticing, still holding out the gift. The cycle is more robust to human failure than to divine withdrawal. That asymmetry tells you which side is load-bearing.

## f. Reasoning Skeleton

1. **The cycle requires input before response.** You cannot respond to nothing. (Moroni 7:16 — light is *given* before any human act.)
2. **The input is calibrated, not constant.** God provides more light to those who receive, less to those who harden. (Alma 12:9-11, D&C 50:24.) This is active divine intelligence, not passive broadcast.
3. **The human role is receptive, not generative.** Scripture's verbs: yield, receive, hearken, give heed. Not: create, generate, produce, initiate. (Mosiah 3:19.)
4. **Human agency without grace is Pelagianism.** If the human is the engine, God is furniture. The Restoration rejects this. (Saul's conversion, Enos's prayer, the brother of Jared's rebuke.)
5. **Grace without human response is inoperative but still present.** Human failure without divine response is annihilation. The asymmetry reveals the load-bearing element.
6. **Therefore: Grace — the active, responsive, calibrated participation of God — is the sufficient condition for the cycle. Human response is the necessary condition. The cycle is governed by divine calibration.**

The seed does the growing. We just have to not pull it up.
