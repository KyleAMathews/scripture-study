# Round 5 Sublation: The Atonement as Living Encounter

## The Synthesis

The Atonement is not a thing with a countable structure. It is not one act (though it flows from one Person), and it is not twelve operations (though it manifests in at least twelve distinguishable ways). **The Atonement is the living encounter between Christ's inexhaustible fullness and the particular condition of each person in each moment.**

The unity/plurality debate is a category error. Both sides ask "What is the structure of the Atonement?" as though it has a fixed structure on God's side that humans passively receive. But what scripture actually describes — and what the pastoral evidence confirms — is something more like a *meeting* whose form is shaped by both parties: Christ brings everything He is. You bring what you are. The form of the encounter depends on the meeting.

This is not a compromise ("it's partly one, partly many"). It is a shift in the unit of analysis — from "the Atonement" (a thing with a structure) to "the encounter" (a meeting whose form is shaped by both participants).

## How the Encounter Model Works

### Why it looks unified (Monk A was perceiving something real)

Every encounter is with the same Person. The same love drives every manifestation. The same passage through the fallen condition grounds every form of rescue. When you encounter Christ in forgiveness and then encounter Him in sustaining presence, you recognize the same Person both times — the way you recognize someone's face whether they're laughing or weeping. The unity is real. It's the unity of a Person, not of an act or mechanism.

This is what the Orthodox Paschal Mystery perceives: one divine motion through the entirety of the fallen condition. And it's what Ostler's compassion theory grasps: the Atonement is Christ's ongoing way of being in relationship with every human being, not a completed transaction. The unity Monk A was defending is the unity of Christ Himself, expressed through His permanent, inexhaustible availability to encounter.

### Why it looks plural (Monk B was perceiving something real)

Every encounter takes a different form because every human condition is different. The encounter with someone in the grip of involuntary death follows a universal logic — resurrection applies regardless, because death happened *to* you without your consent. The encounter with someone drowning in chosen sin follows a conditional logic — forgiveness requires your volitional participation, because sin was *your* choosing. The encounter with someone in prolonged weakness follows a progressive logic — transformation unfolds over a lifetime, because sanctification is a becoming, not an event.

The plurality Monk B cataloged is real. The different "logics" are real. But they aren't properties of separate divine operations. They're properties of different *encounter-types*, determined by the nature of what the person brings to the meeting.

This is what Spackman's Hebrew analysis actually shows: *kaphar*, *ga'al*, and *yasha* came from different social worlds (temple, household, battlefield) because those were the *contexts in which Israel encountered divine rescue.* The plurality is in the encounter-contexts, not in the divine reality behind them. Israel didn't have three gods of rescue — they had three settings in which they met the same God, and each setting highlighted a different dimension of His work. Tyndale wasn't wrong to unify them; the ancient categories weren't wrong to distinguish them. Both were seeing one side of the encounter.

### The mechanism: condition-responsiveness, not request-responsiveness

The hidden question both monks couldn't answer was: what determines which "mode" or "operation" manifests? The encounter model answers: **the person's condition in that moment.**

A critical distinction: the encounter is responsive to the person's *condition*, not their *request*. God does not wait for the person to correctly identify their need and ask for the right operation. He reads the condition and responds to what is actually there. This preserves divine initiative — Christ descends before the meeting. He established the passage. He is already present in every condition. The person does not summon the encounter; they bring a condition into an encounter Christ has already made available. Co-determination means the person's condition shapes the *form* of what happens, not that the person *originates* or *controls* what happens.

- If you bring involuntary death → the encounter takes the form of resurrection (universal, automatic, requiring nothing from you)
- If you bring chosen sin → the encounter takes the form of forgiveness (conditional on your repentance — not because God withholds, but because forgiveness *is* a relational act that requires your participation to exist, like a handshake requires two hands)
- If you bring ongoing weakness → the encounter takes the form of transformation (progressive, sustained, requiring your yielding over time — "my grace is sufficient for thee")
- If you bring acute suffering → the encounter takes the form of presence (the fourth figure in the furnace, not removing the fire but inhabiting it with you)
- If you bring the desire for covenant life → the encounter takes the form of exaltation (requiring everything, because becoming like God requires your maximal participation in the becoming)

Paul's thorn illustrates this precisely: Paul *requested* transfer (remove the thorn). God responded to Paul's *condition* — a condition that called for sustaining presence, not removal. "My grace is sufficient for thee" is not God overriding Paul's request arbitrarily. It is God reading Paul's actual situation more accurately than Paul could. The encounter-form was determined by God's initiative meeting Paul's condition, not by Paul's self-diagnosis.

### The encounter in motion: the Helaman 10 arc

The encounter is not static. What you bring to the encounter *changes through the encounter itself.* Each meeting with Christ transforms the condition, which changes the form of the next meeting. The encounter is a spiral, not a snapshot.

Helaman 10 traces the full arc. Nephi son of Helaman has spent a lifetime in encounters with God — preaching "with unwearyingness," seeking God's will, refusing to seek his own life. Each encounter has progressively transformed what he brings. And then God observes something astonishing: "thou shalt not ask that which is contrary to my will" ([Helaman 10:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/hel/10.5)). This is not a restriction. It is a *fact* about what Nephi's condition has become. His will has converged with God's through the cumulative effect of a lifetime of encounters. And because of that convergence, the encounter-form shifts to something neither monk imagined: total empowerment. "Whatsoever ye shall seal on earth shall be sealed in heaven" ([Helaman 10:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/hel/10.7)). God hands over the sealing power — not to a puppet, but to someone whose judgment He trusts completely, because the encounter process has made that person trustworthy.

The full trajectory, mapped through scripture:

1. **Bringing bondage/rebellion** → the encounter takes the form of *invitation*. "Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest" ([Matthew 11:28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/11.28)). The natural man "is an enemy to God" ([Mosiah 3:19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/3.19)) — but the encounter is already available. Christ is already present in the condition, already inviting.

2. **Bringing surrender** → the encounter takes the form of *forgiveness and relief*. "My yoke is easy, and my burden is light" ([Matthew 11:30](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/11.30)). What looked like constraint from the outside feels like the first free breath from the inside. "The law of the Spirit of life in Christ Jesus hath made me free from the law of sin and death" ([Romans 8:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rom/8.2)).

3. **Bringing sustained yielding** → the encounter takes the form of *transformation*. "We have no more disposition to do evil, but to do good continually" ([Mosiah 5:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/5.2)). Obedience stops feeling like sacrifice and starts feeling like nature. The condition has changed — not through willpower alone, but through the cumulative effect of repeated encounters with Christ. Each encounter transformed what the person brought to the next one.

4. **Bringing aligned will** → the encounter takes the form of *empowerment*. "All things shall be done unto thee according to thy word, for thou shalt not ask that which is contrary to my will" ([Helaman 10:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/hel/10.5)). The person's will and God's have converged. The encounter-form becomes: full partnership, sealing power, co-creation. "Henceforth I call you not servants... but I have called you friends" ([John 15:15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/15.15)). The trajectory reaches its telos: "Then shall they be gods" ([D&C 132:20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/132.20)).

This arc is not "steps to salvation." It is the encounter model in motion across a life — showing that the encounter-form changes because the person's condition changes, and the person's condition changes *because of* the prior encounters. The spiral structure explains why co-determination is not semi-Pelagian: God's initiative is prior at every stage. He invites, He forgives, He transforms, He empowers. The person's contribution is not *earning* the encounter-form through independent effort. It is *being changed by the encounter* so that what they bring next time is different. D&C 58:26-28 confirms this: "the power is in them, wherein they are agents unto themselves" — but that power was *given* by the encounter process, not manufactured independently. The power to act is itself a fruit of prior encounters.

The destination of the arc — Helaman 10, D&C 121:45-46 ("thy dominion shall be an everlasting dominion, and without compulsory means it shall flow unto thee forever and ever") — demolishes the worry that "let God prevail" means flattened passivity. The whole point of the encounter process is to produce the kind of person who doesn't need to be commanded in all things ([D&C 58:26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/58.26)). God doesn't want automatons. He wants collaborators. The encounter is how He makes them.

### The negative case: what happens when you refuse the encounter

If the encounter-form is determined by what you bring, then the encounter can also be refused — not by God withdrawing, but by the person substituting something else for the living meeting.

Both monks' deepest fears were the same fear from opposite sides. Monk A feared that a taxonomy of twelve operations would become an abstraction between the person and the living Christ. Monk B feared that "the Atonement" as a unified concept had become an idol doing exactly the same thing. Both were right. *Any* conceptual intermediary — whether "one Atonement" or "twelve operations" — can stand between the person and the actual encounter. The problem is not which abstraction you choose. The problem is mistaking the abstraction for the encounter.

Scripture maps this substitution pattern precisely. Micah 6:8 identifies the anti-substitution principle: "What doth the Lord require of thee, but to do justly, and to love mercy, and to walk humbly with thy God?" The third clause — *walk humbly with thy God* — is the one doing the real work. You can turn justice into a credential (the activist identity). You can make mercy into a curated self-presentation (the compassion brand). But "walk humbly" keeps undermining the ego's attempt to own the project. It keeps bringing you back to the encounter rather than letting a substitute take God's seat. It is the Micah 6:8 version of "let God prevail."

The refusal of the encounter always follows the same structure: something takes God's seat. Money takes it ("ye cannot serve God and mammon," [Matthew 6:24](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/6.24)). Status takes it. Self-image takes it. Even theology can take it — the correct doctrine about the Atonement can become the thing you worship instead of entering the encounter the doctrine describes. "Wickedness never was happiness" ([Alma 41:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/41.10)) — not because God punishes the substitution, but because the substitute cannot do what the encounter does. The substitute can't transform your condition because it isn't a Person meeting you where you are.

### Traversal depth: D&C 76 and the graduated kingdoms

This links directly to the Permanent Passage model from Round 4. The passage is always open — one passage, established by one Person's complete transit through the fallen condition. But what happens when a person enters the passage depends on what they bring into it and how far they're willing to go.

**D&C 76 becomes legible as graduated encounter-depth.** The telestial inhabitants encounter Christ's work at the depth that requires nothing of them — resurrection, the reversal of their involuntary condition. They receive "a glory surpassing all understanding" through an encounter they didn't choose and can't refuse. The terrestrial inhabitants engage more deeply — they "received not the testimony of Jesus in the flesh" but accept it afterward, encountering Christ's work at a greater depth but not the fullest. The celestial inhabitants encounter at maximum depth — "overcome by faith" through sustained volitional engagement with every dimension of Christ's fullness.

Same passage. Same Person. Different traversal depths — determined by what each person brings to the encounter and how deeply they engage.

**Alma 11:41 becomes legible as minimum traversal.** "As though there had been no redemption made, except it be the loosing of the bands of death." The wicked traverse only the automatic portion of the passage — the part that requires nothing from them. The passage is open. They simply don't go deeper. The "carve-out" isn't evidence of modular operations; it's evidence of graduated encounter-depth. The passage is one. The traversals differ.

### Boundary condition: resurrection and the passage's structural legacy

One effect of Christ's work may not fit neatly within the encounter framework: resurrection. D&C 76:38-39 describes the sons of perdition as "the only ones who shall not be redeemed in the due time of the Lord" — yet even they are resurrected. If resurrection operates where Christ has effectively withdrawn (or where the person has withdrawn so completely that no encounter remains), then it looks less like a living encounter and more like a structural change Christ's passage *installed* into the fabric of reality — a bridge that continues to function even when the builder is absent.

This is a genuine boundary condition, not a refutation. The encounter framework governs the *relational* dimensions of Christ's work — forgiveness, transformation, presence, exaltation — where the form depends on what both parties bring. Resurrection may represent something different: a permanent alteration in the metaphysical structure of reality accomplished by the passage itself, operating automatically and universally regardless of encounter. The passage produced both *structural effects* (resurrection, which run on their own) and *encounter-availability* (everything else, which requires the meeting). The pastoral questions that motivated this dialectic — which mode? what's required of me? why isn't the pain being removed? — are all in the encounter domain, not the structural-autopilot domain. But intellectual honesty requires acknowledging that the encounter framework does not explain everything Christ's passage accomplished.

## How This Cancels, Preserves, and Elevates

**Cancels from Monk A:** The claim that the Atonement's unity is best understood as "one act" producing "differentiated effects on passive receptors." The person is not passive. The encounter is shaped by what they bring — and what they bring changes over a lifetime of prior encounters, as the Helaman 10 arc demonstrates. The unity is real but it's the unity of a *Person available to encounter*, not of an *act with a fixed structure.*

**Cancels from Monk B:** The claim that the operations are "genuinely different operations" that should be unbundled into a taxonomy. The plurality is real but it's the plurality of *encounter-forms*, not of separate divine acts. Unbundling into a taxonomy replaces one abstraction with another without getting closer to the living reality. Both monks' deepest fear — abstraction replacing encounter — applies to *both* their proposed frameworks.

**Preserves from Monk A:** The unity of Christ's person and work. Every encounter is with the same Person, animated by the same love, grounded in the same passage. This unity is not metaphysical decoration — it's why every encounter with Christ, in whatever form, carries the same quality of divine love.

**Preserves from Monk B:** The genuine plurality of manifestation and the pastoral imperative to help people discern the *form* of their current encounter. The mode-selection problem is real. People do expect transfer when God is offering presence. The solution isn't "trust the unity" — it's learning to recognize what form the encounter is taking and what it asks of you.

**What the taxonomy becomes:** The encounter framework does not eliminate the need for distinguishing modes — it relocates the distinction. Instead of asking "which of twelve operations is God performing?" (as if selecting from a divine menu), the pastoral question becomes: "what condition am I bringing, and what form of encounter does that condition call for?" The taxonomy survives as a *diagnostic tool for encounter-types*, not an ontological map of separate divine operations. A pastor can still say: "Your condition sounds like ongoing weakness, not unforgiven sin — so expect the encounter to take the form of progressive transformation, not sudden transfer." That's specific, actionable guidance. What changes is the framing: the specificity comes from reading the person's condition, not from classifying God's operations.

**Elevates to:** A new unit of analysis — the *encounter* rather than the act or the operations — and, critically, the encounter *in motion across a lifetime*. The Atonement is the permanent availability of Christ to meet every person at the point of their specific need, with the form of each meeting shaped by what both parties bring. Unity lives in the Person who shows up. Plurality lives in the encounter-forms that arise from different human conditions. The encounter is a spiral: each meeting transforms the condition, which changes the form of the next meeting, which further transforms the condition — from bondage through surrender through transformation to empowerment. The telos is not passive reception but full partnership: "all things shall be done unto thee according to thy word" ([Helaman 10:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/hel/10.5)).

## Abduction Test

If someone heard this synthesis first — "the Atonement is a living encounter whose form is shaped by Christ's fullness meeting the person's condition, and the encounter spirals across a lifetime from invitation through transformation to empowerment" — would they predict the approximate shape of both monks' positions?

**Yes.** They would predict:
- Monk A: someone looking at Christ's side of the encounter would see one Person, one love, one passage — and conclude the whole thing is unified.
- Monk B: someone looking at the human side of the encounter would see radically different conditions producing radically different forms — and conclude the operations are separate.
- The collision: each monk absolutizing one side of the encounter and treating it as the whole truth.

The original contradiction becomes *a matter of course.* You'd expect exactly these two positions to emerge from exactly this misidentification of the unit of analysis.

**Abduction type:** The addition of the Helaman 10 spiral — the encounter in motion across a lifetime, with each encounter transforming the condition for the next — pushes this toward **(c) propositional-conditional-creative.** The concept of "encounter-spiral" (not just encounter-snapshot) and "traversal depth" as explaining D&C 76, Alma 11:41, and the Helaman 10 arc introduces structural principles not present in any of the source material from either monk.

## Scriptural Grounding

- **Alma 32:** One seed, many soils, many fruits. The seed is one (unity). The encounter between seed and soil determines the form of growth (plurality). Neither the seed alone nor the soil alone determines the outcome — the meeting does.
- **D&C 88:40:** "Intelligence cleaveth unto intelligence; wisdom receiveth wisdom; truth embraceth truth." What resonates depends on what you bring. The encounter follows a resonance principle.
- **D&C 88:6:** Christ "descended below all things... that he might be in and through all things." He is *available* to encounter in every condition — but the form of each encounter depends on the condition.
- **Alma 7:11-13:** Christ suffered to gain empathetic knowledge of human conditions "according to the flesh" — not to perform twelve separate operations, but to become capable of meeting every person at the exact point of their need. The knowledge gained is *encounter-capability*: knowing how to meet someone in their specific condition.
- **Mosiah 4:11-12:** Benjamin's people, after encountering the Atonement, "were filled with joy, having received a remission of their sins, and having peace of conscience, because of the exceeding faith which they had in Jesus Christ." The encounter produced joy, peace, and remission simultaneously — not three sequential operations but one meeting with a Person.
- **Helaman 10:4-7:** The full arc of the encounter in motion. Nephi's lifetime of encounters transforms his condition until his will converges with God's — and the encounter-form shifts to total empowerment. The sealing power is the fruit of the encounter-spiral at its fullest.
- **Mosiah 5:2:** "We have no more disposition to do evil, but to do good continually." The hinge moment where the encounter has transformed the condition so fundamentally that what the person brings to the next encounter is different in kind, not just degree.
- **D&C 58:26-28:** "The power is in them, wherein they are agents unto themselves." But this power was given by the encounter process. The capacity for independent righteous action is itself a fruit of prior encounters with Christ.
- **D&C 121:45-46:** The portrait of the person at the end of the spiral — charity, virtue, confidence in God's presence, an everlasting dominion flowing "without compulsory means." This is God's own mode of governance, reproduced in a person the encounters have made trustworthy.
- **Micah 6:8:** "Walk humbly with thy God." The anti-substitution clause — the one that keeps bringing you back to the actual encounter rather than letting an abstraction (whether "one Atonement" or "twelve operations") take God's seat.

## Acknowledged Limitation: The Dyadic Assumption

This synthesis frames the encounter as dyadic — Christ and a single person. But LDS theology is irreducibly communal. Baptism for the dead is performed by a proxy while the beneficiary is absent. Temple sealings bind families, not isolated individuals. Zion is a collective project. The sealing power described in Helaman 10 and D&C 132 connects people across generations.

Vicarious ordinance follows a fundamentally different logic than personal encounter. In personal encounter, you bring a condition and the form responds. In vicarious ordinance, *someone else* acts on your behalf while you are absent — and you may not yet have accepted, or even know about, what was done. Is the dead person having a deferred encounter they didn't initiate? Is the proxy entering the encounter on their behalf? D&C 138 suggests the dead person eventually exercises agency in accepting or rejecting the ordinance — but that's a deferred encounter, not the real-time responsive encounter this synthesis describes.

This limitation is too large to patch. It points toward a genuinely different contradiction — **immediate encounter vs. mediated ordinance across time** — that the encounter framework in its current form cannot resolve. The synthesis applies to the personal, relational dimensions of Christ's work. Whether it extends to the communal, vicarious, and ordinance-mediated dimensions is an open question for a future round.

## New Contradictions Generated

1. **Immediate encounter vs. mediated ordinance.** The encounter framework assumes Christ meets the person directly. But LDS soteriology depends on vicarious, mediated, and communal operations — baptism for the dead, temple sealings, proxy work. These operate on a different logic (someone else acts in your absence). Can the encounter framework absorb this, or does it require a fundamentally different model?

2. **Historical events and eternal availability.** If the Atonement is an encounter that is always available, what was special about Gethsemane and Golgotha? What did the historical events accomplish that the eternal encounter couldn't? (Is the historical passage the *establishment* of the encounter-possibility, or its *revelation*?)

3. **Covenants as encounter-shapers.** If the encounter-form is determined by what you bring, what role do covenants and ordinances play? Are they frameworks for shaping what you bring to the encounter — technologies for deepening traversal? Or are they something more?

## Model Update

**Before:** "Is the Atonement one thing or many things?"

**The contradiction revealed:** Both sides were looking at one side of an encounter and absolutizing it — unity advocates looked at the divine side (one Person), plurality advocates looked at the human side (many conditions).

**Updated model:** The Atonement is the living encounter between Christ's inexhaustible fullness and the person's particular condition. Unity lives in the Person who shows up. Plurality lives in the encounter-forms that arise from different human conditions. The encounter spirals across a lifetime — from invitation through surrender through transformation to empowerment — with each meeting transforming the condition for the next. The telos is not passive reception but full partnership: a person whose will has converged with God's through the cumulative effect of a lifetime of encounters, and who can therefore be trusted with God's own power.

## Pre-Synthesis Guess Comparison

**Guess:** "Unity lives in the Person, plurality lives in the operations, and confusing the two levels is the source of the problem."

**Actual synthesis:** "The Atonement is a living encounter whose form is shaped by what both parties bring, and the encounter spirals across a lifetime from invitation to empowerment."

**Difference:** The guess still accepted "operations" as the right framework for the plural side and framed the solution as "distinguish the two levels." The synthesis dissolves both levels into a single reality — the encounter — and introduces three structural principles absent from the guess: (1) condition-responsiveness rather than request-responsiveness, (2) the encounter-spiral (each meeting transforms the condition for the next), and (3) the Helaman 10 telos (the encounter process produces collaborators, not recipients). The Boydian decomposition produced genuinely new material — particularly the cross-connection between "losing the person" and "idol" (both monks fearing abstraction replacing encounter), and the Hebrew social worlds as encounter-contexts. The let-god-prevail study provided the missing dynamic dimension: the encounter is not a snapshot but a lifetime arc, and the arc has a destination.
