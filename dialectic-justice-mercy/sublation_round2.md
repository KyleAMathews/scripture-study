# Round 2 Sublation: The Atonement as Permanent Union

## Determinate Negation (Condensed)

### Where Monk A (Completed Event) Specifically Fails

1. **The "effects vs. cause" distinction is too clean.** Alma 7:12 says Christ suffered specifically TO GAIN the capacity to succor. The succoring is the PURPOSE. If the purpose of the event was to create an ongoing capacity, then the ongoing operation is not a "mere effect" — it is the POINT. You don't call digestion a "mere effect" of eating; digestion is why you eat.

2. **The "fruit is not the tree" metaphor backfires.** A tree that bore fruit once and died is a dead tree. A LIVING tree bears fruit continuously. If Christ is alive (both monks agree), then the Atonement is a living tree still bearing fruit. The metaphor, taken seriously, supports the ongoing-force reading.

3. **The Ira Glass challenge is sharp but misses the user's actual experience.** The user did not describe generating a vision through natural creative insight. He described being FILLED by an external force: "the Comforter filleth with hope and perfect love." Someone else is doing the filling — Christ, through the Spirit, presently and actively. The event-focused reading can't explain WHO is filling the young missionary and HOW, if the Filler finished His work 2000 years ago.

### Where Monk B (Ongoing Force) Specifically Fails

1. **The necessity problem remains unresolved.** If grace is fundamentally an ongoing developmental force, why did Christ have to bleed at every pore? What exactly does "opening a channel" mean structurally? If the channel had to be OPENED by a specific act of specific suffering, then the opening IS the load-bearing event, and the ongoing flow is downstream of it. Monk B can't explain the necessity of the cost.

2. **Monk A's hardest challenge stands:** "An Atonement that is primarily an ongoing developmental force could in principle operate without Christ." If it's fundamentally about filling with hope and enabling becoming, why couldn't God do that without the specific, personal, embodied agony? Why the blood? The ongoing-force reading struggles to explain why the PRICE was necessary.

3. **D&C 93:12-13 describes Christ's DEVELOPMENT, not the Atonement per se.** Monk A's "training vs. race" distinction has real force. Christ's growth from grace to grace was preparation FOR the Atonement. It doesn't prove the Atonement itself is processual.

### Shared Assumption

Both monks assume EVENT and PROCESS are different kinds of things — one must be primary, the other derivative. "The Atonement is either what happened then or what happens now." Neither considers that the Atonement might be a STRUCTURAL CHANGE that is simultaneously a historical event and an ongoing reality.

### The Hidden Question

Not "Is the Atonement about what happened THEN or what happens NOW?"

But: **What if the Atonement is a permanent change in the structure of reality — a change that happened at a specific moment but persists eternally, the way a marriage is both a wedding (event) and a union (ongoing reality)?**

## The Synthesis: The Atonement as Permanent Union of Divine and Human Experience

The Atonement is neither a completed transaction filed away in history nor an ongoing process untethered from a specific act. It is the **permanent union of divine and human experience in the person of Christ** — a structural change in reality that happened at a specific historical moment and persists as an ongoing, living reality.

### The Analogy: Wedding and Marriage

A marriage is both an EVENT (the wedding) and an ONGOING REALITY (the daily life of the union). You cannot say the wedding is "primarily" what marriage is — the years of shared life ARE the marriage. But you cannot say the wedding doesn't matter — without that specific moment of covenant and commitment, the ongoing reality doesn't exist. The wedding exists FOR the marriage. The marriage is the point of the wedding. They are two faces of a single structural change: two persons permanently united.

The Atonement is analogous. Gethsemane and Calvary were the "wedding" — the specific, unrepeatable, infinitely costly moment when divine nature and human experience were **fully united in one person.** Christ descended below all things. He experienced every pain, every temptation, every infirmity, every grief (Alma 7:11-12). This was not abstract knowledge — it was knowledge "according to the flesh," experiential, embodied, total.

The ongoing grace — the succoring, the filling with hope and perfect love, the making of weak things strong, the daily presence of a living Christ who stands at the door and knocks — is the "marriage." It is the daily reality that flows from that union. Christ can succor NOW because He entered THEN. He knows how to help you with your specific Wednesday afternoon discouragement because He experienced it "according to the flesh" in Gethsemane. The event created a permanent capacity. The capacity operates continuously. They are not two things. They are one reality with a historical face and an eternal face.

### Why This Resolves Both Failures

**It preserves the event's necessity and cost (Monk A's core insight).** The "wedding" had to happen. Without the specific, historical, embodied descent below all things, Christ would not have the experiential knowledge "according to the flesh" that enables Him to succor. You cannot succor what you have not entered into. Alma 34:10-14 is right: the sacrifice had to be infinite and eternal because the union had to be total — Christ had to experience EVERYTHING so that no human suffering would be outside His experiential knowledge. "It is finished" is true — the descent is complete. He has been everywhere. There is no pain He hasn't felt, no temptation He hasn't faced, no depth He hasn't reached. That completeness is what makes the ongoing operation possible.

**It preserves the ongoing operation as more than "mere effect" (Monk B's core insight).** The succoring, the filling with hope, the making of weak things strong — these are not downstream effects of a past event. They are the POINT of the event. They are the marriage for which the wedding existed. Christ suffered "that he may know according to the flesh how to succor his people" (Alma 7:12) — the succoring IS the purpose. The living Christ who stands at the door and knocks is not distributing benefits from a completed transaction. He is living out the ongoing reality of a permanent union with human experience. He knows your pain because He entered it. He can fill you with hope because He is present with you in it.

**It explains the Ira Glass problem (Monk A's sharpest challenge).** The user's zone conference experience was not generic inspiration. It was the operation of a SPECIFIC PERSON who had SPECIFICALLY ENTERED INTO the full range of human experience — including the experience of being a shy, awkward missionary struggling with a new language. Christ could fill that young man with a "dizzying perspective on what we can become" not because He is generically inspiring but because He has been where that young man is. The hope is grounded in a Person who knows you "according to the flesh" — not in an abstract vision. Any inspiring vision can give you "good taste." Only a person who has been where you are and knows the way out can fill you with the specific hope that YOU can make it. That specificity — the experiential knowledge gained through the descent — is what makes Christ's Atonement irreplaceable.

**It explains why "grace for grace" (D&C 93:12-13) is relevant but not in the way either monk claimed.** Christ's growth "from grace to grace" was not merely training for the event (Monk A) nor proof that the Atonement is processual (Monk B). It was Christ's own experience of the developmental pattern He would later enable for everyone else. He went first. He walked the path of becoming — from grace to grace, not receiving a fulness at the first — so that He could be the GUIDE for everyone who follows. The ongoing Atonement is Christ guiding you through the same developmental process He Himself experienced, with full experiential knowledge of what each step feels like.

### Connection to Round 1

Round 1 established that justice and mercy are both projections of a single reality — perfect love. Round 2 adds: **the Atonement is the moment when that perfect love permanently entered into the human condition, creating an ongoing union that enables the developmental process of becoming.**

The "demands of justice" (round 1) are what perfect love looks like to the one in separation. The "ongoing grace" (round 2) is what perfect love looks like to the one in the process of becoming. The Atonement is the structural event that made ongoing grace possible — not by paying a debt but by creating a permanent bridge between divine nature and human experience.

The user's lived theology is confirmed and deepened:
- The Atonement gives vision of what you can become — YES, because Christ has walked the path and can show you the way
- Vision creates hope — YES, because the hope is grounded in a Person who knows your specific situation "according to the flesh"
- Hope sustains the work of transformation — YES, because Christ is actively, presently succoring you through the process He Himself experienced
- "The Comforter filleth with hope and perfect love" — YES, and the "perfect love" is the love of a Person who has entered permanently into your condition, not an abstract force

## Model Update

- **Before (Round 1):** Justice and mercy are projections of perfect love. The Atonement is what perfect love does when it encounters suffering.
- **After (Round 2):** The Atonement is the permanent union of divine and human experience — the moment when perfect love fully entered the human condition, creating an ongoing capacity to succor, guide, and transform. The historical event (Gethsemane/Calvary) and the ongoing operation (grace, hope, becoming) are not two different things but the wedding and the marriage of the same structural reality. Christ suffered to ENTER IN, and He continues to operate because the entry is permanent.
- **Because:** The event-only reading can't explain why a living Christ continues to actively succor (Alma 7:12) if the work is "finished." The ongoing-force reading can't explain why the specific historical suffering was necessary if grace is a general developmental process. But if the event CREATED a permanent union — Christ entering into the full range of human experience — then both the event's necessity and the ongoing operation's reality are explained. The event had to happen because you can't succor what you haven't entered into. The ongoing operation is real because the entry is permanent.

## New Contradictions

1. **The individual specificity problem:** If Christ experienced "all" human suffering in Gethsemane, did He experience YOUR specific suffering? Is the union generic (all suffering) or particular (each person's unique pain)? Alma 7:11-12 seems to claim particularity. What does that mean for a single being experiencing billions of unique lives?

2. **The completed-becoming problem:** If salvation is a process of becoming, does it ever complete? Is there a moment when you ARE the new creature, or is becoming infinite? "Grace for grace... until he received a fulness" — Christ's process had an endpoint. Does ours?

3. **The pedagogy-practice gap:** If this synthesis is correct — that the Atonement is a living union, not a transaction — then the transactional language used in most LDS worship (paying debts, settling accounts, worthiness checklists) is not just simplification but potential DISTORTION. What would Atonement-centered practice look like if built on the union model rather than the transaction model?
