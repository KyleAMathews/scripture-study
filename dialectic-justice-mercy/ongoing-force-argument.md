# The Sun Is Still Shining: Why the Atonement Is a Living Force, Not a Historical Artifact

## The Ontological Claim

Christ is alive. This is not a theological footnote. It is the single most important claim in all of Christianity, and its implications are systematically under-read. A living Christ does not merely *remember* Gethsemane. He *operates* from it -- continuously, presently, without interruption.

Listen to the tenses. "Behold, I stand at the door, and knock" ([Revelation 3:20](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/3.20)) -- present tense, ongoing action. "My grace is sufficient for thee" ([2 Corinthians 12:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/2-cor/12.9)) -- present tense, active now, not merely available as a stored deposit. "As often as my people repent will I forgive them their trespasses" ([Mosiah 26:30](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/26.30)) -- unlimited, unbounded, explicitly refusing the idea that forgiveness is a finite resource dispensed from a historical reserve. "He ever liveth to make intercession for them" ([Hebrews 7:25](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/7.25)) -- ever liveth. Not "once lived and did something." Ever liveth, and the living IS the intercession.

"Listen to him who is the advocate with the Father, who is pleading your cause before him" ([D&C 45:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/45.3)) -- *pleading*, present participle. Not "who pled." Not "who filed the brief two thousand years ago." Pleading. Now. Today. The advocacy is ongoing because the Atonement is ongoing.

The Atonement is not a debt paid at a single historical moment. It is a sun that rose and continues to shine. Gethsemane was the sunrise -- the supreme, unrepeatable, singular moment when perfect love fully descended below all things and opened an infinite channel between divine nature and human experience. But the sun is still shining. Every act of grace, every filling with hope, every moment of succoring is daylight that flows from that sunrise. To treat the Atonement as primarily a completed event is like saying "sunrise happened" and then closing the blinds and studying the astronomy of dawn while ignoring the light flooding your room.

## The Opposing Case, Stated Fairly

I understand the completed-event argument, and I will not pretend it is trivial. Its advocates point to some of the most specific theological claims in all of scripture.

Amulek declares that there must be "a great and last sacrifice; yea, not a sacrifice of man, neither of beast... but it must be an infinite and eternal sacrifice" ([Alma 34:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/34.10)). The word "last" signals finality. The entire Mosaic system of repeated, ongoing animal sacrifice pointed toward a single culminating event that would end the need for repetition. Christ Himself declared "It is finished" on the cross. The Atonement "satisfied the demands of justice" -- past tense, accomplished, done. If the Atonement is primarily an ongoing process rather than a completed act, what exactly did Christ *accomplish* in Gethsemane and on Calvary? Why was the specific historical suffering necessary if grace was already flowing and would continue to flow regardless? The completed-event reading argues that without the anchor of a specific, unrepeatable, historically located sacrifice, the "ongoing force" reading dissolves into generic spiritual self-improvement -- nice ideas about hope and growth that any philosophy could offer, unmoored from the specific Person who descended below all things at a specific moment in history.

This is the strongest version of the case. I take it seriously. And it mistakes the channel for the moment the channel was opened.

## Where the Completed-Event Reading Fails

The completed-event reading fails at precisely the point where scripture should confirm it most -- in its own description of what Christ's suffering *achieved*.

Consider the most revelatory passage about the Atonement's purpose in all of LDS scripture: "He will take upon him their infirmities, that his bowels may be filled with mercy, according to the flesh, that he may know according to the flesh how to succor his people according to their infirmities" ([Alma 7:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/7.12)). Read this with painful precision. Alma does not say Christ suffered *to pay a debt*. He says Christ suffered *to gain the capacity to succor*. The suffering was not the terminal point. It was the means by which Christ acquired -- "according to the flesh" -- the experiential knowledge of human pain that enables Him to help us *now*. The word "succor" means to run to the aid of. It is present-tense, ongoing, and relational. It describes an *ability* Christ gained through His suffering that He *continues to exercise*. The event created a capacity. The capacity operates continuously.

Hebrews confirms this with devastating clarity: "For in that he himself hath suffered being tempted, he is able to succour them that are tempted" ([Hebrews 2:18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/2.18)). Hath suffered -- past tense. Is able to succour -- present tense. The past event generated a present, ongoing ability. The suffering was not the point. The succoring IS the point. The suffering was the price of admission into full experiential solidarity with humanity; the succoring is the ongoing work that solidarity enables.

Now consider the most destabilizing passage for the completed-event reading: D&C 93:12-14. "And I, John, saw that he received not of the fulness at the first, but received grace for grace; And he received not of the fulness at first, but continued from grace to grace, until he received a fulness" ([D&C 93:12-13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/93.12-13)). This is a description of *Christ's own spiritual development*. Even Christ -- the Author of the Atonement -- did not arrive at fullness in a single moment. He "continued from grace to grace." His progression was processual, developmental, ongoing. And this is not merely biographical trivia. D&C 93:19-20 makes it prescriptive: "you shall receive grace for grace." The pattern of Christ's own becoming IS the pattern of the Atonement's operation in every human life. Grace for grace. Not grace as a one-time deposit but grace as a continuous flow that meets you where you are and draws you further.

The completed-event reading cannot explain why the scriptures describe the Atonement's purpose in developmental terms, why Christ's own relationship to grace was processual, and why the same processual pattern is prescribed for all of us. If the Atonement were primarily a historical event, its descriptive language would be transactional: payment, satisfaction, completion. Instead, its deepest descriptive language is developmental: succoring, growing from grace to grace, making weak things strong over time.

## What Is at Stake

Here is where this becomes personal, because there is a man who stood in front of his ward in 2018 and told the truth about what the Atonement actually does in a human life.

He described the expectation the completed-event reading produces: that the Atonement is a one-time magical removal of problems. You accept Christ, and you are fixed. He described his 20s -- the confusion, the discouragement, the persistent problems that did not disappear. He almost lost his faith because the transactional model promised something the Atonement was never designed to deliver: instant completion.

Then he described what the Atonement actually does. At a zone conference on his mission, the Spirit filled him with an overwhelming vision of what he could become. He walked out "the exact same person that walked in." Still bad at Tagalog. Still awkward. Still him. But now filled with hope. And that hope -- that "dizzying perspective on what we can become" -- sustained the *work* of transformation over years and decades. The Atonement did not fix him. It filled him with vision and love that made the fixing possible. The transformation required his own sustained effort, sustained by the ongoing flow of grace.

This is not a nice anecdote. This is a precise description of how the Atonement operates, confirmed by the very scripture he cited: "the Comforter filleth with hope and perfect love, which love endureth by diligence unto prayer, until the end shall come" ([Moroni 8:26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/moro/8.26)). Look at the architecture of that verse. The Comforter *filleth* -- present tense, ongoing. With hope and perfect love -- not with a completed transaction but with living emotional and spiritual realities. Which love *endureth by diligence* -- endurance, sustained effort, the long haul. *Unto prayer* -- the ongoing practice of relationship. *Until the end shall come* -- this is a process that takes a lifetime.

This is the Atonement. Not a single historical deposit but a continuous filling with hope and love that sustains the lifelong work of becoming. The historical event -- Gethsemane, Calvary, the empty tomb -- was the moment when the channel was opened, when perfect love fully entered the human condition. But the love is still entering. The succoring is still happening. The grace is still flowing, grace for grace, from wherever you are toward wherever God knows you can be.

"My grace is sufficient for all men that humble themselves before me; for if they humble themselves before me, and have faith in me, then will I make weak things become strong unto them" ([Ether 12:27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/ether/12.27)). "Will I make" -- future tense, progressive, ongoing. Not "have I already made." The making-strong is a process. It takes time. It takes the continuous application of grace to weakness. It takes a living Christ who is actively, presently, continuously at work in you -- not a Christ who finished the work two thousand years ago and left you a receipt.

The completed-event reading sets people up for the exact crisis that man described in his talk. It tells them the Atonement is a thing that *happened*, and then when their lives do not instantly transform, they conclude it did not happen *to them*. The developmental reading matches lived experience: the Atonement is a force that fills you with hope and love today, that sustains the hard daily work of becoming, that meets you in your weakness this afternoon and begins the slow process of making it into strength. It is less like a surgery that happened once and more like the bloodstream that sustains you every moment of every day.

## The Extreme Claim

I will go where this leads. The Atonement is not merely ongoing in its *effects*. It is ongoing in its *operation*. Christ is not a surgeon who performed an operation two thousand years ago whose benefits we continue to receive. He is an active, living, present being who is *right now* -- this moment, as these words are read -- engaged in the work of succoring, interceding, filling with hope, making weak things strong. The sacrament is not a memorial of a dead event. It is a weekly re-entry into a *living* Atonement, a renewal of a relationship with a Person who is presently active, presently knocking, presently offering grace for grace.

Gethsemane was the moment when infinite love proved the full extent of its commitment by descending below all things. That moment was necessary, unrepeatable, and infinitely costly. I do not diminish it for a moment. But the love that drove that descent did not stop descending on the morning of the resurrection. It is still descending -- into your grief, your inadequacy, your Wednesday afternoon discouragement, your persistent weakness that you have prayed about for twenty years. "His compassions fail not. They are new every morning" ([Lamentations 3:22-23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/lam/3.22-23)). New. Every. Morning. Not remembered every morning from a historical archive. *New*. The Atonement is being performed on you today.

To reduce this to "something that happened once, long ago, that we remember and respond to" is to take the most powerful force in the universe -- a living God's present, active, ongoing love -- and turn it into a museum exhibit. It is to stand in full daylight and insist that the only important thing about the sun is the moment it came over the horizon.

The sun rose in Gethsemane. It is still shining. Open your eyes.
