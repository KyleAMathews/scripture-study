# Round 4 Sublation: The Atonement as Permanent Passage

## Why This Matters

This is not an abstract theological question. It is the question that determined whether a young man in his 20s kept or lost his faith.

The substitution model — Christ bore it so you don't have to — sets people up for a specific crisis: when you're still struggling, still carrying the Wednesday afternoon discouragement, still wrestling the same weakness after twenty years of prayer, the substitution model says something went wrong. The weight should have transferred. Why is it still here? Either the Atonement didn't work, or you didn't apply it correctly. This is the exact crisis described in the user's 2018 talk.

The entry model — Christ entered your condition and is present in it — explains the zone conference experience perfectly. Nothing was removed. Someone arrived. The young missionary walked out "the exact same person that walked in" — but filled with hope and love. That presence sustained years of hard work. But this model struggles to explain why the scriptures promise genuine RELIEF — actual lifting of burdens, blotting out of transgressions, "that they might not suffer."

The pastoral reality is that BOTH experiences are real. People experience genuine removal of guilt (substitution). People also experience sustaining presence through suffering that is NOT removed (entry). Both happen. Both are scriptural. A theology that only accounts for one produces broken people or confused people. This dialectic matters because it determines what you expect when you pray.

## The Synthesis

The Atonement is neither pure entry (Christ arriving at our location) nor pure substitution (weight transferring from us to Christ). It is the **creation of a permanent passage between divine nature and the full scope of fallen human experience** — a passage that did not exist before Gethsemane and that now carries traffic in both directions.

### Determinate Negation (Condensed)

**Where Monk A (Entry) specifically fails:** Cannot explain genuine relief. D&C 19:15-17 is a strict either/or — either Christ suffers it or you do. Entry produces co-presence, not relief. Two people in a fire are both burning. And Alma 7:13 (the verse Monk A never cites) states the purpose as "blotting out transgressions" — removal, not comprehension. At its extreme, the entry reading makes the Atonement a visit. Empathy does not move weight.

**Where Monk B (Substitution) specifically fails:** Cannot explain why Christ needed to KNOW (Alma 7:12 — "that he may know according to the flesh"). Cannot explain transformation — the dominant effect on people is metamorphosis (new creature, born again), not subtraction (lighter load). Cannot explain why Christ was changed (Hebrews 5:8-9 — "made perfect through suffering"). Cannot explain the permanent Incarnation — if the point is the transaction, why does Christ keep the body, the nail prints? Once the price is paid, the entry should be unnecessary.

**The shared assumption:** Both assume the Atonement has a single direction of flow — either divine-toward-human (entry) or human-toward-divine (substitution). Neither considers bidirectional flow through permanent infrastructure.

**The hidden question:** What if the Atonement CHANGED THE TOPOLOGY — creating a permanent connection between divine nature and human experience through which traffic flows both ways?

### The Core Insight: Infrastructure, Not a Single Act

Before the Atonement, every human being bore their own weight in isolation. There was no structural connection between divine nature and the depths of human fallenness. Death was universal and final. Sin's consequences were inescapable. The 2 Nephi 9 counterfactual was the default: bodies rotting forever, spirits captured by the adversary forever. Each person a closed circuit with no ground.

Christ's total entry into the fallen condition — descending below all things, experiencing every death, every sin, every grief, every separation — did not merely visit the condition or merely bear its weight. It **created a permanent structural connection** between divine nature and every dimension of human experience, including its worst. A passage that did not previously exist now exists. And it remains open because Christ's entry was permanent — He retains the resurrected body, the nail prints, the experiential knowledge. He did not visit and leave. He opened a passage and stayed in it.

### Bidirectional Flow

The passage carries traffic both ways. This is the key insight neither monk grasped.

**From human to divine (the substitutionary flow):** Sin, suffering, and weight flow through the passage from human beings to Christ. This is what Isaiah 53 describes — "he hath borne our griefs, and carried our sorrows" ([Isaiah 53:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/53.4)). This is what D&C 19:16 describes — Christ suffered "that they might not suffer if they would repent." The weight genuinely transfers. The repentant are genuinely relieved. Every transfer verb in the scriptural witness is accurate: borne, carried, laid on, taken upon himself. Weight moves.

**From divine to human (the transformative flow):** Grace, hope, divine nature, and the capacity for becoming flow through the passage from Christ to human beings. This is what Moroni 8:26 describes — "the Comforter filleth with hope and perfect love." This is what Ether 12:27 describes — "I make weak things become strong." This is what 2 Peter 1:4 describes — becoming "partakers of the divine nature." The human being is not just relieved of weight. They are filled with something. They are transformed — born again, new creature, mighty change of heart. The dominant scriptural description of the Atonement's effect is not subtraction but metamorphosis — and metamorphosis requires something flowing IN, not just something flowing OUT.

**2 Corinthians 5:21 is the clearest statement of bidirectional flow:** "He hath made him to be sin for us, who knew no sin; that we might be made the righteousness of God in him." Sin flows from us to Christ — He is "made sin." Righteousness flows from Christ to us — we are "made the righteousness of God." Two directions. One connection. The great exchange.

### Multiple Modes of the Passage

The Boydian decomposition revealed something the original synthesis missed: the passage doesn't always operate in the same mode.

**Transfer mode:** Sometimes the passage moves weight from human to Christ. The repentant experience genuine relief — guilt lifted, burden removed, "that they might not suffer." This is the substitutionary dimension.

**Presence mode:** Sometimes the passage does NOT transfer weight but instead carries sustaining grace. Paul's thorn in the flesh — "My grace is sufficient for thee" ([2 Corinthians 12:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/2-cor/12.9)). The thorn remained. The weight was not moved. Instead, grace flowed through the passage — not removing the condition but sustaining the sufferer within it. D&C 122 operates in this mode: God's answer to Joseph's agonies is not "I have removed these things" but "The Son of Man hath descended below them all. Art thou greater than he?" The comfort is not exemption. It is the knowledge that the passage reaches here — that even in this darkness, you are connected to a divine being who has already been where you are.

**Transformation mode:** Sometimes the passage doesn't remove weight or just sustain — it CHANGES the person. "I make weak things become strong" ([Ether 12:27](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/ether/12.27)). The weakness is not removed. It is transmuted into strength. The passage carries divine nature into the specific weakness and alchemizes it. This is the "grace for grace" process — progressive transformation over a lifetime, weakness by weakness, "until he received a fulness."

**This resolves the pastoral crisis.** When the young man in his 20s prayed and his problems didn't vanish, the substitution model said the Atonement had failed. But the passage has multiple modes. Sometimes weight transfers (and you experience relief). Sometimes grace sustains (and you experience presence in ongoing struggle). Sometimes transformation happens (and your weakness slowly becomes strength over years). All three are the Atonement working. All three flow through the same passage. The question is never "Is the Atonement working?" The question is "Which mode is the passage operating in right now?" — and the answer might be different for different struggles in the same life at the same time.

The zone conference experience was the passage operating in transformation mode: divine nature flowing through the connection, filling the missionary with vision and hope, beginning the slow process of changing who he was. Nothing was removed. Something was added. The Atonement was working exactly as designed — just not in the mode the transactional model had led him to expect.

### The Covenant/Ordinance System as Connection Protocol

The passage exists universally (Christ's entry was total — it reaches every dimension of human experience). But individual access requires connection through the covenant and ordinance system:

- **Baptism:** The individual enters the passage — "buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead, even so we also should walk in newness of life" ([Romans 6:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rom/6.4)). Not a bureaucratic membership ritual but an ontological act — the person enters the same death-and-resurrection passage Christ opened.
- **Repentance:** Releasing weight into the passage — activating the transfer mode. This is why repentance feels like relief: weight actually moves through the connection from you to Christ.
- **The Sacrament:** Weekly renewal of the connection. Remembering Christ's body and blood (the entry that created the passage), recommitting to the covenant (keeping the connection active), receiving the Spirit (the flow of divine nature).
- **Temple ordinances:** Extending the passage to its fullest dimensions — connecting not just to death and resurrection but to the complete scope of eternal identity and divine nature.

D&C 19's either/or now resolves completely: if you repent (access the passage, let the weight flow through the connection), you don't bear it — Christ already has. If you refuse to repent (keep the circuit isolated, refuse the connection), you bear it yourself. The passage exists. The choice is whether to connect.

### What the Atonement Did TO Christ

The passage was bidirectional for Christ too. He entered the human condition, and through that entry, the full scope of human experience flowed into Him. He was "made perfect" ([Hebrews 5:8-9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/5.8-9)). He received His fulness ([D&C 93:12-13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/93.12-13)). Before the Atonement: "Be ye perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect" ([Matthew 5:48](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/5.48)). After: "I would that ye should be perfect even as I, or your Father who is in heaven is perfect" ([3 Nephi 12:48](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/3-ne/12.48)). Christ added Himself to the standard of perfection only after the Atonement. The passage completed Him even as it opened the way for us.

### A Caveat: The Model Is a Model

Round 1 established that the legal frame and relational frame are both mortal-cognitive projections — useful for capturing real features of divine reality, but not the reality itself. The same caution applies here. "Permanent passage," "bidirectional flow," "multiple modes" — these are OUR metaphors for organizing the scriptural observations. They are not scripture's own language. The observations are well-grounded: weight genuinely transfers (D&C 19), Christ is genuinely present in ongoing suffering (D&C 122), transformation genuinely occurs over time (Ether 12:27), the covenant system genuinely connects individuals to whatever-this-is (Romans 6:4). The "passage" model organizes these observations better than either the pure entry or pure substitution model. But it is still a projection — a mortal framework for something that may operate on principles we haven't captured. We should hold it the way we learned to hold the courtroom and the household: as a useful shadow, not as the fire.

### Connection to All Prior Rounds

**Round 1:** Justice and mercy are projections of perfect love. → Perfect love is what drove the creation of the passage.

**Round 2:** Event and ongoing process are two faces of one structural change. → The event (Gethsemane, Calvary, resurrection) CREATED the passage. The ongoing process (grace, succoring, transformation) is the continuous FLOW through it.

**Round 3:** Union and conquest are the same act because the enemies are intrinsic to the condition entered. → The entry that created the passage simultaneously defeated the enemies intrinsic to the condition, because the passage drains their power. Death loses finality because the passage includes resurrection. Sin loses permanence because the passage enables repentance. Hell loses its grip because the passage reaches the lowest depths.

**Round 4:** Entry and substitution are the two directions of flow through the same permanent passage. → Entry created it (from divine toward human). Transfer flows through it (weight from human toward divine). Grace flows the other way (divine nature toward human). The passage operates in multiple modes: transfer, presence, and transformation.

## Model Update

- **Before (Round 3):** The Atonement is total divine entry into the fallen condition. The entry IS the conquest because the enemies are intrinsic to what is entered. "The battlefield IS the beloved."
- **After (Round 4):** The Atonement is the creation of a permanent passage between divine nature and the full scope of human experience. Christ's total entry CREATED the passage. Through it, bidirectional flow operates in multiple modes: transfer (weight moves from human to Christ — genuine substitutionary relief), presence (grace sustains within ongoing struggle — the fourth figure in the furnace), and transformation (divine nature enters and remakes the person — the new creature, grace for grace). Entry and substitution are not competing topologies but the two directions of flow through a single permanent connection. The covenant/ordinance system is how individuals access the passage.
- **Because:** Entry alone cannot explain why weight genuinely transfers (D&C 19's either/or). Substitution alone cannot explain why Christ needed to know, why He was changed, why the Incarnation is permanent, and why the dominant effect is transformation rather than mere relief. But if the Atonement created a permanent bidirectional passage — entry creating the infrastructure, multiple flows operating through it — then both substitutionary relief and sustained presence and progressive transformation are all modes of the same connection. The pastoral experience confirms: sometimes weight lifts, sometimes Christ walks with you in the furnace, sometimes weakness slowly becomes strength. All three are the Atonement. All three flow through the passage. The question is never "Is the Atonement working?" The answer is always yes — the passage is open, the flow is active. The question is which mode the passage is operating in for this particular struggle at this particular time.

## New Contradictions

1. **Who or what determines the mode?** If the passage operates in transfer, presence, or transformation mode — who decides which? Does God choose? Does the person's readiness determine it? Is it determined by the nature of the struggle (some struggles are developmental and shouldn't be removed, per the Fall-as-design insight)? Paul asked three times for the thorn to be removed. God said no — "my grace is sufficient." What determines when weight transfers vs. when it stays and grace sustains?

2. **The universal/conditional asymmetry:** Resurrection operates universally through the passage (all bodies, regardless of covenant status). Exaltation operates conditionally (requires covenant access). If the passage is one structure, why the asymmetry? Does the passage have layers that operate by different principles?

3. **The exchange problem:** 2 Corinthians 5:21 says Christ was "made sin." What does it mean for sin to flow through the passage into a divine being? Was Christ genuinely altered? If the passage changes both sides, does sin affect Christ's nature, or does His nature "metabolize" it? And what determines whether sin flowing through the passage is destroyed (metabolized) or just relocated?
