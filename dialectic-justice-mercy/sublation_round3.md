# Round 3 Sublation: The Battlefield IS the Beloved

## The Synthesis

The Atonement is neither a union that dissolves illusory enemies nor a conquest that clears ground for subsequent relationship. It is a **total divine entry into the fallen condition** — and because the "enemies" (death, hell, the devil's power) are structural pathologies WITHIN that condition rather than independent forces external to it, **total entry into the condition is simultaneously total encounter with the enemies.** The union IS the conquest. Not because the enemies are unreal, but because they live inside the thing being entered.

### The Core Insight: Enemies That Are Intrinsic, Not External

Both monks made the same mistake: they assumed the enemies and the beloved occupy different space. Monk A (Union) minimized the enemies — calling them "privations," mere shadows that dissolve when light arrives. Monk B (Conquest) separated the enemies from the human condition — treating death, hell, and the devil as independent forces occupying a field that Christ must cross, sword in hand, before He can reach humanity.

Neither is right. The scriptural picture is stranger and more terrible than either allows.

Consider what Jacob actually describes in 2 Nephi 9. Without the Atonement: bodies rot permanently (death is not an external enemy but what happens TO bodies when spirit departs), spirits become "like unto" the devil (hell is not a separate prison but what happens TO spirits when separated from God and body). The catastrophe is not an invasion by a foreign army. It is the fallen condition left to run its course — entropy, corruption, dissolution operating on beings that were designed for union but have been separated.

But here a crucial reframing is needed. The Fall was not a catastrophe that derailed God's plan. "Adam fell that men might be; and men are, that they might have joy" ([2 Nephi 2:22-25](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/2.22-25)). The conditions of mortality — death, separation, exposure to opposition — are features of a developmental environment designed to produce beings who know good from evil and can choose freely. The "enemies" are therefore not purely pathological. They are the real dangers built into a training ground with real stakes. Death is terrible AND necessary. Separation is painful AND what makes agency possible. The Atonement does not merely FIX what the Fall broke — it COMPLETES what the Fall began, ensuring that the developmental environment's real risks do not result in permanent destruction.

This means the enemies occupy a strange middle position that neither monk grasped. They are not mere absences (Monk A) — Jacob is right to call death and hell a "monster" with a "grasp." The structural dangers of fallenness have their own terrible momentum. They are actively destructive forces with real power to permanently trap beings if left unaddressed. But they are also not independent foreign armies (Monk B) — they are the inherent risks of a developmental design. Like the real dangers of wilderness training: the exposure, the exhaustion, the predators are real and can genuinely kill you, but they are features of the environment, not an invasion.

The devil complicates this picture and deserves direct treatment. He is not a structural pathology — he is a literal personal being with agency who chose rebellion (D&C 76:25-28). But his POWER operates through the structural conditions of fallenness. He "leadeth them captive at his will" ([Moses 4:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/pgp/moses/4.4)) by exploiting the separations that already exist — tempting through the flesh, leveraging ignorance, amplifying the isolation of being separated from God. The Atonement defeats the devil along two axes: it removes his primary leverage (the structural separations — death reversed by resurrection, sin's weight borne by Christ, hell's grip loosened by grace) AND it produces beings who, having been succored and filled with hope, are no longer susceptible to his influence. He retains his agency but loses his arsenal. Like a con artist whose marks have all been warned: he is still a person, but his operation is dismantled.

This means that when Christ enters TOTALLY into the fallen condition — descending below all things, experiencing every pain, every infirmity, every death, every separation — He is not entering a neutral space and then separately fighting enemies. **He is entering the domain WHERE the enemies operate, and the enemies ARE the pathologies of that domain.** To enter fully into death is to encounter death. To enter fully into the weight of sin is to encounter hell. To absorb the full scope of what separation does to beings is to engage the devil's entire arsenal, because the devil's arsenal IS separation.

### The Mechanism: Why Entry Defeats the Enemies

The question both monks struggled with — how does suffering in Gethsemane "defeat" death? — resolves when you see that the enemies are internal to the condition entered.

**An immune system defeats a pathogen not by fighting it from outside but by binding to it** — literally joining with it, entering into contact with it — and neutralizing it through that binding. The antibody's union with the pathogen IS the conquest of the pathogen. They are the same act. The inflammation (the pain, the fever) is the cost of the engagement, not evidence of a separate military operation.

Christ's total entry into the fallen condition works analogously. He takes upon Himself death — not fighting death from outside but actually dying, entering into death, experiencing it from the inside. And because He is a divine being whose nature cannot be permanently held by death, **His passage through death breaks death's claim to universality.** Death held all of humanity because no one had ever passed through it and emerged with body permanently restored. Christ did. The resurrection — spirit and body "inseparably connected" ([D&C 93:33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/93.33)) — is proof that the pathology of separation is not ultimate. A divine being entered the condition, endured its worst, and emerged whole. The bands break not by force applied from outside but by a being passing through them from within.

This is exactly what Hebrews 2:14 describes, and both monks cited it without grasping what it proves: Christ took on flesh and blood — He **entered the human condition** — "that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death." The mechanism of destruction is participation. Not combat. Not mere presence. Participation: entering into the thing, binding with it, and proving by passage through it that it is not ultimate.

But Monk B's insight must be preserved more honestly than simply calling the enemies "real." The enemies don't just exist inside the condition — they SHAPE the entry. Without them, entry would be painless incarnation. It is precisely because death is real, because sin has genuine weight, because hell actively grasps — that total entry is agonizing. The enemies are not mere adjectives attached to an otherwise serene act of union. They are what make the entry an act of heroic endurance. Monk B was right that the Atonement is about power — specifically, the power to endure total entry into a condition that is actively trying to destroy the one entering. The conquest is not a side effect of the union. The conquest is what gives the union its specific character: costly, agonizing, victorious. A union without enemies would be a visit. A union against enemies that are intrinsic to the condition being entered is the Atonement.

### Why This Is Not Monk A's Position

Monk A said: "Light arrives and darkness ceases. The entry IS the conquest." This sounds like the synthesis, but it's not, because Monk A's version empties the enemies of reality. If death is mere absence and the conquest is effortless — light arriving, darkness vanishing — then why did it hurt? Why the blood from every pore? Why "God, the greatest of all, to tremble because of pain" ([D&C 19:18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/19.18))?

The synthesis says: the enemies are real. They have teeth. Death is not an absence — it is an active structural corruption with genuine power to destroy. But its power is INTRINSIC to the fallen condition, not external to it. And that is precisely why total entry is so costly. Christ didn't just "arrive" like light filling a room. He entered into the full scope of what fallenness does to beings — every pain, every death, every separation, every horror — and it nearly destroyed Him. "Which suffering caused myself, even God, the greatest of all, to tremble because of pain, and to bleed at every pore, and to suffer both body and spirit — and would that I might not drink the bitter cup, and shrink" ([D&C 19:18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/19.18)). This is not light effortlessly dispelling darkness. This is a being entering a condition whose internal pathologies are so powerful, so actively destructive, that total entry into them is agonizing even for God.

The pain is not from an external enemy striking blows. The pain is from the SCOPE and SEVERITY of what is being entered into. The fallen condition, experienced in totality — every human death, every grief, every sin's weight — is that terrible. The enemies are real because the condition is that bad. And the conquest is real because enduring that total entry without being consumed by it proves the enemies are not ultimate.

### Why This Is Not Monk B's Position

Monk B said: "The Atonement is fundamentally about power — the capacity to destroy what destroys." This sounds like the synthesis, but it's not, because Monk B's version requires a separate military operation distinct from the relational entry.

Monk B claimed that if union were the fundamental shape, painless incarnation would suffice. But the synthesis reveals why this is wrong: painless incarnation would be PARTIAL entry. Living a comfortable human life and returning to heaven would enter the pleasant parts of the human condition without entering the pathological parts. And the pathological parts — death, sin's weight, hell's reality — are precisely WHERE the enemies operate. You cannot defeat enemies that are intrinsic to the condition by entering only the nice parts of the condition. You must enter ALL of it, including the worst of it, for the entry to be total enough to encounter and overcome every pathology.

The pain was not the cost of a separate military campaign. The pain was the cost of TOTAL entry into a condition whose pathologies are genuinely terrible. Monk B was right that the pain was the cost of defeating real enemies. But the mechanism of defeat was not combat — it was the completeness of the entry. Christ didn't fight death. He died. He didn't battle sin's weight from outside. He bore it. He didn't assault hell. He descended into it. And by passing through all of it as a divine being who could not be ultimately consumed, He proved that none of it is final.

### Alma 7:12 Unified

Monk B correctly noticed that Alma 7:12 contains two purposes: loosing the bands of death AND succoring infirmities. But they are not sequential (first conquest, then union). They are **simultaneous aspects of the same total entry:**

- "He will take upon him death, that he may loose the bands of death which bind his people" — entering into death IS what looses the bands, because a divine being passing through death and emerging proves death is not permanent.
- "He will take upon him their infirmities, that his bowels may be filled with mercy, according to the flesh, that he may know according to the flesh how to succor his people" — entering into human suffering IS what creates the capacity to succor, because experiential knowledge enables empathy.

Same act. Same entry. Two results: the enemies are defeated (because the entry proves they are not ultimate) and the relational capacity is created (because the entry produces experiential knowledge). Union and conquest are not two phases. They are two descriptions of what total divine entry into the fallen condition accomplishes.

### How Christ's Passage Becomes Operative: The Covenantal Infrastructure

A valid critique of the original synthesis: it explains what Christ did cosmically but not how that cosmic act reaches individual human lives. Christ passed through death and emerged whole — but everyone still dies. Christ bore the weight of sin — but people still sin. If the conquest is complete, why are the enemies still operative?

The answer lies in the distinction between the structural possibility and its individual activation. Christ's passage through the fallen condition created a permanent structural change: death CAN be reversed, sin CAN be overcome, hell's grip CAN be broken. Before the Atonement, these were impossible — 2 Nephi 9's counterfactual is absolute. After it, they are possible but must be individually activated.

The activation mechanism is the ordinance and covenant system — what Restoration theology calls the "gospel of Jesus Christ." Baptism is not a bureaucratic membership ritual. It is the individual's entry into Christ's death and resurrection: "buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead... even so we also should walk in newness of life" ([Romans 6:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rom/6.4)). The sacrament is not a memorial of a dead event but a weekly re-entry into the ongoing covenant relationship. The temple ordinances extend the activation across generations and into the fullest dimensions of exaltation.

This explains the "already but not yet" pattern: the conquest is structurally complete (the passage has been made, the enemies proven non-ultimate) but individually progressive (each person must be drawn into the passage through covenant, ordinance, and the ongoing operation of grace). The Atonement created the cure; the ordinances administer it; the Spirit sustains the patient through recovery. All three are necessary. None alone is sufficient.

This also connects back to Round 2's insight about the Atonement as both event and ongoing process: the event (Christ's passage) created the structural possibility. The ongoing process (grace, succoring, Spirit filling with hope) is how individuals are drawn into that passage over a lifetime of covenant-keeping and becoming.

### What the Atonement Did TO Christ

One dimension the entire dialectic has underweighted: the Atonement was not only something Christ did FOR humanity. It was something that constituted Christ's own becoming. "Though he were a Son, yet learned he obedience by the things which he suffered; And being made perfect, he became the author of eternal salvation" ([Hebrews 5:8-9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/5.8-9)). He "received not of the fulness at the first, but received grace for grace" ([D&C 93:12-13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/93.12-13)). The Atonement was the act through which Christ received His fulness — the culmination of His own "grace for grace" development.

This deepens the synthesis: the total entry into the fallen condition was not a fully-formed God descending into an alien environment. It was the act through which Christ Himself was "made perfect" — His own developmental completion. The entry transformed both sides: Christ gained the experiential knowledge and fulness that completed His becoming, and humanity gained a being who had passed through their condition and could now succor them from within it. The Atonement was bidirectional: Christ entered humanity's condition, and through that entry, His own divine nature reached its fulness.

The most striking textual evidence: Christ's own rhetoric shifts across the Atonement. In the Sermon on the Mount (before the Atonement), He says "Be ye therefore perfect, even as your Father which is in heaven is perfect" ([Matthew 5:48](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/5.48)). He points to the Father alone as the standard. But in 3 Nephi (after the Atonement, after the resurrection, after receiving His fulness), He revises: "I would that ye should be perfect even as I, or your Father who is in heaven is perfect" ([3 Nephi 12:48](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/3-ne/12.48)). He adds Himself. The Atonement is the event between these two statements — the event through which Christ completed His own becoming and could now stand alongside the Father as a standard of perfection. He could not make this claim before Gethsemane. He could after.

### Connection to Prior Rounds

**Round 1** established that justice and mercy are projections of a single reality — perfect love. The separation between them is a mortal-cognitive artifact.

**Round 2** established that event and process are two faces of a single structural change — the Atonement as permanent union, like a wedding and a marriage.

**Round 3** adds: **union and conquest are the same act, because the enemies are intrinsic to the condition being entered.** This is the same pattern as Round 1 — mortal minds separate things that are unified in the divine act — but applied at a deeper level. We project "union" and "conquest" as separate categories because our experience divides relational acts (embracing, entering, bonding) from violent acts (fighting, breaking, destroying). But in the Atonement, the embrace IS the battle. The entry IS the weapon. The love IS the force. Christ's total entry into the fallen condition — driven by perfect love — simultaneously heals the relational separation AND defeats the structural enemies, because the enemies ARE the separation, experienced in its full destructive power.

The Joseph Smith anchor quote from Round 1 still holds: God is simultaneously "more liberal in His mercies" AND "more terrible to the workers of iniquity." Round 3 adds: the Atonement is simultaneously the deepest act of relational love (total entry into the beloved's condition) AND the most violent act of cosmic war (total defeat of the structural enemies within that condition). Both dials turned to the same setting. Same act. Same love. Same entry. Different descriptions of what that entry accomplishes.

## Model Update

- **Before (Round 2):** The Atonement is a permanent union of divine and human experience — like a wedding and a marriage. Event and ongoing operation are two faces of one structural change.
- **After (Round 3):** The Atonement is a total divine entry into the fallen condition — a developmental environment with real dangers, not a pure catastrophe — and because the "enemies" (death, hell, the devil's power) are intrinsic to that condition rather than independent external forces, the entry IS the conquest. The enemies are not mere absences (they have teeth — Jacob's "monster" is real) but neither are they foreign armies (they are the inherent dangers of the developmental environment). Christ entered totally, endured the worst the condition could inflict, and emerged whole — proving the dangers are not ultimate. The devil is a personal agent whose power operates through these structural conditions; the Atonement dismantles his leverage without annihilating his person. The conquest is structurally complete but individually activated through the covenant/ordinance system (baptism as entry into Christ's death and resurrection, sacrament as ongoing renewal, temple as fullest activation). And the Atonement was bidirectional: it constituted Christ's own becoming ("learned obedience," "made perfect," received His fulness) even as it opened the way for humanity's becoming.
- **Because:** The union reading cannot explain the enemies' reality. The conquest reading cannot explain the mechanism (participation, not combat — Hebrews 2:14). But if the enemies are real dangers intrinsic to a developmental environment, then total entry IS total engagement. The same act that creates relational union simultaneously defeats the structural enemies. The pain was the cost of the scope of entry. The victory was the passage of a divine being through the worst of the condition without being consumed. The individual activation happens through covenants and ordinances that draw each person into Christ's passage. And the entry was itself Christ's developmental completion — He was "made perfect" through it, which means the Atonement was not a fully-formed God visiting an alien environment but the culminating act of a being who "received grace for grace" until this final, total entry constituted His fulness.

## New Contradictions

1. **Entry vs. Substitution:** The synthesis says Christ entered TOTALLY into the fallen condition (going where we are). But D&C 19:16-17 says Christ suffered so that we would NOT have to. Entry means going to the sufferer's location. Substitution means bearing what the sufferer will not then bear. Can Christ simultaneously enter INTO our suffering and take it AWAY FROM us? These appear structurally opposed.

2. **The bidirectional becoming problem:** If the Atonement constituted Christ's own becoming ("made perfect through suffering"), then Christ was not fully formed before Gethsemane. What does this mean for the pre-mortal Christ who created worlds and led the council in heaven? How does a being who is already supreme among intelligences (Abraham 3:19) still need to be "made perfect"?

3. **Universal resurrection vs. conditional exaltation:** The Atonement makes resurrection universal (everyone, including the wicked) but exaltation conditional (requiring covenant faithfulness). If the conquest is the same act as the union, why does the conquest apply universally while the union applies conditionally? This suggests they may not be fully identical after all.
