# It Is Finished: Why the Atonement Is a Completed Event, Not an Ongoing Force

## The Claim

"It is finished" ([John 19:30](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/19.30)). Three words. Past tense. Declarative. Not "it is beginning," not "it is now available as a developmental resource," not "it is an ongoing process you will participate in." Finished. Done. The most important thing that ever happened in the history of the cosmos happened once, in a specific garden and on a specific hill, in the body of a specific man, and it is over. That is what the Atonement is. Everything else -- the hope, the transformation, the filling with perfect love, the long developmental arc of becoming -- is the human response to a completed act. It is not the Atonement itself.

Amulek states the ontological structure with a precision that leaves no room to maneuver: "it is expedient that there should be a great and last sacrifice... it must be an infinite and eternal sacrifice" ([Alma 34:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/34.10)). Great and LAST. Not "great and ongoing." Not "great and continuously distributed across human experience." Last. The entire Mosaic system -- every lamb, every bullock, every burnt offering across millennia -- pointed forward to this single event, "every whit pointing to that great and last sacrifice; and that great and last sacrifice will be the Son of God, yea, infinite and eternal" ([Alma 34:14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/34.14)). The whole scaffolding of history was erected to frame one act. Not a process. An act.

Paul drills this to bedrock: "For in that he died, he died unto sin once" ([Romans 6:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rom/6.10)). The author of Hebrews repeats it with escalating force: "now once in the end of the world hath he appeared to put away sin by the sacrifice of himself" ([Hebrews 9:26](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/9.26)). "Christ was once offered to bear the sins of many" ([Hebrews 9:28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/9.28)). "We are sanctified through the offering of the body of Jesus Christ once for all" ([Hebrews 10:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/10.10)). "This man, after he had offered one sacrifice for sins for ever, sat down on the right hand of God" ([Hebrews 10:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/10.12)). He sat down. The work is complete. The priest who offers an ongoing sacrifice does not sit. Christ sat down because the offering was finished.

"For by one offering he hath perfected for ever them that are sanctified" ([Hebrews 10:14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/10.14)). One offering. Perfected. For ever. The language is not ambiguous.

## The Opposing Case, Stated as Its Advocates Would

The strongest version of the "ongoing force" argument goes something like this: in actual lived experience, the Atonement is not encountered as a historical event at all. It is encountered as a present reality -- a living current of grace that fills the soul with hope and perfect love, that sustains the daily work of transformation, that operates in each person's unique process of becoming. The zone conference experience is the paradigm case: a young missionary walks into a room and is flooded with spiritual light, overwhelmed with a vision of what he can become. He walks out the same person who walked in -- still bad at Tagalog, still awkward -- but now filled with hope. The transformation that follows takes years. The Atonement, on this reading, is not the event in Gethsemane; the Atonement is the hope that fills and the love that sustains and the grace that enables the long, hard, personal work of becoming a new creature.

Moroni 8:26 is the anchor text: "the Comforter filleth with hope and perfect love, which love endureth by diligence unto prayer, until the end shall come." This is explicitly processual -- enduring, diligent, ongoing. Alma 7:11-12 describes Christ taking upon Himself pains, afflictions, temptations, sicknesses, infirmities, and death, "that he may know according to the flesh how to succor his people according to their infirmities." The emphasis is not on the completed act but on the ongoing capacity to succor -- present tense, continuous, relational. And D&C 93:12-13 delivers the capstone: Christ Himself "received not of the fulness at the first, but received grace for grace... and continued from grace to grace, until he received a fulness." Even Christ's own development was processual. If the Son of God Himself grew from grace to grace rather than arriving complete, how can we say the Atonement is fundamentally about a finished event rather than an ongoing developmental reality?

This case is eloquent, experientially grounded, and deeply felt. And it is a category error of the most dangerous kind.

## Where the "Ongoing Force" Reading Fails

The failure is precise, and it is fatal: the "ongoing force" reading conflates the Atonement with its effects. Hope is an effect of the Atonement. Transformation is an effect. The Comforter filling with perfect love is an effect. The capacity to succor is an effect. But effects are not causes. The warmth you feel from a fire is not the fire. The light by which you read is not the bulb. And the ongoing experience of grace in daily life is not the Atonement -- it is what the Atonement makes possible.

This distinction is not pedantic. It is the load-bearing wall of the entire edifice.

Consider what happens if you remove it. If the Atonement IS the ongoing developmental force rather than the historical event that generates it, then what exactly did Christ accomplish in Gethsemane? The answer, on the "ongoing force" reading, is unclear -- perhaps He inaugurated a process, perhaps He opened a channel, perhaps He demonstrated a pattern. But "inaugurated a process" is not what Amulek describes. Amulek describes a sacrifice so specific that "there is not any man that can sacrifice his own blood which will atone for the sins of another" ([Alma 34:11](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/34.11)). He describes a necessity so absolute that "there can be nothing which is short of an infinite atonement which will suffice for the sins of the world" ([Alma 34:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/34.12)). The Atonement's power lies in its singularity, its unrepeatable specificity, the fact that it was done by this particular being at this particular cost and that no other being and no other cost could have accomplished it.

The Ira Glass analogy in the user's talk reveals exactly where the "ongoing force" reading drifts into trouble. Ira Glass is describing a natural creative process: you develop good taste, you see the gap between your taste and your work, and you close the gap through years of effort. This is a true and useful description of creative development. But it has nothing to do with Christ's Atonement. Any inspiring vision can give you "good taste for what sort of person you can become." A great teacher can do this. A profound book can do this. An encounter with beauty can do this. If the Atonement merely gives vision and hope -- if that is its "whole point" -- then what differentiates it from any other source of inspiration? What makes it specifically Christ's sacrifice rather than generic spiritual uplift?

The answer is: the cost. The specificity. The blood. The fact that "God, the greatest of all, to tremble because of pain, and to bleed at every pore, and to suffer both body and spirit" ([D&C 19:18](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/19.18)). The fact that "I, God, have suffered these things for all, that they might not suffer if they would repent" ([D&C 19:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/19.16)). This is substitutionary language. Someone suffered so that someone else would not have to. That is not a developmental process. That is a transaction completed at unimaginable cost by a specific person at a specific time.

## What Is at Stake

If the Atonement is primarily an ongoing developmental force, it loses its anchor in history and becomes a principle. Principles are general. Principles are abstract. Principles do not bleed. And a principle cannot do what Abinadi says Christ did: "having ascended into heaven, having the bowels of mercy; being filled with compassion towards the children of men; standing betwixt them and justice; having broken the bands of death, taken upon himself their iniquity and their transgressions, having redeemed them, and satisfied the demands of justice" ([Mosiah 15:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/15.9)). Every verb here is past tense. Having ascended. Having broken. Taken upon himself. Redeemed them. Satisfied. The demands of justice are not being continuously satisfied in an ongoing developmental process. They were satisfied. Once. By a specific act of a specific person who stood in a specific place between us and the full weight of cosmic law.

The "ongoing force" reading does not deny any of this explicitly. It simply moves the emphasis until the event becomes background and the experience becomes foreground -- until Gethsemane is the generating station and what really matters is the electricity in the wires. But generating stations matter. Cut the wire from the station and the lights go out. The zone conference experience -- that overwhelming flood of hope and light -- was not a free-floating spiritual phenomenon. It was the downstream effect of a man who bled at every pore in a garden outside Jerusalem so that a young missionary in the Philippines two thousand years later could be filled with a hope that is grounded not in optimism, not in positive thinking, not in "good taste," but in the objective historical fact that the debt has already been paid. The hope is real because the event is real. Dissolve the event into a process and the hope becomes just another nice feeling.

## The Extreme Position

Here is where I will not flinch: D&C 93:12-13 -- Christ receiving "grace for grace" -- does not prove that the Atonement is processual. It proves that Christ's mortal development was processual. His development and His Atonement are not the same thing. He grew from grace to grace in preparation for the act, the way an athlete trains for years in preparation for a single race. The training is processual. The race is an event. No one says the marathon is "primarily an ongoing developmental process" because the runner trained for it over years. The training serves the event. Christ's growth from grace to grace served Gethsemane. It was not Gethsemane.

And here is the claim pushed to its sharpest edge: without the completed event, there is nothing to have faith IN. Faith is not faith in a process. Faith is not faith in development. Faith is faith in Christ -- a person who did a specific thing. "He will take upon him death, that he may loose the bands of death which bind his people" ([Alma 7:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/7.12)). Will take. And He did. And it is done. The ongoing filling with hope, the transformation, the becoming -- all of it is the fruit of a tree that was planted on a Friday afternoon on a hill called Golgotha when a man said "It is finished" and meant it. The fruit is real. The fruit is ongoing. But the fruit is not the tree.

An Atonement that is "primarily" an ongoing developmental force is an Atonement that could, in principle, operate without Christ. Any sufficiently inspiring vision could fill you with hope. Any sufficiently powerful spiritual experience could sustain transformation. But only one person bled at every pore. Only one person stood between us and justice. Only one sacrifice was infinite and eternal. The Atonement is not primarily what it does to us. The Atonement is what He did. And what He did is finished.
