# The Atonement as Union: Why the Conquest Reading Fails

## I. What the Atonement IS

The Atonement is the permanent, irreversible entry of divine nature into human experience -- and by that entry, the permanent creation of a bridge across which human nature can pass into divine life. It is not fundamentally a battle. It is fundamentally a joining.

The key verb is not "conquer" or "defeat." The key verb is "descend." "He that ascended up on high, as also he descended below all things, in that he comprehended all things, that he might be in all and through all things" ([D&C 88:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/88.6)). Notice the grammar of purpose: He descended *in that he comprehended* -- the descent IS the comprehension. And the purpose clause is not "that he might destroy all enemies" but "that he might be in all and through all things." The telos is presence. Permeation. Indwelling. Union.

When Nephi is asked "Knowest thou the condescension of God?" ([1 Nephi 11:16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/11.16)), the word itself tells the story. Condescension: to descend with, to come down to be alongside. Not condemnation of an enemy but con-descent with the beloved. The angel does not ask "Knowest thou the victory of God?" He asks about the coming-down, the entering-in.

Alma 7:11-12 is the clearest ontological statement we have of what the Atonement does: Christ went forth "suffering pains and afflictions and temptations of every kind" so "that his bowels may be filled with mercy, according to the flesh, that he may know according to the flesh how to succor his people according to their infirmities." The suffering is not warfare. It is the acquisition of experiential knowledge. Christ suffered TO KNOW, and He knows in order TO SUCCOR. The entire arc is relational: entering into experience creates the capacity for ongoing relationship. The logic is the logic of empathy carried to an infinite extreme -- not the logic of combat.

And the culminating ontological claim: "spirit and element, inseparably connected, receive a fulness of joy" ([D&C 93:33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/93.33)). "And when separated, man cannot receive a fulness of joy" ([D&C 93:34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/93.34)). The resurrection -- the Atonement's signature act -- is defined here not as victory over an enemy but as the permanent union of spirit and element that makes joy structurally possible. The resurrection is a wedding of body and spirit: "their spirits uniting with their bodies, never to be divided; thus the whole becoming spiritual and immortal" ([Alma 11:45](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/11.45)). "Never to be divided." That is the language of covenant union, not battlefield triumph.

D&C 88:15-16 makes this explicit: "The spirit and the body are the soul of man. And the resurrection from the dead is the redemption of the soul." Redemption IS the reuniting. The Atonement redeems by making things that were separated inseparable.

And Christ's own final prayer before the Atonement began is not a war cry. It is the prayer of John 17: "That they all may be one; as thou, Father, art in me, and I in thee, that they also may be one in us" ([John 17:21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/17.21)). The Atonement's purpose, from Christ's own mouth, stated at the moment of its inception, is oneness. Union. Indwelling. Not conquest.

## II. The Conquest Reading's Best Case

The conquest advocate has real scripture and should not be dismissed cheaply. Jacob's vision in 2 Nephi 9 is genuinely terrifying: without the Atonement, "our spirits must have become like unto [the devil], and we become devils, angels to a devil, to be shut out from the presence of our God" ([2 Nephi 9:9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/9.9)). This is ontological catastrophe -- permanent capture by an adversary. And the language Jacob uses is unmistakably military: "that monster, death and hell" ([2 Nephi 9:10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/9.10)). A monster is something you fight, not something you marry.

Mosiah 15:8-9 is equally forceful: "God breaketh the bands of death, having gained the victory over death; giving the Son power to make intercession for the children of men." Bands are broken. Victory is gained. And the author of Hebrews concurs: Christ took on flesh "that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death, that is, the devil" ([Hebrews 2:14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/heb/2.14)).

The conquest reading says: these are not metaphors. Death is a real structural power that holds creation captive. The devil is a real adversary with real power. Christ entered the arena and fought and won. You cannot have union with a prisoner until you break the chains. Liberation precedes relationship. The conquest is the necessary condition for everything else.

This is a strong argument. I take it seriously. And it is wrong.

## III. Where the Conquest Reading Breaks

The conquest reading fails at three specific, identifiable points.

**First, it cannot explain the mechanism.** If the Atonement is primarily a battle, what is the weapon? How does bleeding at every pore in Gethsemane "defeat" death? How does absorbing the weight of human sin constitute a military victory? The conquest reading borrows the language of war but cannot describe the actual combat. There is no clash, no exchange of blows, no strategic maneuver. What happened in Gethsemane was that Christ entered into the full scope of human suffering and bore it. That is not fighting. That is entering in. The conquest reading uses military language to describe an event whose actual structure is relational -- and then cannot explain why the relational act produces the military result.

**Second, it cannot explain the ongoing operation.** If Christ won the battle, the war is over. But the Atonement is manifestly not over. Christ continues to succor, to fill with hope and perfect love, to stand at the door and knock, to make weak things strong. The conquest reading treats these ongoing operations as "effects" of a past victory -- spoils of war distributed to the liberated. But Alma 7:12 says the suffering happened specifically so that Christ could succor. The succoring is the purpose, not the spoils. A general does not fight a war in order to learn how to comfort the wounded. But a parent enters into a child's suffering precisely in order to know how to help. The ongoing relational operation is not downstream of the conquest. The conquest reading has the teleology backwards.

**Third, it cannot explain the resurrection as scripture actually describes it.** If resurrection is victory over death, you would expect the language of domination: death is destroyed, annihilated, conquered. And some verses do use that language. But the deepest descriptions of what the resurrection IS are relational: "the spirit and the body is restored to itself again" ([2 Nephi 9:13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/9.13)); "their spirits uniting with their bodies, never to be divided" ([Alma 11:45](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/11.45)); "spirit and element, inseparably connected, receive a fulness of joy" ([D&C 93:33](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/93.33)). The resurrection is not the destruction of an enemy. It is the permanent union of things that were separated. Death is not conquered like an opposing army. Death is healed like a wound -- by the rejoining of what was torn apart. The conquest language describes what union *looks like from the outside*. The union is what actually happens.

## IV. Why Union Accounts for Conquest, but Not Vice Versa

This is the decisive asymmetry.

The union reading can fully explain every conquest passage. "Breaking the bands of death" is what permanent union of spirit and body does to the condition of separation. Bands are broken not by force applied against them but by the joining of what they held apart -- the way a cast is broken not by attacking it but by the healed bone making it unnecessary. "The monster death and hell" is what separation looks like when it has gone on long enough to become a structural feature of existence -- and union dissolves it the way light dissolves darkness, not by fighting it but by being present where it was. Christ "destroys" the devil not by overpowering him in combat but by entering into the human condition so fully that the devil's primary weapon -- the power to separate beings from God and from their own bodies -- is rendered permanently inoperative. You cannot separate what has been inseparably connected.

But the conquest reading cannot account for the union language. If the Atonement is fundamentally a battle, why does Christ pray for oneness in John 17? Why does Alma say He suffered to *know how to succor*? Why is the resurrection described as *reuniting* rather than *vanquishing*? Why is the key word "condescension" -- a coming-down-with -- rather than "domination"? The conquest reading must treat all of this as secondary, as the relational aftermath of a military event. But there is too much of it, and it is too structurally central. The Intercessory Prayer is not an afterthought. Alma 7:12 is not a footnote. D&C 93:33-34 is not a minor doctrinal aside. The union language is not a downstream effect of conquest. It is the primary reality, and the conquest language describes one of its consequences.

## V. The Extreme Claim

Here is where I push this to its hardest edge.

If the Atonement is fundamentally union -- the permanent joining of divine and human experience -- then death, hell, and the devil are not independent realities that God must fight. They are consequences of a prior separation, and they have exactly as much power as the separation gives them. Death is what happens when spirit and element are divided. Hell is what happens when a person is separated from God. The devil's power is the power to persuade beings to choose separation. None of these are ontologically independent. They are all parasitic on the condition of disunion. They are shadows cast by the absence of connection.

This means the conquest language, taken as fundamental, actually *aggrandizes the enemy*. It grants death, hell, and the devil an ontological weight they do not possess. It treats them as co-equal forces that God must contend with rather than as privations -- absences of union that dissolve when union is established. The conquest reading, pushed to its logical conclusion, produces a cosmos with two poles: God and the devil, locked in genuine combat, the outcome uncertain until Christ wins. But that is not the Restoration's cosmos. In the Restoration's cosmos, the devil is a fallen son, not a rival god. Death is a temporary condition of separated spirit and element, not an independent power. Hell is the self-inflicted torment of those who cannot tolerate God's presence. These are not enemies in the military sense. They are wounds. And you do not fight a wound. You heal it. By joining together what was torn apart.

The most uncomfortable version of this: the Atonement's "battle" against the devil is not a contest between equals or even near-equals. It is the asymmetric action of presence entering absence. Light does not fight darkness. Light arrives, and darkness is simply no longer there. Christ descends below all things, and by descending -- by being present in the furthest reaches of separation -- He makes it so that nowhere in the cosmos is entirely cut off from God. The victory is not won by force. The victory is the union itself. The entry is the conquest. And anyone who frames it as a battle has mistaken the shadow for the fire.

Hebrews 2:14 proves this inadvertently: Christ took on flesh and blood -- He *entered into the human condition* -- "that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death." The mechanism of destruction is not combat. It is participation. He became what we are so that the separation the devil exploits would be permanently bridged. The very verse the conquest reading leans on describes union as the instrument that produces the conquest. The union is the deeper category. It always was.
