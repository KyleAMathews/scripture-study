# Round 4 Determinate Negation: Entry vs. Substitution

## 5.1 Surface Contradiction

Monk A (Entry): The Atonement's fundamental topology is Christ going TO where we are — total divine participation in the human condition. The comfort is presence, not exemption. "The Son of Man hath descended below them all" (D&C 122:8). The substitutionary language describes a consequence of total entry.

Monk B (Substitution): The Atonement's fundamental topology is Christ bearing AWAY what would otherwise fall on us. "I have suffered these things for all, that they might not suffer if they would repent" (D&C 19:16). Entry is the necessary means of reaching the weight; the bearing-away is the point.

## 5.2 Shared Assumptions

1. **Both assume a static topology.** Both treat the landscape of suffering as fixed: Christ either moves toward it (entry) or it moves toward Him (substitution). Neither considers that the Atonement might CHANGE the topology itself — creating a structural connection that didn't previously exist.

2. **Both assume the Atonement's flow is unidirectional.** Monk A: divine nature flows toward humanity (presence, comprehension). Monk B: human weight flows toward Christ (bearing, carrying). Neither considers that the flow might be BIDIRECTIONAL — weight moving one way and grace moving the other through the same connection.

3. **Both assume the Atonement operates on pre-existing suffering as a fixed quantity.** Monk B makes this explicit (D&C 19's either/or — a definite quantum that must be borne by someone). Monk A implicitly accepts it by arguing that Christ enters a condition that already exists. Neither asks whether the Atonement transforms what suffering IS, not just who bears it or who is present in it.

4. **Both treat the Atonement as a single act with a single topology.** Neither considers that the Atonement might create ongoing INFRASTRUCTURE — a permanent passage — through which multiple operations (entry, transfer, transformation) occur simultaneously and continuously.

## 5.3 Determinate Negation

### Where Monk A (Entry) Specifically Fails

1. **Cannot explain genuine relief.** D&C 19:15-17 is a strict either/or: either Christ suffers it or you do. The entry reading predicts co-presence (both Christ and the sufferer are in the same condition). It cannot explain why the repentant are RELIEVED — why weight actually MOVES. Two people in the same fire are both burning. Presence does not extinguish flames. → This reveals: the entry reading has no mechanism for TRANSFER. It can explain why Christ is with you but not why you're lighter.

2. **Cannot explain Alma 7:13.** The entry reading builds on 7:12 (suffered to know how to succor — the epistemological purpose). But 7:13 states: "the Son of God suffereth according to the flesh that he might take upon him the sins of his people, that he might blot out their transgressions." The purpose here is not knowing but REMOVAL — blotting out. → This reveals: the entry reading selectively cites Alma 7:12 while ignoring 7:13, which states a substitutionary purpose in the same breath.

3. **At its extreme, makes the Atonement a visit.** As Monk B correctly notes: empathy does not move weight. A doctor who fully comprehends your cancer but cannot remove it has not healed you. → This reveals: the entry reading struggles to explain salvific efficacy — WHY entry produces actual change, not just divine understanding.

### Where Monk B (Substitution) Specifically Fails

1. **Cannot explain the epistemological dimension.** Alma 7:12 says Christ suffered "that he may know according to the flesh how to succor." Hebrews 4:15: "touched with the feeling of our infirmities." A substitute doesn't need to understand the weight — a substitute needs a checkbook. → This reveals: the substitution reading has no explanation for why Christ's KNOWING was the stated purpose of the suffering.

2. **Cannot explain transformation.** The dominant effect of the Atonement on people is not subtraction (weight removed) but metamorphosis (new creature, born again, mighty change of heart, new heart). Substitution predicts lighter people. Scripture describes changed people. → This reveals: the substitution reading only accounts for the removal direction of the Atonement's operation, not the infusion direction (grace flowing IN).

3. **Cannot explain why Christ was changed.** Hebrews 5:8-9 ("made perfect through suffering"), the Matthew 5:48 → 3 Nephi 12:48 shift. A substitute is not constituted by the act of bearing. → This reveals: the substitution reading treats Christ as a fully-formed agent performing an external act, when the scriptures describe the Atonement as constitutive of Christ's own becoming.

4. **Makes the Incarnation disposable.** If the point is the transaction, why does Christ retain a resurrected body? Why the nail prints? Why "I will come to you"? Once the price is paid, the entry should be unnecessary. → This reveals: the substitution reading can't account for the permanence of the connection.

## 5.4 The Hidden Question

Not "Did Christ enter our condition or bear our weight?"

But: **What if the Atonement created a STRUCTURE — a permanent passage between divine and human nature — through which BOTH entry and transfer operate as two directions of flow?** Entry created the passage. Transfer flows through it. Grace flows the other way. The Atonement is infrastructure, not a single-direction act.

## 5.5 Boydian Decomposition

### Atomic Parts (unstructured, stripped of position)

- Christ descended below all things (D&C 88:6)
- Purpose: "that he might be in all and through all things" — permeation/omnipresence
- Purpose: "that he may know according to the flesh how to succor" — experiential knowledge (Alma 7:12)
- Purpose: "that he might blot out their transgressions" — removal (Alma 7:13)
- Purpose: "that they might not suffer if they would repent" — substitutionary relief (D&C 19:16)
- The suffering is a definite transferable quantity — either Christ or the sinner bears it (D&C 19:15-17)
- Isaiah 53: every verb is a transfer verb — borne, carried, laid on, bare
- "Taketh away the sin of the world" (John 1:29) — removal language
- "Standing betwixt them and justice" (Mosiah 15:9) — interposition
- "Bare our sins in his own body" (1 Peter 2:24) — physical location of transferred weight
- "Made him to be sin for us... that we might be made the righteousness of God in him" (2 Corinthians 5:21) — BIDIRECTIONAL EXCHANGE
- Christ retains resurrected body, nail prints — permanent physical consequence of entry
- "I will not leave you comfortless: I will come to you" (John 14:18) — permanence of presence
- The fourth figure in the furnace (Daniel 3:25) — presence INSIDE the ordeal, not removal of it
- D&C 122:7-8 — God's answer to suffering is "I have been there," not "I have removed this"
- "Comforter filleth with hope and perfect love" (Moroni 8:26) — grace flowing TO the person
- "Make weak things become strong" (Ether 12:27) — transformative grace, not subtractive
- "Born again" / "new creature" / "mighty change of heart" — metamorphosis, not relief
- "Partakers of the divine nature" (2 Peter 1:4) — divine nature flowing TO humanity
- Baptism as "buried with him... into death" (Romans 6:4) — individual entry into Christ's passage
- Sacrament — weekly renewal of connection
- "Grace for grace" — progressive flow, not one-time transfer
- Christ was "made perfect" through suffering (Hebrews 5:8-9) — bidirectional constitution
- Matthew 5:48 → 3 Nephi 12:48 — Christ's own perfection completed through the Atonement
- Mosaic sacrificial system — lamb dies INSTEAD OF sinner, scapegoat carries sins AWAY
- "My grace is sufficient for thee" (2 Cor 12:9) — sometimes grace sustains WITHOUT removing the thorn

### Cross-Domain Connections

1. **2 Corinthians 5:21 + 2 Peter 1:4 = bidirectional flow.** Sin flows from us to Christ ("made him to be sin for us"). Divine nature flows from Christ to us ("partakers of the divine nature"). These are not two separate acts but two directions of traffic through a single connection. Neither monk noticed this because each was focused on only one direction of flow.

2. **D&C 122 + D&C 19 = two operations of the same connection.** D&C 122: God's answer is presence — "I have been there" (the connection exists, and through it, Christ is with you). D&C 19: God's promise is relief — "that they might not suffer" (through the same connection, weight actually transfers). Both are real. Both happen. They are not contradictory promises but different modes of the same passage.

3. **Romans 6:4 + the covenant/ordinance system = individual connection protocol.** Baptism is described as the individual entering Christ's death and resurrection — entering the SAME passage Christ opened. The sacrament renews it. Repentance activates the transfer flow. Temple extends to fullest dimensions. The ordinances are not bureaucratic — they are the mechanism by which individuals connect to the passage.

4. **2 Corinthians 12:9 (Paul's thorn) = the passage sometimes carries grace instead of transferring weight.** The thorn was not removed. Instead, grace was sufficient. This suggests the passage has multiple modes: sometimes weight transfers (substitutionary mode), sometimes grace sustains (entry/presence mode), sometimes transformation occurs (becoming mode). The passage is more versatile than either monk's single-direction model.

5. **Adjacent domain: electrical grounding.** Before the Atonement, each person is a closed circuit — all charge (weight, suffering) stays in the system. Christ's total entry creates a ground — a connection through which charge can drain. Repentance is connecting to the ground. Refusing repentance is staying isolated. The charge is real (Monk B is right). The ground must be physically connected (Monk A is right — entry creates the pathway). And the ground also enables POWER to flow in (grace, divine nature). Bidirectional flow through infrastructure.

6. **Adjacent domain: the LDS temple veil.** In temple theology, the veil separates mortal from divine. Christ's Atonement is the permanent rending of the veil — creating an opening through which traffic flows both ways. The veil ceremony itself enacts passage through a barrier from one state to another. The Atonement as permanent opening in the veil between conditions.

## 5.6 Sublation Criteria

The synthesis must:
- **Preserve from Monk A:** Entry is constitutive, not instrumental. The Incarnation is permanent. Christ is present IN suffering, not just removing it from a distance. The epistemological dimension (knowing, comprehending) is real and purposive.
- **Preserve from Monk B:** Transfer is real, not metaphorical. Weight genuinely moves. The repentant are genuinely relieved, not just accompanied. D&C 19's either/or is accurate. The substitutionary verbs in Isaiah 53 describe something that actually happens.
- **Dissolve the shared assumption:** The Atonement is not a single-direction act (either toward or away-from). It is infrastructure — a permanent bidirectional passage — through which multiple operations occur.
- **Answer the hidden question:** The Atonement created a permanent passage between divine nature and human experience. Entry created the passage. Transfer flows through it. Grace flows the other way. The covenant/ordinance system connects individuals to the passage.

### Expected Validation Responses

- Monk A should say: "Yes, entry is constitutive — it created the passage. But I was wrong that the Atonement is fundamentally about presence. The passage enables genuine transfer too. I was trapped in a unidirectional model (divine-toward-human) when the flow is bidirectional."
- Monk B should say: "Yes, transfer is real — weight genuinely moves. But I was wrong that entry is merely instrumental. Entry created the infrastructure through which transfer operates, and the infrastructure carries grace the other direction too. I was trapped in a unidirectional model (human-toward-divine) when the flow is bidirectional."
