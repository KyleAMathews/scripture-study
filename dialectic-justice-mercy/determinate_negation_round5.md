# Round 5: Determinate Negation — Unity vs. Plurality of "the Atonement"

## Pre-Synthesis Guess

"The Atonement is one Person's complete passage through the fallen condition, but 'the Atonement' as a concept is a mortal-cognitive projection that bundles distinct operations which need to be distinguished for pastoral and theological clarity — unity lives in the Person, plurality lives in the operations, and confusing the two levels is the source of the problem."

## 5.0 Internal Tensions (Self-Sublation)

### Monk A (Unity) Undermines Himself

1. **The act/eternal contradiction.** He argues the Atonement is "one continuous act" — then says it's "the permanent structure of the relationship between divine and mortal" that existed before 33 AD (Christ "slain from the foundation of the world"). An eternal structure is not an act. His own logic pushes toward something more like a *field* or *condition of existence* than an act — which dissolves the very category ("one act") he's defending.

2. **The person/mechanism contradiction.** His deepest claim is that fragmenting loses "the person behind the operations." But every analogy he reaches for is impersonal — gravity, sunlight, prisms. If the unity is fundamentally about personhood, why can't he illustrate it without depersonalizing it? His argument keeps needing the very abstraction he accuses the other side of.

3. **The unfalsifiable retreat.** Every time plurality appears at the experiential level, he retreats to "the source is one" — a metaphysical claim no human can verify from the human side. If the receptor's condition determines everything about what you experience, then "the source is one" has zero practical content. His unity becomes true and useless simultaneously.

### Monk B (Plurality) Undermines Himself

1. **The source concession.** He explicitly affirms the operations "flow from one Person, one love, one series of historical events." If they share a common source, motivation, and historical ground, what makes them *genuinely different operations* rather than *distinguishable aspects* of one work? He never draws the ontological line between "aspects" and "genuinely different things."

2. **The idol replaces an idol.** He calls "the Atonement" an idol — a human-made abstraction between you and God's actual work. But his proposed replacement (a taxonomy of twelve numbered operations) is equally an abstraction. You can't encounter "Operation 5: Sustaining Presence" any more directly than you can encounter "the Atonement." He replaces one conceptual intermediary with another and calls it progress.

3. **The pastoral argument doesn't need ontology.** His strongest case is that people expect transfer when God intends presence. But this pastoral problem could be solved by teaching that the same Person works in different ways depending on the situation — without requiring the operations to be *ontologically separate*. He treats a pedagogical problem as an ontological discovery.

## 5.1 Surface Contradiction

**Monk A:** "The Atonement is one continuous act; twelve operations are differentiated effects of a single cause."

**Monk B:** "The twelve operations are genuinely different; bundling them under one name imports false logic and creates pastoral harm."

**Apparent question:** Is "the Atonement" one thing or many things?

## 5.2 Shared Assumptions

1. **The twelve operations are real and distinguishable.** They agree on the catalog; they disagree on what it means.

2. **The operations flow from the same Person.** Even Monk B affirms this.

3. **The question is about the structure of what God does.** Both look at God's side of the equation. Neither asks what happens at the *meeting point* between God's work and the person.

4. **The human is a passive recipient.** Operations "reach" you (A) or "apply to" you (B). Neither frames the human as a constitutive participant in determining what happens. Monk A's "receptor" language and Monk B's "which operation is active?" both treat the person as a fixed surface that divine action lands on.

5. **The Atonement has a fixed ontological structure** — whether one or many — independent of the encounter. Both debate what it IS as though the answer is a stable fact about God's side alone.

**Most important shared assumption: #3.** Both ask "What did God do?" as though answering correctly will resolve everything. Neither asks: "What happens when God's work meets this particular person in this particular moment?"

## 5.3 Determinate Negation

**Monk A fails** because his unity is *explanatorily empty.* It retreats to a level of abstraction where it cannot be wrong — and therefore cannot be useful. Knowing "the source is one" tells you nothing about what's happening in any given encounter with Christ or what response is required. The unity claim does no work.

**→ What's missing:** A structural principle explaining *why* the unified reality produces the specific differentiation pattern it does — not just "different receptors" but what about the encounter determines the form.

**Monk B fails** because his plurality is *disconnected at the root.* He catalogs operations and their different logics but cannot account for what connects them. He affirms one Person, one love, one series of events — then insists on "genuinely different operations" without explaining where the ontological boundary is.

**→ What's missing:** An account of what makes encounters with Christ's forgiveness, Christ's succoring, and Christ's resurrection feel like encounters with the *same reality* to the person experiencing them.

**The failures are complementary:**
- A sees unity but can't explain differentiation
- B sees differentiation but can't explain unity
- Each monk's blind spot is precisely what the other can partially see

## 5.4 The Hidden Question

Both monks ask: "What is the structure of the Atonement?" — as though it has a fixed structure (one or many) independent of the encounter.

**The hidden question:** What determines the form that Christ's redemptive work takes in any particular encounter between God and a person?

Neither monk can answer this because both look only at God's side. The mechanism of encounter — the meeting point where divine initiative meets human condition — is invisible to both.

## 5.5 Boydian Decomposition

### Generic Space

Both positions share: "Christ did X → X has structure Y (one/many) → Y implies Z about how we should think/teach." Both assume the right *conceptual representation* will solve the problems.

### Atomic Components (stripped of source)

1. Christ's passage through the fallen condition as one continuous motion
2. Multiple effects following different rules from single cause (gravity analogy)
3. Alma 42:23 causal chain — one source, cascading effects
4. Receptor determines which effects apply (involuntary → universal; volitional → conditional)
5. Orthodox Paschal Mystery as indivisible event
6. "Slain from the foundation of the world" — eternal, not merely historical
7. Fragmenting loses the person behind the operations
8. Ostler: "atonement is happening all the time"
9. Twelve operations with six different logics
10. Alma 11:41 — scripture carving off resurrection as universal minimum
11. Three Hebrew terms from three social worlds (temple, household, battlefield)
12. Historical contingency of the unified concept
13. Pastoral harm from false expectations (expecting transfer, getting presence)
14. D&C 76 graduated kingdoms
15. Benjamin vs. Abinadi as competing atonement theologies within one text
16. "The Atonement" as idol — abstraction between person and God's actual work
17. Mode-selection becomes tractable when you unbundle

### Cross-Domain Connections

**Connection A: Components 7 + 16 — "Losing the person" and "idol" are the same worry from opposite sides.** Monk A fears twelve operations will lose the person. Monk B fears the unified concept has become an idol obscuring the person's actual work. Both are worried about *abstraction replacing living encounter.* Both are right — both kinds of abstraction ("one Atonement" AND "twelve operations") can stand between a person and the living encounter with Christ.

**Connection B: Components 6 + 8 + 13 — Eternal structure + always happening + pastoral harm.** If the Atonement is eternal and always-happening (not a completed past act), then the pastoral question isn't "which operation from 33 AD is being applied to me?" but "what is Christ doing with me *right now*?" The question shifts from taxonomy of past acts to discernment of present encounter.

**Connection C: Component 11 + 5 — Hebrew social worlds + Paschal Mystery.** The Hebrew terms came from different social worlds because those were the *contexts in which Israel encountered divine rescue.* Temple → purification encounter. Household → kinship-redemption encounter. Battlefield → deliverance encounter. The plurality isn't in God's action — it's in the **contexts of encounter.** Like knowing the same person differently as spouse, parent, and colleague — not three people, but three encounter-contexts revealing different dimensions of one person.

**Connection D: Component 4 + 9 — Receptor determines effects + different logics.** If the receptor's condition determines which effects apply, and those effects follow different logics, then the logic isn't inherent to "the operation" — it's inherent to the *type of encounter.* Involuntary condition → universal logic. Volitional condition → conditional logic. Ongoing limitation → progressive logic. The logic tracks the encounter-type, not separate operations.

### Adjacent-Domain Material

**Buber's I-Thou:** The fundamental reality is not a thing (with a structure to analyze) but a *meeting* (whose form is shaped by both parties).

**D&C 88:40:** "Intelligence cleaveth unto intelligence; wisdom receiveth wisdom; truth embraceth truth." A resonance principle — what manifests in the encounter depends on what you bring to it.

**Alma 32:** One seed (from one source), but the fruit depends on the encounter between seed and soil — the person's particular tending, their particular ground.

### Emergent Structure

The idea that the *encounter* is the unit of analysis has organizational properties in neither input:
- Not "one source, many effects" (A) — because it gives the human condition a constitutive role
- Not "many operations" (B) — because it doesn't carve the work into separate acts
- Introduces *co-determination:* the form of each encounter is shaped by what both parties bring
- This structural principle doesn't trace back to either monk

## 5.6 Sublation Criteria

The synthesis must:
- **Preserve from A:** Unity of Christ's person and work — one Person, one love, one passage
- **Preserve from B:** Genuine plurality of manifestation and pastoral need for specificity — different encounters follow different logics because they address different human conditions
- **Dissolve:** The shared assumption that the Atonement has a fixed ontological structure (one or many) independent of the encounter between Christ and the person
- **Answer the hidden question:** What determines the form? → The *meeting* of Christ's fullness with the person's specific condition in that moment
