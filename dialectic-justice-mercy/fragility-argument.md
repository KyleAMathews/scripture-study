# The Perpetual Fragility of Spiritual Development

## Electric Monk B: Agency as the Irreducible Ground

---

## I. What Agency IS

The irreversibility thesis misidentifies what is most fundamental about persons. It treats the rhythm's accumulated trust and knowledge as though they settle into the bedrock of the soul, becoming part of what the person IS. But it has the ontology backwards. The bedrock is not trust, not knowledge, not even character. The bedrock is agency -- and agency is not a feature of persons but the constitutive principle of their existence.

D&C 93:29-30 is the most metaphysically radical passage in the canon: "Intelligence, or the light of truth, was not created or made, neither indeed can be. All truth is independent in that sphere in which God has placed it, to act for itself, as all intelligence also; otherwise there is no existence." That final clause -- "otherwise there is no existence" -- is the load-bearing claim. Remove the capacity to act, and you do not get a diminished person. You get nothing at all. Agency is not a gift God bestowed on pre-existing beings. Agency is what makes them beings in the first place. It is co-eternal with God. It precedes spirit birth, embodiment, mortality, covenants, the rhythm, theosis -- all of it. Every structure the plan of salvation erects is built atop this foundation, and no structure built atop a foundation can retroactively fuse with it.

Moses 4:3 confirms the point from the negative direction: Satan "sought to destroy the agency of man, which I, the Lord God, had given him." God's response was not correction or rehabilitation. It was war. Satan was cast out of heaven -- the most extreme punishment in the entire pre-mortal narrative -- for proposing to override agency. If even God treats the destruction of agency as the one intolerable act, then any theology that posits a transformation deep enough to functionally override future choosing has unwittingly joined Satan's program. The irreversibility thesis does not intend this, but it cannot escape it.

## II. The Irreversibility Thesis at Its Strongest

My opponent's best case: The rhythm does not merely deposit knowledge and trust into a person; it reconstitutes the person. Each completed cycle of orientation-risk-recognition-calibration changes what the agent IS, such that reversal is not a return to a prior state but a corruption of something ontologically new. The sons of perdition confirm this -- they cannot become what they become without having first been deeply transformed. Their perdition is not mere ignorance restored but knowledge turned against itself, light become darkness. The ratchet is real because the person who completed the cycle is not the same person who began it. To "unchoose" what you have become is categorically different from never having chosen it. The rhythm writes itself into the structure of the soul.

I know this argument. I understand its appeal. It offers the comfort of permanence in a theology that otherwise makes everything contingent. And it correctly observes that a transformed person's fall looks different from an untransformed person's stasis. I will now show why it fails.

## III. The Precise Failure

The irreversibility thesis confuses the *consequences* of falling with the *nature* of falling. Yes, a person who falls after deep spiritual development suffers differently than one who never developed. Alma 24:30 says exactly this: "after a people have been once enlightened by the Spirit of God, and have had great knowledge of things pertaining to righteousness, and then have fallen away into sin and transgression, they become more hardened, and thus their state becomes worse than though they had never known these things." The Amalekites' fall was worse. Their hardening was deeper. Their suffering was greater. All of this is true.

But "worse" is a difference of *degree*, not *kind*. The irreversibility thesis needs it to be a difference of kind -- needs the fall of the enlightened to be a metaphysically distinct event, a "corruption of permanent change" rather than a cessation of ongoing choice. And scripture refuses to grant this. It says their state becomes *worse than though they had never known these things* -- comparing it directly to the state of those who never knew. Same axis. Different position. The comparison itself proves the underlying continuity.

Here is the deeper failure: the irreversibility thesis treats character as substance when it is actually activity. It imagines that the rhythm deposits something INTO the person -- a changed nature, a new ontological layer -- that then exists independently of the ongoing choosing that produced it. But this is exactly backwards. What looks like "transformed character" is the visible shape of ongoing choosing. Character is not a thing you have; it is a thing you are doing. The moment the doing ceases, the character does not "corrupt" -- it simply stops. A fire that goes out has not "corrupted"; it has ceased to be fed.

The irreversibility thesis needs character to be more like a scar -- a permanent structural change to tissue -- than like a fire. But agency-as-constitutive means persons are not tissue. They are not substance that can be permanently marked. They are activity. They are the act of choosing, irreducibly and at every moment. The rhythm does not write itself into the soul because the soul is not a surface that accepts writing. The soul IS the writing -- ongoing, voluntary, and stoppable.

## IV. Why Fragility Is the Deeper Principle

Every gain the rhythm produces -- every increment of trust, every deepening of knowledge, every expansion of capacity -- is real. I am not diminishing the rhythm's power. I am saying that its power is the power of a living relationship, not the power of an ontological stamp. And living relationships require ongoing participation from both parties.

D&C 93:28 says: "He that keepeth his commandments receiveth truth and light, until he is glorified in truth and knoweth all things." Note the verb: *keepeth*. Present tense. Continuous aspect. Not "he that kept" or "he that once received." The compounding of truth and light is conditioned on ongoing keeping. The verse does not describe a process that, once started, completes itself. It describes a process that requires sustained agency at every point along the trajectory.

D&C 84:41 makes the fragility explicit even at the highest covenant level: "whoso breaketh this covenant after he hath received it, and altogether turneth therefrom, shall not have forgiveness of sins in this world nor in the world to come." This is not describing a theoretical impossibility. It is warning against an actual possibility. The oath and covenant of the priesthood -- the highest covenant structure in the theology -- can be broken. If even this can be broken, there is no ratchet.

And the pre-mortal evidence is devastating to the irreversibility thesis. One-third of the hosts of heaven -- beings who lived in God's presence, who knew Him directly, who had whatever spiritual development the pre-mortal state affords -- chose to follow Lucifer. They did not fall from ignorance. They fell from knowledge. They fell from *presence*. If dwelling in God's literal presence for an unspecified duration of pre-mortal existence does not produce an irreversible ontological transformation, then nothing in mortality's rhythm will either.

## V. The Uncomfortable Conclusion

If I am right, there is no safe harbor. Not calling and election sure, not the second comforter, not theosis itself. At every point along the trajectory, the person must continue to choose. The rhythm compounds trust and knowledge, and that compounding makes continued choosing progressively more natural, more aligned with the person's experienced reality, more deeply what they *want* to do. I grant all of this. But "progressively more natural" is not "inevitable." The gap between 99.99% likely and metaphysically certain is the gap between a living being and a mechanism. Close that gap and you have not perfected the person -- you have replaced them with an automaton.

2 Nephi 2:27 is the architecture of the whole plan: men "are free to choose liberty and eternal life, through the great Mediator of all men, or to choose captivity and death." Free *to choose*. Not free until they have chosen enough times that the choosing becomes unnecessary. The freedom does not expire. It does not graduate into something higher than itself. Freedom IS the highest thing. Theosis is not the end of choosing; it is choosing perfected -- sustained, voluntary, moment-by-moment alignment with God's will, maintained forever because the person forever chooses it, not because they have been rendered incapable of choosing otherwise.

This means God Himself, on the LDS account, maintains His godhood by ongoing choice. "I, the Lord, am bound when ye do what I say" (D&C 82:10) -- bound by His own integrity, not by metaphysical necessity. If God's perfection were ontologically locked rather than perpetually chosen, His faithfulness would not be praiseworthy. It would be mechanical. The glory of God is that He *could* be otherwise and never is. And the trajectory of theosis is to become like that -- not to have choice removed, but to have it perfected into the perpetual, voluntary, fully informed fidelity that characterizes divine life.

## VI. The Inferential Skeleton

1. **Premise:** Intelligence is co-eternal with God and constitutively agentive (D&C 93:29-30). Agency is not a feature of persons but the ground of their existence.
2. **Premise:** No transformation, however deep, can override the constitutive ground of a being without annihilating the being (Moses 4:3 -- the attempt to destroy agency warrants the most extreme response in the canon).
3. **Inference:** Therefore the rhythm's gains -- trust, knowledge, character -- exist as ongoing expressions of agency, not as deposits that supersede it.
4. **Premise:** What exists as an ongoing expression of agency requires ongoing agency to sustain (D&C 93:28 -- "keepeth," present tense, continuous).
5. **Premise:** Scripture confirms that even the highest covenants can be broken (D&C 84:41) and that beings with direct knowledge of God have chosen rebellion (the pre-mortal fall, the sons of perdition).
6. **Conclusion:** The rhythm's compounding is perpetually fragile. Every gain is real but every gain requires ongoing choosing to sustain. There is no ratchet, no ontological lock, no point at which the person's agency becomes functionally irrelevant to their continued development.

The fragility is not a flaw in the system. It is the system. It is what distinguishes a cosmos of persons from a cosmos of things. It is what makes love possible, what makes faithfulness praiseworthy, what makes theosis an achievement rather than an inevitability. The rhythm's perpetual fragility is not the threat to spiritual development. It is the condition without which spiritual development is not development at all but mere mechanism wearing the mask of growth.
