# Round 3 Determinate Negation: Union vs. Conquest

## 5.1 Surface Contradiction

Monk A (Union): The Atonement's fundamental shape is relational entry — Christ descending into, comprehending, and permanently bonding with the human condition. Death/hell/devil are privations (consequences of separation) that dissolve when union is established. "Light arrives and darkness ceases."

Monk B (Conquest): The Atonement's fundamental shape is cosmological victory — Christ defeating death, hell, and the devil as real structural enemies that held creation captive. Union is what becomes possible after the conquest clears the ground. "You cannot succor a prisoner still in chains."

## 5.2 Shared Assumptions

1. **Both assume union and conquest are different KINDS of acts.** Union is relational (entering into, bonding with). Conquest is military (defeating, breaking, destroying). These are treated as categorically distinct operations that could not be the same act. One must be primary; the other derivative.

2. **Both assume the "enemies" are either internal or external to the human condition, but not both.** Monk A says enemies are mere privations (internal — just consequences of separation). Monk B says enemies are independent structural powers (external — real forces with real agency that must be independently defeated). Neither considers that the enemies might be real, powerful, actively destructive forces that are NEVERTHELESS intrinsic to the fallen condition rather than external to it.

3. **Both assume the Atonement operates on a field with distinct, pre-defined participants: God, humanity, and (optionally) enemies.** The "battlefield" is conceived as a space between these parties. Neither asks whether the battlefield and the beloved might be the same thing.

## 5.3 Determinate Negation

### Where Monk A (Union) Specifically Fails

1. **The privation argument is too weak for Jacob's language.** Monk A elegantly argues that death and hell are "not ontologically independent" — they're mere absences, like darkness. But Jacob doesn't describe an absence. He describes a MONSTER with a GRASP from which God must prepare an ESCAPE (2 Nephi 9:10). Monk A must euphemize this: "that's what separation looks like from the outside." But a privation doesn't grasp. An absence doesn't devour. Jacob's phenomenology demands more ontological weight for the enemies than Monk A's framework can grant.

2. **"Light arrives and darkness ceases" can't explain the agony.** If the entry is sufficient — if union inherently dissolves the enemies — why did the entry hurt so much? Monk B's challenge stands unanswered: if the fundamental purpose were relational entry, painless incarnation should suffice for gaining experiential knowledge. Christ could have lived a human life, experienced limitation, and returned with empathy. The blood from every pore suggests something is being ABSORBED, not merely ENTERED INTO.

3. **The asymmetry argument ("union explains every conquest passage") is overstated.** Monk A says bands break "when what they held apart is joined." But that describes the RESULT, not the MECHANISM. How does the act of joining produce the breaking? What force does the joining exert on the bands? The union reading describes what things look like after the enemies are gone but doesn't explain the actual process of their defeat.

### Where Monk B (Conquest) Specifically Fails

1. **The mechanism problem remains fatal.** Monk B says Christ "absorbed their full force and still standing." But this is endurance, not fighting. Absorbing a blow is not a military strategy — it's what you do when you can't avoid the blow. And endurance through suffering looks structurally identical to... entering into suffering, which is Monk A's territory. The conquest reading uses military language to describe an act whose actual mechanism (absorbing, enduring, bearing) is relational.

2. **"Peacetime theology" critique cuts against Christ Himself.** If calling the Atonement relational is "peacetime theology that forgot the war," what do we say about Christ's own descriptions of His ongoing work? Good Shepherd, Vine and Branches, Bridegroom, "I stand at the door and knock" — Christ Himself uses relational language for His ongoing Atonement-derived work. Calling this "peacetime theology" requires saying Christ Himself forgot the war He just won.

3. **The "pain proves conquest" argument has a gap.** Not all agonizing acts are combat. The pain of childbirth is not military. The pain of total empathy at infinite scale — experiencing every human grief, sin, and death simultaneously — would be agonizing without any external enemy. Monk B assumes that because the Atonement hurt, it must have been a fight. But the Atonement might hurt because the SCOPE of what was being entered into is that terrible — not because an enemy was striking blows.

## 5.4 The Hidden Question

Not "Is the Atonement a union or a conquest?"

But: **What if the "enemies" (death, hell, devil's power) are not external to the human condition but are the structural pathologies WITHIN it — real, powerful, actively destructive, but intrinsic to fallenness rather than independent of it? If so, then total entry into the fallen condition IS total encounter with the enemies. The union IS the conquest because the battlefield IS the beloved.**

## 5.5 Boydian Decomposition

### Atomic Parts (unstructured)

- Christ descended below ALL things (D&C 88:6) — totality of the descent
- The purpose of descent: "that he might be in all and through all things" — permeation
- The purpose of suffering: to gain capacity to succor (Alma 7:12)
- Alma 7:12 has TWO purposes in sequence: loose bands THEN succor
- The resurrection = permanent union of spirit and body (D&C 93:33-34, Alma 11:45)
- 2 Nephi 9 counterfactual: without Atonement, bodies rot permanently, spirits become devil's captives
- The "monster death and hell" — active, grasping, devouring
- "Bands of death," "chains of hell" — constraint language
- Alma the Younger: "snatched" from the abyss (Mosiah 27:29)
- Christ's pain was real and necessary — but the CAUSE is disputed
- "Absorbing the full force" = endurance, not combat (Monk B's mechanism)
- Light arrives, darkness ceases (Monk A's mechanism)
- John 17: Christ prays for oneness before the Atonement begins
- Hebrews 2:14: Christ took on flesh to destroy the devil — mechanism is PARTICIPATION, not combat
- 2 Nephi 2:26-27: redemption from fall → freedom → capacity to choose
- Mosiah 15:9: breaking bands, gaining victory, THEN filled with compassion
- D&C 19:16-17: the unrepentant "must suffer even as I" — the suffering has transferable weight
- The devil is a literal being in LDS theology — not merely a symbol of absence
- "Condescension" = con-descent, coming down with
- Both monks agree Christ is alive and currently operating

### Cross-Domain Connections

1. **Hebrews 2:14 is the Rosetta Stone.** Both monks cite it. Christ entered flesh "that through death he might destroy him that had the power of death." The mechanism of destruction IS participation. He entered into the condition WHERE the enemy operates, and the entry itself is what destroyed the enemy. This is neither pure union (it's destructive) nor pure conquest (the weapon is entry, not force).

2. **Alma 7:12's two purposes are not sequential but simultaneous.** Monk B reads them as ordered: first liberation, then succoring. But the grammar doesn't require temporal sequence — it may describe two aspects of the same act. Taking upon him death (entering into mortality, including its end) IS what looses the bands (because a divine being entering death and emerging proves death is not permanent). Taking upon him infirmities IS what creates the capacity to succor (because experiential knowledge enables empathy). The entry into death IS BOTH the loosing of bands AND the acquisition of empathic capacity — simultaneously.

3. **Adjacent domain: immunology.** An immune response works by antibodies BINDING TO (uniting with) the pathogen, which NEUTRALIZES (conquers) it. The binding IS the neutralization. The fever/inflammation (pain) is the cost of the engagement itself, not evidence of a separate military operation. The immune system doesn't fight from outside — it enters, binds, and neutralizes in a single act.

4. **Adjacent domain: LDS birth/begetting language.** Mosiah 5:7: "ye shall be called the children of Christ, his sons, and his daughters; for behold, this day he hath spiritually begotten you." Mosiah 15:3: Christ is "the Father" because of the Atonement. Birth is simultaneously union (profound connection between mother and child), conquest (overcoming the barrier between unborn and born), and agonizing — not because of an enemy but because of the magnitude of what is being accomplished. The Atonement as birth: Christ laboring to bring humanity through death into new life.

## 5.6 Sublation Criteria

The synthesis must:
- **Preserve from Monk A:** The fundamental act is entry/descent/comprehension. The relational dimension (succoring, empathy, ongoing operation) is not secondary.
- **Preserve from Monk B:** The enemies are REAL — not mere privations or metaphors for distance. Death actively holds. Hell actively imprisons. The Atonement actually defeats them at real cost.
- **Dissolve the shared assumption:** Union and conquest are not different kinds of acts requiring prioritization. They are the same act, because the enemies are intrinsic to the condition being entered.
- **Answer the hidden question:** The battlefield IS the beloved. Total entry into the fallen condition IS total encounter with the enemies, because the enemies LIVE WITHIN the condition, not outside it.

### Expected Validation Responses

- Monk A should say: "Yes, the enemies are real — not mere privations. I was wrong to downplay them. But the entry is still the mechanism, and the synthesis shows WHY entry defeats them: because they're intrinsic to the condition entered."
- Monk B should say: "Yes, the fundamental act is entry, not combat in the military sense. But the entry is genuinely destructive of real enemies — it's not just empathy acquisition. The synthesis preserves the real cost and the real victory."
