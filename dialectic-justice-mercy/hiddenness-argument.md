# The Encounter Is Structurally Opaque: Hiddenness as the Condition of Faith

## The Ontological Claim

The encounter between Christ and the person is opaque to the one experiencing it. Not incidentally, not as a temporary inconvenience on the way to clarity, but structurally -- as a constitutive feature of what the encounter IS during mortality. The opacity is not a defect in the signal or a limitation of the receiver. It is the architecture of the relationship between an infinite God and a finite person who is still in the process of becoming.

Here is what the opacity means structurally: the Round 5 synthesis established that the encounter-form is determined by the person's actual condition, not their self-diagnosis or request. God reads the condition. The person brings it. But the person's condition is precisely the thing the person cannot accurately see. The condition includes the person's blindness to their own condition. This is not a peripheral problem. It is the problem. The instrument that would need to assess the encounter -- the person's self-understanding -- is itself part of what God is responding to, and is itself distorted by the Fall. You cannot use a warped mirror to check whether the mirror is warped.

Isaiah states the principle with a spatial metaphor whose literalness is often missed: "For as the heavens are higher than the earth, so are my ways higher than your ways, and my thoughts than your thoughts" ([Isaiah 55:8-9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/55.8)). This is not a polite acknowledgment that God is smarter. It is a claim about dimensional incommensurability. The distance between divine thought and human thought is the distance between the heavens and the earth -- a gap that cannot be closed from below. The person inside the encounter cannot see what God sees, because seeing what God sees would require being what God is. And the person is not that. Not yet.

What would be lost if the encounter were fully transparent? Everything. If the person could see exactly what God was doing, exactly why, exactly what form the encounter was taking and what it required -- the encounter would collapse from a living relationship into a mechanism. The person would not need faith. They would not need trust. They would not need to submit to anything, because they could evaluate the divine action from outside it and decide whether to cooperate based on their own assessment. Transparent encounter is indistinguishable from a transaction: I see the terms, I agree or decline. Mosiah 3:19 does not describe a transaction. It describes becoming "as a child, submissive, meek, humble, patient, full of love, willing to submit to all things which the Lord seeth fit to inflict upon him, even as a child doth submit to his father" ([Mosiah 3:19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/3.19)). "All things which the Lord seeth fit to inflict" -- the phrase presupposes that the person does not see the fitness of the infliction. The Lord sees it. The person submits. The asymmetry is the point.

## My Opponent's Strongest Case

My opponent argues that God has embedded in every person a recognition faculty -- the Light of Christ, the Spirit of Christ -- that is tuned to detect divine action. Moroni 7:16 is the anchor: "the Spirit of Christ is given to every man, that he may know good from evil." My opponent takes this as a structural claim about the architecture of intelligence itself: every person comes equipped with a receiver calibrated to the divine frequency.

My opponent points to Alma 32's seed experiment as a recognition protocol with specific phenomenological markers: the seed "beginneth to enlarge my soul; yea, it beginneth to enlighten my understanding, yea, it beginneth to be delicious to me" ([Alma 32:28](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/32.28)). These markers are not ambiguous, my opponent says. They announce themselves. And Alma 32:35 drives it home: "it is light; and whatsoever is light, is good, because it is discernible, therefore ye must know that it is good."

My opponent grants that the form of the encounter is unpredictable -- Paul was surprised -- but insists that the encounter itself is recognizable while it is happening. Paul knew God had spoken. The disciples on the road to Emmaus knew their hearts burned. The people of King Benjamin knew they had been changed. Unpredictability of form is not opacity of encounter, my opponent says. You can be surprised by what someone says and still know they are speaking.

The strongest version of this case: the recognition faculty and the divine signal are made of the same substance -- Spirit recognizes Spirit, light cleaves to light (D&C 88:40). Because the receiver and the signal share a common nature, recognition is structurally inevitable. Every encounter with Christ announces itself to the person it touches. The sheep know his voice.

This is a serious and scripturally grounded argument. I state it in the strongest terms my opponent would endorse.

## Why It Fails at the Structural Level

It fails because it confuses two different things: the capacity to detect that something is happening with the capacity to understand what is happening and what it means.

Moroni 7:16 says the Spirit of Christ enables you to "know good from evil." That is a binary discrimination. It is not an encounter-reading protocol. Knowing that something is good does not mean knowing what God is doing, why, what condition He is responding to, or what the encounter requires of you. The Light of Christ tells you: this is from God. It does not tell you: God is giving you sustaining presence rather than transfer because your actual condition is weakness, not unforgiven sin, and what He requires of you is patient endurance rather than repentance. My opponent's own key passage -- Alma 32 -- proves this. The seed experiment tells you the seed is good. It does not tell you what kind of tree it will become, when it will fruit, or what the fruit will taste like. Alma is explicit: "after ye have tasted this light is your knowledge perfect?" ([Alma 32:35](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/32.35)). No. The knowledge is not perfect. The seed is real. The recognition that something divine is happening is real. But the meaning, the trajectory, the form -- these remain opaque.

Paul's case proves my point, not my opponent's. My opponent says Paul knew God had spoken. Granted. But Paul asked three times for removal. Three times. Paul's self-diagnosis of his own condition was wrong, and he did not know it was wrong until God told him. The encounter was opaque to Paul in every way that matters pastorally: he did not know what form it was taking (presence, not transfer), why (his condition called for it), or what it required of him (glory in weakness, not petition for relief). Knowing that God was speaking while being wrong about everything God was saying is not transparency. It is opacity dressed in the clothing of contact.

The most devastating case is D&C 121. Joseph Smith in Liberty Jail was as close to God as any person alive. He had conversed with the Father and the Son face to face. He had translated scripture by the gift and power of God. And from that jail he cried: "O God, where art thou? And where is the pavilion that covereth thy hiding place?" ([D&C 121:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/121.1)). This is not a man who lacks the recognition faculty. This is a prophet with the recognition faculty developed to the highest mortal degree, and the encounter is opaque to him. He cannot see what God is doing. He cannot detect God's presence. The pavilion -- a covering -- blocks his view. God's answer, when it comes, does not explain what is happening. It says: "My son, peace be unto thy soul; thine adversity and thine afflictions shall be but a small moment" ([D&C 121:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/121.7)). God offers presence, not explanation. The opacity remains. Joseph must trust without seeing.

Job 38-42 is the supreme scriptural statement of structural opacity. Job has demanded an explanation. He wants to know why. God answers -- and the answer is not an explanation. It is an overwhelming display of divine scale: "Where wast thou when I laid the foundations of the earth?" ([Job 38:4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/job/38.4)). For four chapters God inventories the cosmos. Not one word of it addresses Job's question. God does not explain what He was doing in Job's suffering. He reveals the incommensurability between divine action and human comprehension. And Job's response is not "Now I understand." It is: "I have heard of thee by the hearing of the ear: but now mine eye seeth thee" ([Job 42:5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/job/42.5)). Job does not gain transparency. He gains encounter. The encounter replaces the need for transparency. He sees God. He never sees the reason.

## What My Opponent Is Actually Seeing

My opponent is pointing at real phenomena. The Spirit's witness is real. The burning in the bosom is real. Alma 32's seed experiment produces real experiential markers. Moroni 7's discernment criteria work.

But these phenomena are not evidence of transparency. They are evidence of *contact*. They tell you that God is present. They tell you that something real is happening. They tell you that the source is divine, not counterfeit. What they do not tell you is what God is doing, why, what condition He is reading in you, or what the encounter requires of you next.

Contact is not comprehension. A child held by a parent in the dark knows they are held. They do not know why the lights are off, how long the dark will last, or what the parent is doing about it. The warmth of the arms is real information -- it tells the child they are not alone. But it does not make the dark transparent. The entire structure of faith depends on this distinction.

This is precisely what Nephi understood: "I know that he loveth his children; nevertheless, I do not know the meaning of all things" ([1 Nephi 11:17](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/11.17)). Contact: "I know that he loveth his children." Opacity: "I do not know the meaning of all things." Nephi does not experience these as contradictory. He holds them simultaneously. Love is known. Meaning is hidden. The encounter is real and opaque at the same time.

## The Extreme Claim

Here is the strongest, most uncomfortable version of my thesis:

The encounter with Christ is structurally opaque to the one inside it, and this opacity is permanent for as long as mortality lasts. It does not diminish with spiritual maturity. It does not thin as you progress along the encounter-spiral. The forms of opacity change -- a new convert is confused about whether God is speaking at all; a prophet is confused about why God is silent when he should be speaking -- but the opacity itself is a constant of the mortal condition.

The evidence is in the text. Ether 12:6 does not say "ye receive no witness until you are spiritually mature enough to perceive it." It says "ye receive no witness until after the trial of your faith." The witness is structurally deferred -- withheld until the trial is over. This is not a deficiency to be overcome by better discernment training. This is the operating system. Faith requires acting without the witness. The witness comes after the act. If you could recognize the encounter in real time with full clarity, you would not need faith, and faith is the entire point: "we walk by faith, not by sight" ([2 Corinthians 5:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/2-cor/5.7)). Paul does not say we walk by faith until we get good enough at sight. He says faith IS the mode of walking.

President Nelson's account of Jill demonstrates this with pastoral specificity. Jill wanted healing for her dying father. The encounter-form she received was one word: *myopic*. She was devastated. She could not recognize what God was doing. She expected intercession and got what felt like rebuke. Only after her father passed did the word begin to work in her -- not as explanation but as reorientation. "Myopic caused me to stop, think, and heal. That word now fills me with peace." The encounter was real the moment she received it. She could not recognize what it was until long afterward. The opacity was not a failure of her spiritual development. It was the mechanism by which the encounter did its work. Had she understood instantly, she would have processed the word intellectually and moved on. The opacity forced her to carry it, wrestle with it, and let it reshape her over time. The hiddenness was the delivery system.

If I am right, then teaching people to "recognize the encounter-form" is not merely unhelpful -- it is a new species of the original pastoral problem. The Round 5 synthesis identified the danger of any conceptual intermediary standing between the person and the living Christ. A taxonomy of encounter-forms, taught as a recognition protocol, becomes exactly such an intermediary. The person learns to ask "Am I in the presence mode or the transfer mode?" instead of crying out "O God, where art thou?" -- and the cry is closer to God than the taxonomy.

## The Reasoning Skeleton

1. The encounter-form is determined by the person's actual condition, not their self-diagnosis (Round 5 synthesis, established).
2. The person's self-understanding of their own condition is itself distorted by the Fall -- the condition includes the inability to see the condition clearly.
3. **Therefore, the very thing that determines the encounter-form is the thing the person cannot see.** THIS IS THE LOAD-BEARING CLAIM.
4. The recognition faculty (Light of Christ, Spirit) provides contact -- awareness that God is present, that the source is divine -- but not comprehension of what God is doing, why, or what the encounter requires.
5. Scripture consistently shows the witness arriving after the trial (Ether 12:6), not during it. The structural sequence is: act in faith, receive the encounter, understand later (or not at all).
6. This opacity is theologically necessary: without it, faith collapses into sight, trust collapses into evaluation, and the developmental environment of mortality loses its purpose.
7. The opacity changes in form but not in degree across the encounter-spiral. Prophets are as structurally unable to see what God is doing as new converts -- they are confused about different things at a higher register (D&C 121:1).
8. Teaching encounter-form recognition reproduces the expectation problem the encounter model was designed to dissolve.

Claim 3 is structurally load-bearing. If it falls -- if the person CAN see their own condition clearly enough to anticipate the encounter-form -- then the opacity dissolves and my opponent wins. But the entire canon of scripture about the Fall, the natural man, the veil, and the necessity of faith testifies that mortal self-knowledge is radically incomplete. "Now we see through a glass, darkly" ([1 Corinthians 13:12](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/1-cor/13.12)). Not "now we see through a glass, slightly smudged." Darkly. The glass does not clear until "face to face" -- which is the eschaton, not a midlife achievement. Every person who has stood before God in scripture and thought they understood what was happening was wrong about something essential. Every single one.

## Scriptural Grounding Summary

- **Ether 12:6**: The witness comes after the trial. Opacity is the operating condition of faith, not a bug to be patched.
- **Isaiah 55:8-9**: The dimensional gap between divine and human cognition is as vast as heaven is above earth. Self-understanding cannot bridge it.
- **D&C 121:1-7**: The most developed prophet of the dispensation cannot detect God's presence or action during his extremity. God answers with presence, not explanation.
- **Job 38-42**: God's response to the demand for transparency is not explanation but overwhelming encounter. Job sees God and stops asking why.
- **1 Corinthians 13:12**: Mortal perception is structurally dark. Clarity comes face to face -- an eschatological reality, not a developmental milestone.
- **2 Corinthians 12:7-9**: Paul's self-diagnosis was wrong. He could not see his own condition. God responded to what was actually there, not to Paul's interpretation.
- **Mosiah 3:19**: Submission to "all things which the Lord seeth fit to inflict" presupposes the person cannot see the fitness. The Lord sees. The person submits.
- **1 Nephi 11:17**: Nephi holds contact and opacity simultaneously: "I know that he loveth his children; nevertheless, I do not know the meaning of all things."
- **Alma 32**: The seed experiment proves contact (the seed is good, the source is divine), not comprehension (what God is doing, why, or what comes next). Alma explicitly asks: "is your knowledge perfect?" No.
- **President Nelson / Jill**: The encounter-form ("myopic") was unrecognizable to Jill in the moment. The opacity was the delivery system, not a failure of discernment.
