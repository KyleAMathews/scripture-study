# The Atonement Is One Act: A Committed Argument for Unity

## The Ontological Claim

What IS the Atonement? It is Christ's total passage through the fallen condition -- a single, unrepeatable structural event in which divine nature entered, inhabited, and emerged from the full scope of mortality, death, sin, hell, and embodied limitation. Everything else -- resurrection, forgiveness, sanctification, succoring, the making of weak things strong, the filling with hope and perfect love -- is consequence. The twelve operations cataloged in our prior analysis are real. Their different rules are real. But they are twelve effects of one cause, twelve colors refracted through one prism, twelve rivers flowing from one underground spring. To treat them as twelve separate acts is to mistake the refractions for the light.

The word itself tells the truth that the analysis obscures: at-one-ment. Not at-twelve-ments. The English word Tyndale coined to translate *katallagē* carries in its morphology the very claim I am making. Spackman is right that Hebrew *kaphar*, *ga'al*, and *yasha* referred to distinct operations -- ritual purification, kinship obligation, military victory. But Tyndale was not wrong to unify them. He was seeing something the ancient categories, in their necessary concreteness, could not say: that behind the priest purging the altar, the kinsman buying back the slave, and the warrior routing the enemy stands one divine intention expressing itself through every available cultural form. The plurality is in the human reception. The unity is in the divine act.

This is what the Orthodox tradition understands when it speaks of the Paschal Mystery as a single event. Christ's incarnation, suffering, death, descent into hell, and resurrection are not five sequential acts that happen to occur in the same week. They are one continuous divine motion -- God entering fully into the condition of creation and emerging with creation permanently joined to Him. The liturgy does not commemorate five discrete moments; it makes present the entirety of Christ's paschal work as one indivisible reality. The *triduum mortis* -- those three days in the tomb -- constitutes the mystical space in which the Resurrection is already being accomplished, not a gap between two separate operations.

## The Opponent's Strongest Case

The plurality advocate has formidable evidence, and I must state it in terms they would endorse.

They observe that twelve distinguishable operations follow radically different rules. Resurrection is universal, unconditional, automatic -- it applies to every human being who has ever lived regardless of faith, repentance, or covenant status. Forgiveness requires a broken heart, a contrite spirit, and active repentance -- "unto none else can the ends of the law be answered" ([2 Nephi 2:7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/2.7)). Exaltation demands a lifetime of covenant-keeping and maximal moral effort. These are not different shades of the same thing. They are structurally different operations with structurally different conditions.

The plurality advocate's sharpest weapon is Alma 11:41: "the wicked remain as though there had been no redemption made, except it be the loosing of the bands of death." This verse explicitly carves resurrection off as the universal minimum and says that for the wicked, the rest of the Atonement effectively does not exist. If the Atonement were genuinely one act producing one unified effect, this carve-out would be incoherent. You cannot carve a piece off of something that is structurally indivisible.

They also point to D&C 76's graduated kingdoms, where different people receive categorically different post-resurrection outcomes -- celestial, terrestrial, telestial -- calibrated to what law they were able to "abide." If the Atonement is one act, why does it produce a spectrum? And they point to Spencer's finding that the Book of Mormon itself contains multiple competing atonement theologies -- Benjamin's monarchical blood-theology versus Abinadi's divine-descent theology -- suggesting that even the scriptural authors did not share a single unified concept.

This is a powerful case. I do not wave it away.

## Why the Plurality Reading Fails at the Structural Level

The plurality advocate's error is not in the data but in the inference. They see twelve operations with different rules and conclude: twelve acts. But this does not follow. A single cause routinely produces multiple effects that follow different rules.

Consider gravity. One force. It holds planets in orbit (periodic, elliptical), pulls apples to the ground (immediate, linear), generates tides (cyclical, dependent on proximity), and bends light (relativistic, conditional on mass-energy density). These effects follow entirely different mathematical descriptions. An observer cataloging gravitational phenomena might generate a list as varied as our twelve atonement operations -- some universal, some conditional, some periodic, some one-time. But no physicist would conclude that gravity is therefore twelve different forces. The plurality is in the effects. The unity is in the source.

Alma 42:23 sees this clearly: "mercy cometh because of the atonement; and the atonement bringeth to pass the resurrection of the dead; and the resurrection of the dead bringeth back men into the presence of God; and thus they are restored into his presence, to be judged according to their works." This is a causal chain. One source, cascading effects. The atonement produces resurrection, which produces return to God's presence, which produces judgment, which produces differentiated outcomes. The differentiation at the end does not prove plurality at the source.

What about Alma 11:41's explicit carve-out? The wicked remain "as though there had been no redemption made, except it be the loosing of the bands of death." This verse does not prove the Atonement is multiple acts. It proves that one act can produce effects that apply differently depending on the receptor. Sunlight falls on soil and on rock. The soil grows plants; the rock does not. This does not mean the sunlight aimed at soil is a different sunlight than the sunlight aimed at rock. The Atonement's universal operations (resurrection) and conditional operations (forgiveness, sanctification) differ not because they come from different sources but because the conditions they address are different: physical death happened *to* you involuntarily, so its remedy applies involuntarily; sin was *chosen* by you volitionally, so its remedy requires your volitional response.

The Spackman objection -- that *kaphar*, *ga'al*, and *yasha* were distinct operations in their Hebrew contexts -- is a point about linguistic history, not about ontology. The fact that ancient Israelites used different words drawn from different social worlds (temple, kinship law, battlefield) tells us how they *conceptualized* divine rescue in their cultural context. It does not tell us whether the divine reality behind those conceptualizations was itself plural. Ancient peoples also had different words for the morning star and the evening star before discovering they were Venus. Linguistic plurality does not entail ontological plurality.

## What the Plurality Reading Loses

When you fragment the Atonement into twelve separate operations, you lose the most important thing about it: the *person* who stands behind all twelve. The Atonement is not a set of divine services offered a la carte. It is one person -- Jesus Christ -- doing one thing: entering fully into the condition where we are and opening a permanent passage back to the Father. The resurrection is not a separate act from the forgiveness, which is not a separate act from the succoring. They are all consequences of the same structural reality: a divine being passed through the entire fallen condition and survived it, and that passage permanently altered the relationship between divinity and mortality.

Blake Ostler understood this when he argued that "atonement is happening all the time as an ongoing process" -- that it is not a completed transaction but God's way of being in relationship with the world in every moment. The compassion theory grasps something the transactional models miss: the Atonement is not a mechanism external to Christ that He activated and then stepped away from. It IS Christ's ongoing relationship to every human being who has ever suffered or sinned. You cannot fragment a person's relationship to you into modular operations without losing the relationship itself.

## The Extreme Version

Here is the hardest, most uncomfortable form of this thesis: there is no such thing as "the Atonement" conceived as a discrete event or collection of events that happened between Thursday night and Sunday morning in approximately 33 AD. What happened during those hours was the visible, historical eruption of something that is eternally true about the nature of God's relationship to creation. Christ was "slain from the foundation of the world" ([Revelation 13:8](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/rev/13.8)). The Atonement is not an intervention into an otherwise un-atoned cosmos. It is the permanent structure of the relationship between the divine and the mortal -- the eternal fact that God has entered fully into the condition of His creatures and has not withdrawn.

The historical events in Gethsemane, on Golgotha, and at the empty tomb are the temporal manifestation of an eternal reality -- the moment when what was always true became visible within history. This is why pre-Crucifixion saints could receive forgiveness: not because the Atonement's benefits were applied retroactively, but because the Atonement was never confined to a historical moment in the first place. It is, as Alma 34:14 insists, "infinite and eternal" -- not merely large and long-lasting, but structurally beyond temporal limitation.

## The Reasoning Skeleton

My argument rests on five claims, each load-bearing:

1. **Christ's passage through the fallen condition was one continuous act**, not a sequence of discrete operations. (If this falls, my argument collapses into mere assertion.)
2. **Multiple effects with different rules can flow from a single cause.** The resurrection/exaltation asymmetry is explained by the different natures of the problems addressed (involuntary death vs. volitional sin), not by plurality at the source.
3. **The scriptural language of unity ("infinite atonement," "great and last sacrifice," the singular definite article) reflects ontological unity**, not mere convenience. The scriptures call it one thing because it IS one thing.
4. **Fragmenting the Atonement into operations loses the person behind the operations.** Christ is not a service provider offering modular salvation packages. He is a person who entered fully into our condition, and every "operation" is a dimension of that entry.
5. **The Atonement is eternal in structure, not merely historical in execution.** Christ was "slain from the foundation of the world." The historical events are the temporal eruption of an eternal relationship, not the invention of that relationship.

The structurally load-bearing claim is number 1. If Christ's passage through mortality, death, sin-bearing, descent, and resurrection is genuinely one continuous motion rather than a series of separate acts, then every observation the plurality advocate makes -- twelve operations, different rules, graduated kingdoms -- is fully explained as differentiated effects of a single cause. If claim 1 falls -- if someone can demonstrate that the Gethsemane suffering, the crucifixion, and the resurrection are ontologically independent acts that merely happened in sequence -- then the unity position becomes an aesthetic preference rather than a structural argument.

But claim 1 does not fall. The Orthodox tradition has held it for two millennia. The scriptures insist on it: "it must needs be an infinite atonement" (2 Nephi 9:7) -- one thing, one sacrifice, one passage. Not infinite atonements. Not a menu. One act of a God who descended below all things, that He might comprehend all things, that He might be in and through all things, the light of truth ([D&C 88:6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/dc/88.6)). The descent and the comprehension and the illumination are three words for one divine motion. The Atonement is one because Christ is one, because the love that drove it is one, because the fallen condition He entered is one, and because the passage He opened through it is one.

To fragment it is to lose it.
